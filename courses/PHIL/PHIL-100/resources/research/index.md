## Documents

- research-02.md — Socrates and Plato's early dialogues: the Apology (examined life, elenchus, Socratic ignorance) and the Meno (paradox of inquiry, recollection, knowledge vs. true belief)
- research-03.md — Plato's mature philosophy: Theory of Forms, the Republic's three analogies (Cave, Sun, Divided Line), tripartite soul, justice in soul and city, philosopher-king

- research-05.md — Hellenistic schools and the Phaedo: Stoicism (Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, dichotomy of control), Epicureanism (ataraxia, death argument, friendship), Plato's Phaedo (four immortality arguments, philosopher's life)

## Key Themes

- **Philosophy as a way of life**: From Socrates through the Hellenistic schools, ancient philosophy is not merely theoretical — it is a form of practice, a training in how to live and face death
- **The question of knowledge**: What can we know, and how? The Pre-Socratics, Plato, and Aristotle all give different answers; the division between reason and sense experience runs through the entire tradition
- **Virtue and the good life**: Every thinker in this course addresses this: What makes a human life go well? Answers range from the examined life (Socrates), Forms-directed knowledge (Plato), to cultivated character (Aristotle), to control over judgment (Stoics), to simple pleasure (Epicurus)
- **The soul and death**: The course opens with Heraclitus's logos and closes with the Phaedo — between them is a sustained conversation about whether anything survives death and whether fearing death is rational
- **The Plato-Aristotle fault line**: The philosophical split between Plato (abstract, rationalist, Forms-based) and Aristotle (empirical, practical, immanent) defines the shape of Western philosophy for two millennia

## Recommended Session Count

**10 sessions** — the pre-decided arc is well-calibrated to the material volume. The Pre-Socratics and Plato's early dialogues each warrant full sessions; Plato's Republic warrants two (metaphysics and political philosophy are substantively distinct); Aristotle warrants two (Metaphysics critique + Ethics is a genuine shift); the Hellenistic schools warrant two (Stoics and Epicureans are meaningfully different); and the Phaedo as a capstone synthesizes all earlier threads. Ten sessions is not padded — cutting further would mean compressing genuinely distinct ideas.

## Coverage Gaps

- **Epicurus's Principal Doctrines**: the full text was not fetched; key content was obtained from the Letter to Menoeceus and secondary sources. The Principal Doctrines are short and easily accessible at MIT Classics (classics.mit.edu/Epicurus/princ_doctrines.html). The Tutor should direct students there directly.
- **Anaximenes**: research-01 covers the core Pre-Socratics but omits Anaximenes (who proposed air as the archē). He is philosophically minor for a 100-level course and is reasonably omitted.
- **Plato's Symposium and Phaedrus**: not covered — these would be natural additions for a 101 course on Platonic love and rhetoric.
- No critical gaps for the 10-session arc as designed. All session topics have strong source coverage.
