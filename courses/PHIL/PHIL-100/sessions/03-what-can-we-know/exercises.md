Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: Meno's Paradox — Take It Seriously
**Type:** Thought experiment

Before dismissing Meno's paradox as a trick, try to feel its force.

Think of something you want to understand better — a skill, a concept, a person. Now apply the paradox:
- If you already understand it, why are you trying to learn more?
- If you don't understand it at all, how would you recognize progress?

**What to observe / record:**
- Is there a middle ground between "I know it completely" and "I know nothing about it"? What would that look like?
- If there is a middle ground, does the paradox still hold?
- Does the recollection theory seem like a satisfying answer to you, or does it raise new problems?

Write 1–2 paragraphs.

## Exercise 2: The Slave-Boy Demonstration
**Type:** Reading / Analysis

Find the section of the *Meno* where Socrates questions the slave boy about the square (approximately 82b–85b in standard Plato editions). Read it carefully.

**What to observe / record:**
1. Does Socrates ever *tell* the boy the answer? Or does he only ask questions?
2. The boy starts confident, then becomes confused, then arrives at the right answer. Which of these stages does Socrates value most, and why?
3. Do you think this demonstration proves that the boy "remembered" something? Or is there another explanation for how he arrived at the answer?

Write 3–5 sentences for each question.

## Exercise 3: Knowledge vs. True Belief — A Personal Test
**Type:** Reflection / Analysis

Think of three things you believe. For each one, try to determine whether you have *knowledge* (in the Platonic sense — you can explain why it's true and your belief won't be shaken by a clever argument) or merely *true belief* (you believe it, you might even be right, but you couldn't fully defend it).

Examples: a political belief, a scientific belief, a moral belief.

**What to observe / record:**
- For each belief: can you state *why* it is true — not just that it is true?
- Could a skilled debater make you doubt it?
- Does the distinction between knowledge and true belief matter for how you act on these beliefs?

Write a short list with 2–3 sentences per belief.
