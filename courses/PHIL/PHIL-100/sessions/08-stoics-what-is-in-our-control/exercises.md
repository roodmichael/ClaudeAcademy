# Session 08 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

---

## Exercise 1: Applying the Dichotomy of Control
**Type:** Reflection / Analysis

Choose a current source of stress or anxiety in your life — something real, not invented.

Now apply Epictetus's dichotomy:
1. What aspects of this situation are genuinely up to you (your judgment, your response, your actions)?
2. What aspects are not up to you (other people's behavior, outcomes you cannot control, circumstances)?
3. Are you spending more energy on the "not up to us" column than on the "up to us" column?

**What to observe / record:**
- Does separating the two categories help or does it feel like a denial of something real?
- Is there anything that felt like it was "up to others" that actually has a component that is up to you?

Write 2–3 paragraphs.

## Exercise 2: Close Reading the Enchiridion
**Type:** Reading / Analysis

Read the opening five chapters of the *Enchiridion*. They are short — maybe 10 minutes total.

Pick the one passage that strikes you as either most powerful or most difficult to accept.

**What to observe / record:**
1. Quote the passage.
2. State what Epictetus is claiming.
3. State your reaction: do you find it convincing? Freeing? Impossible? Explain why.

Write 3–5 sentences for each step.

---

## Exercise 3: Stoics vs. Aristotle on External Goods
**Type:** Analysis

Aristotle says you need external goods (a measure of health, wealth, friends, and good luck) to live a fully flourishing life. Stoics say virtue alone is sufficient — the virtuous person in poverty and poor health is still fully flourishing.

**What to observe / record:**
1. Which position seems more realistic to you?
2. Which seems more genuinely inspiring?
3. Is there a meaningful difference between a philosophy that is *realistically* accurate about what humans need and one that sets a high standard that most people won't meet but should aim for?

Write 2–3 paragraphs.
