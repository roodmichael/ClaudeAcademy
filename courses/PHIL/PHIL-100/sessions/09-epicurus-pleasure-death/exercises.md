Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: The Symmetry Argument — Take It Seriously
**Type:** Analysis

Epicurus argues: the time before your birth did not disturb you; the time after your death is the same; therefore death should not disturb you.

**What to observe / record:**
1. Reconstruct the argument carefully in your own words.
2. Try to find the weakest point in the argument. (For example: some philosophers argue that pre-natal non-existence and post-mortem non-existence are actually different — we anticipate the future but not the past. Does this objection work?)
3. If the argument is correct, what follows practically? Should you stop thinking about death at all?

Write 2–3 paragraphs.

## Exercise 2: Desire Inventory
**Type:** Reflection

Epicurus classifies desires as natural-and-necessary, natural-but-unnecessary, or empty. Make a brief inventory of 6–8 things you currently want or pursue.

For each: classify it, and ask whether pursuing it tends to produce peace or more wanting.

**What to observe / record:**
- Are there any desires you classified as "natural and necessary" that Epicurus would classify as "empty"?
- Are there any you classified as "empty" that you still think are worth pursuing? Why?
- Does the exercise change anything about how you want to organize your time?

Write 1–2 paragraphs reflecting on the inventory overall.

## Exercise 3: Stoics vs. Epicureans on the Good Life
**Type:** Analysis

You now have both schools in view. Compare them directly:

1. Where do they agree? (Both seek tranquility, both engage seriously with the fear of death, both have practical daily disciplines)
2. Where do they differ? (Virtue vs. pleasure, engagement vs. withdrawal, role of friendship, attitude toward community)
3. If you had to choose one school as a practical guide for your own life right now, which would it be and why?

Write 2–3 paragraphs. Be specific — don't just say "they balance each other out."
