# Session 04 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

---

## Exercise 1: Drawing the Divided Line
**Type:** Analysis

Draw Plato's Divided Line on paper (or describe it in writing). Label all four sections with their names and corresponding mental states. Then place five examples from your own experience at different levels:

- Something you've only seen in a reflection or photograph (section 1)
- A physical object you interact with regularly (section 2)
- A mathematical or logical truth (section 3)
- Something you think might be a Form — an abstract truth grasped only by reason (section 4)

**What to observe / record:**
- Does the hierarchy feel right to you? Do mathematical truths feel more "real" than physical objects?
- Can you think of anything that doesn't fit neatly into one section?

Write 1 paragraph on what the exercise reveals.

## Exercise 2: Cave Narrative
**Type:** Analysis / Reading

Re-read the Allegory of the Cave (Republic VII, 514a–517c) in your text.

Now narrate the experience from the *perspective of the prisoner*: what does each stage feel like? Focus on the emotional and experiential dimension — the discomfort of the fire's light, the confusion outside the cave, the difficulty of looking at the sun.

**What to observe / record:**
- At what stage would you want to stop and go back to the cave?
- What does the prisoner's return to the cave reveal about the relationship between knowledge and power? Why would the other prisoners reject or mock him?

Write 2–3 paragraphs in first person as the prisoner.

## Exercise 3: Forms in Everyday Judgment
**Type:** Thought experiment

Consider these three judgments:
1. "This painting is beautiful."
2. "That action was just."
3. "The number 7 is odd."

For each judgment, ask:
- Are you reporting a fact about an object in the world?
- Are you reporting a subjective preference?
- Or are you appealing to some standard that exists independently of both you and the object?

**What to observe / record:**
- Which of these judgments, if any, seem to require something like a Platonic Form to make sense of?
- Does beauty feel more or less "objective" than justice? More or less objective than mathematical truth?

Write 1–2 paragraphs.
