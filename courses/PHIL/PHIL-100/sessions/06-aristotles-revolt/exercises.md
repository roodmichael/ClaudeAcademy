# Session 06 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

---

## Exercise 1: Aristotle vs. Plato on Forms
**Type:** Analysis

Aristotle objects that Plato's Forms explain nothing because "participation" is just a metaphor.

Try to steelman both positions:
1. Write the strongest version of Plato's defense: why might Forms be necessary to explain things like beauty or justice?
2. Write Aristotle's objection to that defense as sharply as possible.

**What to observe / record:**
- Who is more convincing to you, and why?
- Is there a position that could incorporate something from both?

Write 1–2 paragraphs for each side.

## Exercise 2: The Function Argument
**Type:** Analysis / Application

Aristotle's Function Argument says: the good for X is excellent performance of X's characteristic function.

Test the argument:
1. Apply it to a role or activity: what is the function of a doctor? A teacher? A friend?
2. For one of those roles, does "excellent performance of the function" seem like a satisfying account of what makes that person good?
3. Apply it to "human being" — does "rational activity" fully capture what makes a human life go well? What might it leave out?

**What to observe / record:**
Write 2–3 sentences for each step. Be willing to push back on Aristotle.

## Exercise 3: What Is Eudaimonia?
**Type:** Reflection

Aristotle says eudaimonia is:
- An activity, not a passive state
- Objective — it does not depend purely on how you feel
- Pursued for its own sake, not as a means to something else

Think about someone you consider to be living a genuinely good life. It doesn't have to be a famous person.

**What to observe / record:**
- What makes you judge their life as good? Is it happiness, achievement, character, relationships, something else?
- Does Aristotle's account match your judgment, or does it diverge?
- Is there something that feels like flourishing to you that Aristotle's account might miss?

Write 2–3 paragraphs.
