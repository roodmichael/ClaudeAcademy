# Session 01 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: The Archē Question
**Type:** Thought experiment

Pick any ordinary object in the room with you right now — a coffee mug, a chair, a phone. Now ask the Pre-Socratic question: what is it *fundamentally*? Follow the reasoning as far as it goes.

Thales would say: ultimately, water (or whatever his archē is). Anaximander would say: it came from the indefinite and will return to it. A modern physicist might say: atoms, then quarks, then quantum fields.

**What to observe / record:**
- At what point does your answer feel satisfying, and at what point does it generate another "but what is *that* made of?" question?
- Does the question have a stopping point, or does it regress infinitely?
- What would a truly satisfying answer to "what is it fundamentally?" look like?

Write 3–5 sentences on what you notice.

## Exercise 2: Heraclitus vs. Parmenides
**Type:** Analysis

Read these two statements side by side:

> "You cannot step into the same river twice, for other and yet other waters are flowing." — Heraclitus (DK22B12)

> "For the same thing is for thinking and for being." — Parmenides (DK28B3)

**What to observe / record:**

Answer these questions in writing (a short paragraph each):
1. What is Heraclitus saying about identity and change? Do you agree that the river is "different" each time? In what sense?
2. Parmenides claims that thought and being are identical — that you cannot even think about what does not exist. Is this convincing? Can you think of a counterexample?
3. Who is more persuasive to you, and why?

There is no right answer here. The goal is to think carefully, not to arrive at a conclusion.

## Exercise 3: Zeno's Arrow
**Type:** Thought experiment

Zeno's Arrow paradox: at any instant in time, a flying arrow is at rest — it occupies a fixed position and is not moving. Every instant is like this. So how does motion occur?

Pause for a moment before reading further. Try to state what's wrong with this argument before looking for an answer.

**What to observe / record:**
- What is your intuitive objection to Zeno's argument?
- Is it a good objection, or does Zeno have a reply?
- What does this paradox reveal about the relationship between reasoning and observation? If your senses tell you the arrow is moving but logic says it isn't, which do you trust?

Write 4–6 sentences on your response.
