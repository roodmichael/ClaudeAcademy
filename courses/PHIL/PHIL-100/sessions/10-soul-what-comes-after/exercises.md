## Exercise 1: Evaluating the Four Arguments
**Type:** Analysis

Rate each of Plato's four arguments for soul immortality on a scale of 1–5 for persuasiveness, where 1 = not convincing at all and 5 = very convincing. For each rating, give a reason.

1. The Cyclical Argument (life and death generate each other)
2. The Argument from Recollection (the soul pre-existed birth)
3. The Affinity Argument (the soul is akin to the eternal Forms)
4. The Final Argument (the soul has life essentially, like fire has heat)

**What to observe / record:**
- Which argument do you find most compelling? Which do you find weakest?
- Do any of the arguments depend on each other — would one fail if another fails?
- Do the arguments add up to a convincing cumulative case, or do their individual weaknesses undermine the whole?

Write 2–3 sentences per argument plus a short paragraph on the overall case.

## Exercise 2: Comparing Death Across the Course
**Type:** Analysis / Synthesis

You have now encountered five distinct positions on how to relate to death:
1. **Socrates** (Apology): death is either rest or continuation; a just person has nothing to fear
2. **Plato** (Phaedo): the soul is immortal; philosophy is preparation for death; death is the culmination of the philosophical life
3. **Aristotle**: death ends the activity of the soul; eudaimonia is an activity of this life
4. **Epictetus/Stoics**: death is not "up to us"; accepting it as natural releases us from fear
5. **Epicurus**: death is non-existence; the symmetry argument shows it cannot harm us

**What to observe / record:**
- Which position do you find most convincing as a philosophical argument?
- Which position do you find most *useful* — most practically helpful for how you live?
- Are these the same answer?

Write 3–4 paragraphs.

## Exercise 3: The Last Scene
**Type:** Reading / Reflection

Read the final pages of the *Phaedo* — Socrates' death scene. (It begins approximately at 115a and runs to the end of the dialogue.)

**What to observe / record:**
1. How does Plato describe Socrates' behavior? What details does he include?
2. Why do you think Plato ends with this scene rather than with a philosophical argument?
3. What is the effect on you as a reader?

Write 2–3 paragraphs.
