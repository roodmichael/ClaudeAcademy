# Session 07 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

---

## Exercise 1: Mapping Your Own Virtues with the Doctrine of the Mean
**Type:** Analysis / Reflection

Choose two virtues you think are relevant to your life right now. For each:
1. Identify the two vices on either side (excess and deficiency)
2. Where do you currently tend to fall — toward the excess, the deficiency, or the mean?
3. What habit would move you toward the mean?

Example: if you chose "patience" — the excess might be passivity (never expressing legitimate urgency), the deficiency might be irascibility (losing your temper too easily). Where do you sit?

**What to observe / record:**
- Does Aristotle's framework help you think about these traits more clearly, or does it distort them?
- Is "the mean" always a genuinely virtuous position, or can you think of cases where the mean might be too moderate?

Write 2–3 sentences per virtue, plus a short paragraph on the overall framework.

## Exercise 2: Habituation in Practice
**Type:** Reflection

Aristotle says we become courageous by doing courageous acts, generous by doing generous acts. Think about a characteristic you have that you did not choose to have — something that is now second nature.

**What to observe / record:**
- How did it develop? What repeated actions or experiences formed it?
- Was there a point when it was effortful that is now automatic?
- Does this support or complicate Aristotle's account of how virtues are built?

Write 2–3 paragraphs.

## Exercise 3: Contemplation vs. Civic Life
**Type:** Analysis

Aristotle argues in Book X that contemplation (theōria) is the highest form of happiness. But throughout Books II–IX he emphasizes civic virtue — justice, friendship, courage — as central to the good life.

**What to observe / record:**
1. State the tension as precisely as you can: in what circumstances would the two ideals conflict?
2. Which do you find more compelling — the life of philosophical withdrawal or the life of civic engagement?
3. Is there a way to hold both — or does Aristotle have to choose?

Write 2–3 paragraphs. Reference specific passages from Books II and X if you can.
