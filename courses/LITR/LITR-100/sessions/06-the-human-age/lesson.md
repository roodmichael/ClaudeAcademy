# Session 06: The Human Age
**Course:** LITR-100
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to describe how the Kojiki uses Emperor Jimmu's narrative to construct imperial legitimacy, analyze genealogy as a political argument, and identify the shift in narrative register from mythology to proto-history.

---

## Opening

Emperor Jimmu is said to have founded Japan in 660 BCE. The Kojiki was compiled in 712 CE.
That is over a thousand years after the events it is describing — in a period with no
writing, no independent historical record, no corroborating Chinese or Korean sources.

Modern historians regard Jimmu as legendary, not historical. Yet the Kojiki presents him
as the first emperor, traces his lineage to Amaterasu through five generations, and
provides him with a narrative of military conquest, divine guidance, and ceremony.

The question is not whether Jimmu existed. The question is what the Jimmu narrative *does*
— what political, theological, and ideological work it performs in the text and for the
culture that produced it.

---

## The Transition: What Changes in the Middle Volume

The Middle Volume opens with Emperor Jimmu, and the prose immediately feels different from
the Upper Volume. Notice the shifts:

**What is gone**: Spontaneous divine births, creation acts, explicit divine intervention
at every turn, the sense that the cosmos itself is being built in real time.

**What remains but attenuates**: Divine ancestry (Jimmu is the great-great-grandson of a
god, not a god himself); divine guidance in moments of crisis; the three imperial regalia
as ongoing symbols.

**What is new**: A named, located military campaign (Kyushu to Yamato); specific place
names that correspond to real geography; enemies with names and motives; a court
ceremony (Jimmu's enthronement); the beginning of a reign-by-reign chronology.

The text is now presenting *legend dressed as history*. It is not history in the modern
sense — there is no independent corroboration — but the narrative conventions have shifted
toward historical chronicle.

---

## Emperor Jimmu: The Divine Genealogy

Jimmu's full genealogical name, as given in the Kojiki, is essentially a recitation of
divine ancestry. He is:
- Son of Ugayafukiaezu
- Grandson of Hoori (who visited the sea god's palace)
- Great-grandson of Ninigi (who descended from heaven)
- Great-great-grandson of Amaterasu's son Ame-no-Oshihomimi
- Great-great-great-grandson of Amaterasu herself

Each generation is one step more human than the last. Ninigi is the bridge; his immediate
descendants are semi-divine; Jimmu is human enough to be an emperor. The genealogical
chain does the theological work of showing how divinity is transmitted through successive
generations without being lost — attenuated, but continuous.

This is the Kojiki's central political argument, made not through assertion but through
narrative: the emperor rules because he is the legitimate heir of a divine line that
stretches back to the origin of the universe.

---

## The Eastern Campaign

Jimmu's founding narrative is a military campaign. From Himuka in Kyushu, he travels
east with his brothers and an army, encountering resistance, receiving divine assistance,
and eventually conquering the Yamato region of central Honshu.

Key episodes in the campaign:

**The divine crow Yatagarasu**: At a moment when Jimmu's campaign is stalled in a dangerous
valley, a great three-legged crow (Yatagarasu) is sent by Amaterasu to guide him. The crow
leads him out of danger and toward his destination. Divine intervention is more subtle here
than in the upper volume — Amaterasu does not appear; she sends a bird.

**The Heavenly Sword**: At another point of crisis, a sword descends miraculously from
heaven and grants Jimmu's army supernatural power to defeat its enemies. The divine
instruments are becoming more indirect, more mediated.

**Dream and oracle**: Jimmu receives guidance through dreams and divine pronouncements.
Direct communication with the gods is giving way to oracular communication — the god speaks
through signs, not voice.

These progressive mediations are structural. The Age of the Gods is characterized by
direct divine action; the human age is characterized by divine action through intermediaries —
birds, dreams, swords that fall from the sky. The world is becoming less directly divine
and more indirectly so.

## Genealogy as Argument

The Middle Volume of the Kojiki is predominantly genealogical. For most of the emperors
between Jimmu and the later, more narratively developed reigns, the text offers little more
than:

- Name (and list of variant names)
- Parentage
- Consorts and children
- Age at death

This can feel like filler to a modern reader. It is not. Each entry in the genealogical
sequence performs the same argument: that there is an unbroken chain of succession, without
gaps or disputed transitions, from the first emperor to the current dynasty.

The completeness of the genealogy is itself the argument. A dynasty that can trace
its succession without interruption, all the way back to a divine origin, has legitimacy
that cannot be challenged by a rival with a shorter or less complete lineage.

The length of the emperors' reigns is also politically significant. Several early emperors
are given reigns of over a hundred years — some over two hundred. The legendary emperor
Ōjin is said to have lived to 130. These extended lives are not meant to be taken literally;
they serve to push the founding of the imperial line back to an antiquity that gives it
parity with (or superiority to) Chinese dynastic claims.

### The 660 BCE Dating

Emperor Jimmu's traditional founding date — 660 BCE — would make the Japanese imperial
line the oldest continuous monarchy in the world, older than any Chinese dynasty. Modern
scholars universally regard this date as a retroactive construction: the Kojiki's
compilers worked backwards from a desired founding date, extending reigns artificially
to reach 660.

This does not mean the dynastic tradition is illegitimate. It means the Kojiki is doing
what ancient chronicles do: claiming the prestige of antiquity for present-day institutions.
The Roman Aeneid did the same for Roman imperial origins; the Hebrew scriptures do the
same for Israelite national identity.

## Jimmu's Enthronement: Ceremony and Cosmos

The Kojiki describes Jimmu's enthronement with considerable ceremonial specificity. He
builds a palace in Yamato, receives divine commands, performs ritual food offerings to
the kami, and formally declares himself the heir of Amaterasu.

The enthronement is not just a political event — it is a cosmic one. It closes the loop
between the Tenson Kōrin (Ninigi's descent from heaven) and the establishment of the
earthly court. The three imperial regalia, transmitted from Amaterasu to Ninigi and now
held by Jimmu, are present at the ceremony. What was divine is now institutional.

---

## Why This Matters

The Jimmu narrative is where the Kojiki's political argument becomes explicit. The Age of
the Gods was building toward this moment: all of the cosmological machinery — the creation,
the cave episode, the kuni-yuzuri, the Tenson Kōrin — exists to explain why *this* emperor,
ruling *this* land, has the right to govern.

When the Kojiki was compiled in 712 CE, the empress who commissioned it (Genmei) was
asserting the legitimacy of a dynasty that had consolidated its rule through a combination
of marriage, conquest, and religious authority. The text gave that dynasty a cosmic origin
story that reached back to the first stirrings of creation.

This is the foundational political text of Japan — not because it is the oldest, but because
it grounds the most important claim any ruling institution can make: "we have always been
here, and we were meant to be here."

---

## Key Takeaways

- Emperor Jimmu is legendary, not historical; the 660 BCE founding date is a retroactive
  construction to claim antiquity comparable to Chinese dynasties.
- His narrative functions as a divine genealogy argument: five steps of transmission from
  Amaterasu to the first emperor, each generation slightly more human than the last.
- The Jimmu sections use progressive mediation (crow, dreams, descending sword) rather than
  direct divine action — the world has become more human, so the gods act more indirectly.
- The genealogical structure of the Middle Volume is itself the political argument:
  completeness and continuity of succession from divine origin to present day.
- The enthronement scene closes the loop between the Tenson Kōrin and the establishment
  of the earthly court, making the institutional and the cosmic one thing.
