# Session 08 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: Trace the Reception History
**Type:** Analysis

The lesson traces the Kojiki through several distinct historical moments: compilation (712),
obscurity (Heian–Edo), Norinaga's commentary (1764–1798), State Shinto (Meiji era), and
post-WWII rehabilitation.

**For each of the five moments, write 2–3 sentences identifying:**

1. What the Kojiki meant or was used for in that period
2. Who was doing the interpreting and what their interests were
3. What reading of the text was being advanced or suppressed

Then write a closing sentence: which of these readings do you find most intellectually
honest, and why?

## Exercise 2: Mono no Aware — Reading a Poem Through Norinaga's Lens
**Type:** Analysis

Motoori Norinaga's concept of **mono no aware** ("the pathos of things") describes a
sensitivity to beauty and transience — the feeling you get when something lovely is
also, precisely because of its loveliness, impermanent.

**Find one poem in your Chamberlain that you feel expresses mono no aware.** This can be
any poem from any section you have read. Write down the poem (or as much of it as you
can capture from the translation).

Then answer:

1. What is the feeling in the poem that Norinaga's concept describes?
2. Does the concept help you understand the poem better, or does it impose something
   on the poem that wasn't already there?
3. If a concept developed in 18th-century Japan is used to read a poem from 712 CE,
   what are the risks? What are the benefits?

## Exercise 3: What Is a Myth? — Applying the Concept
**Type:** Written reflection (200–300 words)

The lesson offers a scholarly definition of myth: a narrative that a community uses to
explain its origins, values, and cosmic order — one that is performed, inhabited, argued
over, weaponized, and critiqued.

**Apply this definition to the Kojiki as a whole:**

Write a short passage arguing: is the Kojiki better understood as a myth, as literature,
as a political document, or as all three simultaneously? Use at least two specific examples
from the text to support your position.

You are not being asked to reach a final verdict. You are being asked to think carefully
about which of these frames is most useful, and why — recognizing that the Kojiki has
been all three things at different moments in its history.
