## Exercise 1: Map the Divine Procession
**Type:** Reading + Analysis

Open your Chamberlain to the Upper Volume and trace the seven generations of heavenly gods.
This requires actually reading the text — not just the lesson summary.

**What to record:**

1. Write down the names of the five Separate Heavenly Deities (Kotoamatsukami) that appear
   at the very opening. Chamberlain translates their names — what do the names mean, and do
   the meanings suggest anything about what these deities represent?

2. Count the generations from the first spontaneous deity to Izanagi and Izanami. How many
   generations is it? Why do you think the text structures the opening as a procession
   rather than jumping directly to the creative couple?

3. Chamberlain's footnotes often explain name etymology. Find one footnote that helps you
   understand a deity's name and describe what it adds to your reading of that character.

## Exercise 2: The Failed First Marriage
**Type:** Thought experiment + Written reflection

The Kojiki explains that Izanagi and Izanami's first child, the Leech-Child, was born
malformed because Izanami spoke first during their ritual. The gods correct the order
and the second attempt produces the islands of Japan.

**Reflect and write a short paragraph (5–8 sentences):**

This is a myth explaining why a specific ritual ordering is correct. Think about a norm or
custom in your own experience — professional, religious, or social — that has an origin
story attached to it. (Examples: why a wedding ceremony follows a specific order, why a
religious rite is performed a certain way, why a professional process has a specific sequence.)

How does having an origin story for a norm — rather than just a rule — change how people
relate to the norm? What does the Kojiki's approach of embedding social norms in creation
narratives tell you about how the text functions?

## Exercise 3: Izanagi in the Underworld — The Moment of Looking
**Type:** Reading + Analysis

Find the scene in your Chamberlain where Izanagi lights a tooth from his comb to see
Izanami in Yomi. Read the passage carefully.

**Questions to answer:**

1. Chamberlain's translation of this scene has a particular tone. How would you characterize
   it — horror, tragedy, disgust, something else? What specific words or phrases in the
   translation produce that effect?

2. The scene requires Izanagi to break a prohibition: "Do not look." Myths involving
   prohibited looking appear across world traditions (Lot's wife, Orpheus and Eurydice,
   Psyche and Eros). What does the prohibition accomplish in each case, and why do the
   characters always break it?

3. After Izanagi escapes and seals the underworld with a boulder, he and Izanami exchange
   words through the boulder. She will kill a thousand per day; he will create fifteen
   hundred. In one sentence, state what cosmological fact this exchange is explaining.
