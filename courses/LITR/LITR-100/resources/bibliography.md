## Tier 4: Well-Known Books (Not Web-Fetchable)

**Chamberlain, Basil Hall. *Ko-ji-ki, or Records of Ancient Matters.* Transactions of the
Asiatic Society of Japan, 1882.**
Coverage: The primary translation used in this course. Literal fidelity, extensive scholarly
footnotes, Victorian prose style. First European-language translation of the Kojiki.

**Heldt, Gustav (trans.). *The Kojiki: An Account of Ancient Matters.* Columbia University
Press (Translations from the Asian Classics), 2014.**
Coverage: A modern prose translation with significant improvements over Chamberlain in
readability and rendering of the poetry. Recommended as a comparison text.

**Philippi, Donald (trans.). *Kojiki.* Princeton University Press / University of Tokyo
Press, 1968.**
Coverage: A scholarly translation considered by specialists to be particularly strong
on the poetry. Less readable as prose than Heldt but philologically precise.

**Chamberlain, Basil Hall. *Things Japanese.* London, 1890.**
Coverage: Chamberlain's popular encyclopedia of Japanese culture; contextualizes the
Victorian-era scholarly framework within which his Kojiki translation was produced.

## Further Reading (Not Used in Course Research — Recommended for Independent Study)

**Aston, W. G. (trans.). *Nihongi: Chronicles of Japan from the Earliest Times to A.D. 697.*
Tuttle, 1972 (original 1896).**
The parallel Victorian-era translation of the Nihon Shoki — essential for comparing how
the same myths are handled in Japan's two foundational chronicles.

**Keene, Donald. *Seeds in the Heart: Japanese Literature from Earliest Times to the Late
Sixteenth Century.* Columbia University Press, 1999.**
Comprehensive coverage of early Japanese literature; includes excellent sections on the
Kojiki, Man'yōshū, and the development of waka.

**Harootunian, H.D. *Things Seen and Unseen: Discourse and Ideology in Tokugawa Nativism.*
University of Chicago Press, 1988.**
Scholarly study of kokugaku and Norinaga's place within it; essential for understanding
the ideological dimension of the Kojiki's rediscovery.
