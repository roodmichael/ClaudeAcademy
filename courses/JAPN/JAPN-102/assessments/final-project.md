# Final Project — JAPN-102
**Covers:** All 11 sessions

---

## Overview

This capstone project has two parts. Part A is an annotated reading portfolio — evidence that you can read authentic Japanese text strategically and analytically. Part B is original writing in Japanese — evidence that the vocabulary, kanji, and nuance knowledge from this course is productively available, not just recognizable.

Both parts require you to work with real Japanese — NHK Web Easy articles and your own Japanese sentences — not constructed exercises.

---

## Part A: Annotated Reading Portfolio

**Select 5 NHK Web Easy articles** from nhk.or.jp/news/easy. They must be 5 different articles on 5 different topics (e.g., politics, environment, health, culture, technology). Choose articles from the past 3 months if possible — current content.

For each article, produce an annotation document with the following sections:

### A1 — Article Information
- Title (in Japanese)
- Date
- Topic category
- Estimated character count (NHK Web Easy articles are typically 200–500 characters)

### A2 — Discourse Structure Map
Identify all discourse connectives in the article and classify them (contrast, causation, addition, summary, sequence). Draw or describe the logical flow: how do paragraphs connect? What is the main claim, and how does the author support or frame it?

### A3 — Vocabulary Notes
Identify 5 vocabulary words from the article that you learned (or reinforced) from this course. For each:
- The word in kanji + reading
- Which session it relates to
- The sentence from the article where it appears
- A brief note on how its meaning was apparent or how you inferred it

### A4 — Unknown Word Log
List any words you could not decode even with the inference protocol. For each:
- The word (kanji form)
- Your best inference attempt (radical, context, part-of-speech)
- The actual meaning (after dictionary lookup)
- What the gap tells you about your next study focus

### A5 — Comprehension Self-Assessment
Write 2 comprehension questions about the article (as if you were writing an exam question). Then answer them. Rate your reading confidence for this article: 1 (understood very little) to 5 (understood almost everything without dictionary).

## Part B: Original Writing

Write a **300-word personal reflection in Japanese** about one of the 5 articles from Part A. Your reflection should:

1. **Summarize** the article's main point in your own words (not a copy of the text)
2. **Give your opinion or reaction** — do you agree with the experts cited? What surprised you? Does this topic connect to something you know from another domain?
3. **Use at least 8 vocabulary items from Sessions 01–09 of this course** — highlight them in bold when you submit
4. **Use at least 2 discourse connectives** from Session 10 in your writing

Your Japanese does not need to be perfect. It needs to demonstrate that you can express thoughts about a real topic using the vocabulary you have built. The Tutor will give feedback on natural usage, not penalize imperfections.

**If 300 Japanese characters feels beyond your current output ability:** Start with 150 characters and a mix of English explanation where needed. The goal is authentic expression, not quantity.

## What to Submit

Submit to the Tutor:
1. Five annotation documents (one per article) — Parts A1–A5 for each
2. Your 300-word reflection in Japanese (Part B) with vocabulary highlighted

## Evaluation Criteria

**Part A:**
- Discourse structure maps are accurate — connectives are correctly classified, logical flow is described
- Vocabulary notes correctly identify course vocabulary in authentic context
- Unknown word log reflects genuine engagement with the inference protocol (not just "I didn't know it")
- Comprehension self-assessment questions are meaningful (not trivial) and correctly answered

**Part B:**
- At least 8 course vocabulary items used correctly (correct meaning, correct collocation, correct register)
- At least 2 discourse connectives used correctly to structure the argument
- The main point of the chosen article is accurately summarized
- The reflection expresses genuine personal engagement with the topic (not a formulaic template)

## Tutor Scoring Guide

**What a strong portfolio looks like:**
- Discourse maps in A2 go beyond "lists of connectives" — they describe the argument shape (e.g., "paragraph 1 introduces problem, paragraph 2 gives cause [ので], paragraph 3 reverses with expert opinion [しかし], paragraph 4 concludes [つまり]").
- Vocabulary notes in A3 show genuine course connection — not just identifying any N3 word but connecting it to a specific lesson concept.
- Unknown word log in A4 demonstrates the inference protocol: "Radical 氵(water) + context (environmental article) → I guessed 水源 (water source). Correct."
- Part B writing uses vocabulary in natural sentences, not forced insertions. Vocabulary items appear where they naturally fit in the argument.

**Common weaknesses to flag:**
- A2 discourse maps that list connectives but do not describe the logical relationship (e.g., "しかし appears in paragraph 2" without explaining what it contrasts).
- Vocabulary notes that cite words from lists rather than the actual article text.
- Part B writing that uses English word order with Japanese words inserted — provide gentle feedback on Japanese sentence construction without discouraging the attempt.
- Register mismatches in Part B — 言う used where 述べる is appropriate, or すごい in a formal reflection.
- Confidence self-ratings (A5) that are systematically too high (student rates 5 but comprehension questions are answered incorrectly). Address gently — the self-assessment is diagnostic, not evaluative.
