# Glossary — JAPN-102: Japanese Language — N4 to N3 Vocabulary and Kanji

All key terms from Sessions 01–11. Alphabetized by English term.

---

**Abstraction shift** — The qualitative change in vocabulary type from N4 to N3. N4 vocabulary is largely concrete and situational; N3 introduces abstract concepts (影響, 可能性, 原因), formal compound verbs (利用する, 述べる), and register distinctions. This shift is the defining challenge of the N4→N3 transition.

**Bushu (部首)** — A radical — the smaller stroke-pattern component used to classify kanji in dictionaries and as a memory anchor. There are 214 official bushu. Approximately 50 are high-frequency enough for JLPT study. See also: Radical.

**Collocation** — A statistically stable word combination that native speakers use habitually. 影響を与える (give/have influence) is a collocation; 影響を言う (*say influence) is not. At N3, knowing a word without its collocations leaves it unusable in natural speech or writing.

**Dictionary convention** — The standard in Japanese dictionaries: on'yomi readings are listed in KATAKANA (セン, KA); kun'yomi readings are listed in hiragana (やま, たべ). Universal across dictionaries including jisho.org.

**Discourse connective (接続詞, せつぞくし)** — A word or phrase that signals the logical relationship between sentences or clauses. Essential for reading comprehension: しかし (contrast), つまり (conclusion), なぜなら (because), また (addition), まず (sequence).

**Dokkai (読解)** — The reading comprehension section of the JLPT. Literally "reading and comprehending." Tests extraction of meaning from written text, not vocabulary recognition alone.

**経験 (けいけん) vs. 体験 (たいけん)** — Near-synonym pair for "experience." 経験 = accumulated experience built over time. 体験 = a specific lived event, often first-time or one-off.

**Go-on (呉音)** — One of three historical waves of Chinese importation that produced on'yomi readings. 4th–6th century CE, from the Wu region of southeastern China. The earliest layer.

**I+1 input** — A vocabulary acquisition method (from Krashen's Input Hypothesis) in which a sentence contains exactly one unknown word and all other words are already known. Optimal for inference-based acquisition.

**Information retrieval (情報検索)** — A JLPT N3 reading question type. The task is to locate specific data (price, date, eligibility condition) in a functional document (event flyer, timetable). Strategy: scan for the specific item requested; do not read the whole document.

**Intransitive verb** — A verb that describes an event happening on its own, with no external agent. Takes が for what undergoes the event: ドアが開く (the door opens by itself). Compare: Transitive verb.

**Jukugo (熟語)** — A compound word formed by combining two or more kanji, each read in on'yomi. The dominant vocabulary form at N3 and above. Examples: 経済 (economy), 解決 (resolution), 政策 (policy).

**Kan-on (漢音)** — The second wave of Chinese importation (7th–9th century, Tang Dynasty). The source of the majority of modern on'yomi readings. 

**Kanji damage** — A popular spaced repetition kanji learning system using humorous mnemonics. One of several tools for systematic kanji acquisition. See also: WaniKani.

**Keigo (敬語)** — The Japanese system of honorific speech, including respectful forms (尊敬語), humble forms (謙譲語), and polite forms (丁寧語). Register distinctions in Sessions 02, 03, and 09 are the entry point to keigo at N3.

**Kun'yomi (訓読み)** — The native Japanese reading of a kanji — the Japanese word that was mapped onto the character's meaning when kanji arrived from China. Conventionally listed in hiragana in dictionaries. Used when: kanji appears standalone, or kanji appears with okurigana (hiragana suffix).

**Mnemonic (記憶術)** — A memory device. For kanji: a story connecting a character's radical components to its meaning and reading, making it memorable.

**Nanori (名乗り)** — A third category of kanji readings used exclusively in proper names. Not predictable from on'yomi or kun'yomi rules. Furigana is the only reliable guide.

**Non-volitional verb** — A verb that describes a perception or state that happens to the speaker without deliberate direction. Takes が: 山が見える (the mountain is visible — it presents itself). Examples: 見える, 聞こえる, わかる, できる.

**Okurigana (送り仮名)** — The hiragana suffix attached to a kanji in verbs and adjectives: 食べ*る*, 高*い*. The key visual signal that the kanji is in its kun'yomi reading.

**On'yomi (音読み)** — The Chinese-derived pronunciation of a kanji, imported from China with the character. Conventionally listed in KATAKANA in dictionaries. Used almost universally in jukugo (two-kanji compound words).

**原因 (げんいん) vs. 理由 (りゆう)** — Near-synonym pair for "cause/reason." 原因 = a physical or causal cause of an event. 理由 = a stated reason or human justification.

**Radical** — See Bushu.

**Register** — The formality level of language appropriate to a social context. At N3, register distinctions become vocabulary-level requirements: 友達 vs. 友人, 言う vs. 述べる, すごい vs. 素晴らしい are register pairs, not stylistic options.

**Rendaku (連濁)** — Sequential voicing: the initial consonant of the second element in a kun'yomi compound softens (k→g, s→z, t→d, h→b/p). Example: 手 (te) + 紙 (kami) → 手紙 (tegami, letter).

**Referential expression** — A word or phrase that refers back to something earlier in the text: これ (this), それ (that), この事, その結果. Identifying referents is a recurring JLPT N3 question type.

**Sentence card** — An Anki flashcard with a complete Japanese sentence on the front (with the target word used naturally in context) and the meaning of the target word on the back. Recommended over word cards at N3 level because it encodes collocation, register, and context.

**Skim-scan-dive** — A three-step reading strategy for N3 passages under time pressure. SCAN questions first → SKIM for structure (first sentences, last sentence, connectives) → DIVE to the relevant section to verify the answer.

**SRS (Spaced Repetition System)** — A study method that schedules flashcard review at optimal intervals (just before forgetting) to maximize long-term retention. Implemented in tools like Anki.

**Tō-on (唐音)** — The third wave of Chinese importation (1185–1573), from later medieval Chinese. The layer of on'yomi closest to modern Mandarin.

**Transitive verb** — A verb that takes a direct object — something the agent acts upon. Takes を: ドアを開ける (I open the door). Compare: Intransitive verb.

**Trap answer** — A deliberately misleading answer choice in a multiple-choice reading question. Three types: half-truths (correct about part of the passage but missing a key contrast), extreme words (絶対に, 必ず), and irrelevant correctness (true but doesn't answer the question asked).

**Volitional verb** — A verb that describes a deliberate, chosen action. Takes を for the direct object: 星を見る (I look at the stars — I directed my gaze). Contrast: Non-volitional verb.

**訪ねる (たずねる)** — Visit a person (neutral register). Distinguished from: 訪れる (おとずれる, visit a place or literary arrival), 訪問する (formal compound verb), 伺う (humble speech for visiting a superior), 尋ねる (to ask/inquire — same pronunciation, different kanji).

**WaniKani** — A web-based spaced repetition kanji learning system. Teaches on'yomi with the kanji, then kun'yomi through vocabulary. A Tier 5 practitioner resource widely used in the Japanese learning community.
