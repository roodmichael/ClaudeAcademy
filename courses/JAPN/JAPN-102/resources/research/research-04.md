## Core Concepts

### Why Near-Synonyms Matter at N3

N3 is the level at which Japanese vocabulary diverges from simple translation equivalents. In N4, vocabulary is largely one-to-one: 食べる = to eat, 見る = to see, 話す = to talk. At N3, the vocabulary expands to include multiple words covering similar territory, each with distinct nuance, register, particle behavior, or contextual restriction. This is the level at which learners stop failing because they don't know a word, and start failing because they don't know which word.

The near-synonym problem has two components:
1. **Semantic nuance:** 経験 vs. 体験, 理由 vs. 原因 — they don't mean exactly the same thing
2. **Register and collocation:** 言う vs. 述べる vs. 話す — they may mean the same thing in different contexts or formality levels

### The Intentionality/Volitionality Distinction

One of the most systematic N3 nuance categories is the split between volitional (intentional) and non-volitional (spontaneous/perceptual) verbs. This appears in multiple pairs:

**見る vs. 見える:**
- 見る (みる) = to look intentionally. You direct your gaze. Takes を particle for the thing seen.
- 見える (みえる) = to be visible, to appear. Something enters your field of vision without your direction. Takes が particle for what is visible.
- 星を見る = "I look at the stars" (intentional)
- 星が見える = "The stars are visible" (they appear; no act of volition)
- 山が見える = "You can see the mountain from here" (perceptual — the mountain presents itself)

**聞く vs. 聞こえる:**
- 聞く (きく) = to listen intentionally. Also "to ask." Takes を particle.
- 聞こえる (きこえる) = to be audible, to reach the ears. Takes が particle.
- 音楽を聞く = "I listen to music" (intentional)
- 音楽が聞こえる = "I can hear music" (it reaches my ears)
- 声が聞こえる = "I can hear a voice" (not: I'm actively listening for one)

**The deeper principle:** This volitional/non-volitional split maps onto Japanese grammar: non-volitional verbs take が, not を. This means choosing the wrong verb also corrupts the particle, and vice versa. Students who learn verb pairs must learn the particle pair simultaneously.

### The Visiting/Inquiring Triple

Three verbs share a reading (たずねる) or overlapping meaning fields around visiting and asking:

**訪ねる (たずねる) — visit a person:**
- Object is always a person (or their home/workplace as their proxy)
- Takes を particle: 友人を訪ねる (visit a friend)
- Intentional, relationship-focused
- Casual to neutral register

**訪れる (おとずれる) — visit a place:**
- Object is a location: 神社を訪れる (visit a shrine), 京都を訪れる (visit Kyoto)
- Extended meaning: abstract arrivals. 春が訪れた (spring has arrived). This poetic usage treats an abstract phenomenon as a visitor.
- More literary register than 訪ねる

**尋ねる (たずねる) — ask/inquire:**
- Same pronunciation as 訪ねる; distinguished by kanji (尋 = seek/inquire)
- The thing asked about takes を: 道を尋ねる (ask for directions), 名前を尋ねる (ask someone's name)
- The person asked takes に: 先生に尋ねる (ask the teacher)
- More formal than 聞く for "ask"; less formal than the honorific 伺う

**Register ladder for visiting:**
- Plain casual: 行く (just go there)
- Neutral formal: 訪ねる (visit, person-focused)
- Literary/elevated: 訪れる (visit, place or abstract)
- Business formal: 訪問する (formal compound verb)
- Humble honorific: 伺う (うかがう) — used when visiting a superior's workplace or home

### Speaking and Saying Verbs

Three verbs cover the territory of "to speak/say":

**言う (いう) — to say:**
- Simple statement, any register, casual or formal
- 何を言うんですか = "What are you saying?"
- Short utterances, direct speech

**話す (はなす) — to talk/speak:**
- Extended interaction, conversational exchange
- 日本語で話す = "speak in Japanese"
- 友達と話す = "talk with a friend"
- Emphasizes communication as a process, not just output

**述べる (のべる) — to state/declare:**
- Formal, often written or official contexts
- 意見を述べる = "state one's opinion"
- 感想を述べる = "express one's impressions"
- Used in presentations, official statements, academic writing
- Would sound stilted in casual conversation

### Receiving/Getting Verbs

**受ける (うける) — to receive something directed at you:**
- Something comes toward the receiver: サービスを受ける (receive service), 治療を受ける (receive treatment)
- The receiver is passive; the thing received was initiated from outside

**得る (える) — to achieve/gain:**
- Something earned or achieved: 知識を得る (gain knowledge), 許可を得る (obtain permission), 信頼を得る (earn trust)
- Agency is with the gainer; they worked for or sought what they got

**手に入れる (てにいれる) — to acquire/get one's hands on:**
- Active acquisition of a concrete or desired object: チケットを手に入れる (get tickets), 情報を手に入れる (get information)
- Most colloquial of the three; emphasizes effort and success

### Transitive/Intransitive Pairs as Near-Synonyms

Japanese transitive and intransitive verb pairs create one of the most common sources of N3 confusion. Students often know one verb in a pair but not the other, or confuse which takes を and which takes が [tofugu.com]:

| Transitive (with を) | Meaning | Intransitive (with が) | Meaning |
|---------------------|---------|----------------------|---------|
| 開ける (あける) | to open [it] | 開く (あく) | to open [itself] |
| 閉める (しめる) | to close [it] | 閉まる (しまる) | to close [itself] |
| 入れる (いれる) | to put in | 入る (はいる) | to enter |
| 出す (だす) | to take out | 出る (でる) | to go out / come out |
| 落とす (おとす) | to drop | 落ちる (おちる) | to fall |
| 変える (かえる) | to change [it] | 変わる (かわる) | to change [itself] |
| 決める (きめる) | to decide | 決まる (きまる) | to be decided |
| 止める (とめる) | to stop [it] | 止まる (とまる) | to stop [itself] |
| 育てる (そだてる) | to raise [a child] | 育つ (そだつ) | to grow up |
| 消す (けす) | to turn off/erase | 消える (きえる) | to disappear/go out |

**Critical note:** Morphological patterns (e.g., -eru/-u or -su/-ru) are unreliable predictors of transitivity. Studies show guessing from pattern works only ~20% of the time. These pairs must be learned as pairs with their particles, not inferred from form [tofugu.com].

### Adjective Nuance: Formality Registers

**すごい vs. 素晴らしい:**
- すごい (sugoi) = casual expression of amazement ("Wow, amazing!")
- 素晴らしい (subarashii) = formal admiration, deeper aesthetic approval ("Truly wonderful")
- すごい in formal writing sounds childish; 素晴らしい in casual speech sounds stiff

**美しい vs. 綺麗な:**
- 美しい (うつくしい) = aesthetic or moral beauty — art, nature, character
- 綺麗な (きれいな) = attractive, clean, orderly — includes cleanliness and neatness
- 綺麗な水 can mean "clean water" (not just beautiful water); 美しい水 would be purely aesthetic

### Noun Register Pairs

The same concept often has casual/formal variants:

| Casual | Formal | Meaning |
|--------|--------|---------|
| 友達 (ともだち) | 友人 (ゆうじん) | friend |
| 家 (いえ/うち) | 住宅 (じゅうたく) | house/home |
| 本 (ほん) | 書物 (しょもつ) | book |
| 先生 (せんせい) | 教師 (きょうし) | teacher |

Using casual forms in formal writing (business letters, official documents, academic contexts) reads as unprofessional at N3.

### The Experience and Reason Pairs

**経験 (けいけん) vs. 体験 (たいけん):**
- 経験 = accumulated experience, built up over time through repeated exposure: 教師の経験 (teaching experience), 海外経験 (overseas experience)
- 体験 = a specific lived event, often a one-time or first-time occurrence: 体験談 (personal experience story), 体験入学 (trial class), スカイダイビングを体験する (experience skydiving)

**理由 (りゆう) vs. 原因 (げんいん):**
- 理由 = a stated reason or justification (social/intentional explanation): なぜ来なかったの？理由を教えて (Tell me your reason for not coming)
- 原因 = a physical cause or causal factor (factual/analytical): 事故の原因は何ですか (What was the cause of the accident?)
- 理由 appears after human decisions/choices; 原因 appears after phenomena and events

## Key Definitions

**Collocation:** A word combination that native speakers use habitually and which sounds "wrong" when one element is substituted by a synonym. 影響を与える (give influence/affect) is correct; 影響を送る (*send* influence) is not. Collocations cannot always be predicted from the meanings of individual words.

**Register:** The formality level appropriate to a social situation. In Japanese, register determines not just vocabulary choice but grammatical forms, sentence endings, and honorific usage.

**Semantic transparency:** How predictable a compound's meaning is from its parts. 経済 (economy: 経 pass-through + 済 settle) has moderate transparency. 油を売る (loaf around, literally "sell oil") has near-zero transparency and must be learned as a fixed expression.

**Transitivity pair (有対動詞, ゆうたいどうし):** A matched set of verbs sharing the same semantic core but differing in whether they take a direct object (transitive) or not (intransitive). Nearly all N3 learners must actively study these.

## Process / Mechanics

### How to Teach Nuance Discrimination

Academic research on collocation acquisition (Frontiers in Psychology, 2023) identifies key teaching principles:

1. **Present pairs/triples together, not individually.** Presenting 見る and then weeks later 見える produces independent memory traces. Presenting them as a pair, with contrasting sentences, builds relational understanding.

2. **Contrast in minimal pairs.** Same sentence frame, different verb: 星を見る / 星が見える. The structural contrast makes the semantic difference visible.

3. **Incorporate auditory practice.** Research shows that visual-only learning of collocations masks comprehension gaps — learners who can recognize the distinction in reading often cannot apply it in listening [Frontiers in Psychology, 2023].

4. **Address L1 interference explicitly.** English speakers do not have a volitional/non-volitional distinction for "see" vs. "can see" as a grammatically significant category. This must be made explicit, not left to inference.

5. **Teach the particle alongside the verb.** For transitive/intransitive pairs and for 見る/見える, the particle change is inseparable from the verb choice. Teaching the verb without the particle produces half-knowledge.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Using 見る where 見える is required.** "I can see the mountain" → *山を見る (wrong — implies you chose to look) vs. 山が見える (correct — the mountain is visible to you). Students default to 見る because it's more familiar.

2. **Confusing 訪ねる and 尋ねる** — both read たずねる. Students write the wrong kanji because they learned the reading without learning which kanji pairs with which meaning.

3. **Using 言う for formal speaking.** A student who writes "報告を言いました" in a business email should have used "報告を述べました." The casual 言う sounds unprofessional.

4. **Applying transitive verbs without the direct object particle.** Students who have learned 閉める often write "ドアが閉めました" instead of "ドアを閉めました." The が vs. を error is the giveaway that transitive/intransitive was not internalized.

5. **Using 理由 for physical causes.** "事故の理由は何ですか" — the accident doesn't have a reason (social justification); it has a 原因 (physical cause). This is the most common near-synonym error in N3 exam practice.

6. **Pattern-guessing transitivity.** Students who try to predict transitivity from verb form (-eru = transitive? -u = intransitive?) will be wrong approximately 80% of the time [tofugu.com].

## Hands-On Elements

- **Minimal pair contrast sentences:** For each major pair (見る/見える, 聞く/聞こえる, 訪ねる/尋ねる), provide 5 sentences that use both in contrast. Students explain why each is correct.
- **Particle focus drill:** Provide transitive/intransitive verb pairs. Give context sentences with の blank for the particle. Students fill in を or が and justify.
- **Register classification:** Present 10 situations (job interview, texting a friend, writing a university report). Students choose the correct word from a register pair for each situation.
- **Near-synonym discrimination JLPT practice format:** Sentence fill-in-the-blank with 4 near-synonym options. Mirrors the actual JLPT vocabulary question format.
- **理由 vs. 原因 analysis:** Give 10 scenarios (car accident, missed appointment, won award, weather changed). Students assign 理由 or 原因 and explain.
- **Collocation chain building:** Start with a new N3 noun (影響, 可能性, 信頼). Students use jisho.org to find 5 verbs that collocate with it. Compare chains. Build a class collocation dictionary.

## Expert Debates / Open Questions

**Whether to teach morphological transitivity patterns:** Tofugu explicitly warns against teaching -eru/-u or -su/-ru patterns as transitivity predictors, calling the results "a mess" that works 20% of the time. Some grammar textbooks still teach the patterns with caveats. The research-supported position is: learn pairs as pairs, avoid pattern-prediction.

**Whether nuance is "grammar" or "vocabulary":** The 見る/見える distinction is simultaneously a grammar distinction (volitional vs. non-volitional; を vs. が) and a vocabulary distinction (two different lexical items). At N3, the boundary between grammar and vocabulary becomes genuinely blurry. This is actually pedagogically valuable — it reinforces that the two domains inform each other.

**How much register teaching belongs at N3 vs. later levels:** Some instructors defer formal/casual register distinctions to N2 level. However, at N3, register errors appear regularly on the exam and in authentic situations. Introducing register awareness early — even if not requiring mastery — is the majority practitioner position.

## Connections to Adjacent Topics

- **Research-01 (Vocabulary acquisition):** Near-synonym pairs are the advanced face of vocabulary acquisition at N3. Abstract nouns (経験/体験, 理由/原因) must be learned with their collocations.
- **Research-03 (On'yomi/Kun'yomi):** Register distinctions between verbs often align with reading type: kun'yomi verbs (訪ねる, 話す, 見る) tend to be more colloquial; on'yomi compound verbs (訪問する, 述べる) tend to be more formal.
- **JAPN-101 Grammar:** The transitive/intransitive distinction connects directly to the passive construction (research-03 of JAPN-101): only transitive verbs can be passivized into the standard passive form.
