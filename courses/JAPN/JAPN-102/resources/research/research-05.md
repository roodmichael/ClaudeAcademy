## Core Concepts

### What N3 Reading Looks Like

The JLPT official level description for N3 specifies three reading competencies [jlpt.jp]:
1. Comprehend written materials about everyday topics with specific content
2. Grasp summary information like newspaper headlines
3. Understand the main points of slightly difficult everyday writings when contextual aids are present

These three requirements define a specific range of authentic text: above purely scripted textbook dialogue (N4) and below full editorial or academic prose (N2). The N3 reading zone includes daily news, notices, informal essays, personal correspondence, and informational documents. The passages are not simplified for learners — they use natural grammar and authentic vocabulary — but they cover accessible, everyday topics.

### Passage Types on the JLPT N3 Reading Section

The N3 reading section (dokkai, 読解) contains four distinct passage categories [jlptsamurai.com, tcj-education.com, migaku.com]:

**1. Short passages (短文, ~200 characters, 4–5 questions)**
Brief focused texts with a single main point. Often: short emails, notifications, simple instructions, or single-paragraph explanations. Questions test detail comprehension, vocabulary meaning in context, and referential expression (what does "this" refer to?).

**2. Medium passages (中文, ~300–500 characters, 3 questions)**
Developed argument or comparison texts. Often: informal essays, product explanations, advice columns, or social notices. Questions test main idea, stated reasons, and contrast/cause-effect structure.

**3. Long passages (長文, ~700–800 characters, 2 questions)**
Essay-style texts with a clear argument or narrative arc. Often: opinion pieces, explanatory essays, or short personal narratives. Questions focus on the author's main claim and the logical relationship between sections.

**4. Information retrieval (情報検索, varies)**
Real-world functional documents: event flyers, Q&A pages, price lists, timetables, notices. The task is to extract specific data from a multi-element document. Questions test whether students can find the right piece of information efficiently, not read the whole document in depth.

### Text Length and Time Allocation

The reading section is combined with vocabulary and grammar in a single 100-minute block at N3. Expert guidance allocates 40–45 minutes to the reading section, leaving the remainder for vocabulary and grammar questions [jlptsamurai.com, migaku.com]. This means approximately 3 minutes per passage maximum. Students who read linearly, word-by-word, without a strategy will run out of time.

### Key Structural Markers at N3

N3 texts rely heavily on discourse connectives to signal logical structure. Recognizing these is the key to comprehension speed [tcj-education.com, jlptsamurai.com]:

**Contrast and concession:**
- しかし (however) — simple contrast
- でも (but) — casual contrast
- ところが (but/however) — unexpected result
- 〜のに (even though) — emotional contrast (covered in JAPN-101)
- 一方 (いっぽう, on the other hand) — parallel contrast

**Causation and result:**
- だから / ですから (therefore) — personal assertion
- ので (so/because) — objective cause
- その結果 (as a result)
- なぜなら (because) — introduces explanation

**Addition:**
- また (also/moreover)
- さらに (furthermore/additionally)
- それに (besides/what's more)

**Summary/conclusion:**
- つまり (in other words/that is to say)
- 要するに (in sum/to put it simply)
- というわけで (for that reason/thus)

**Sequence:**
- まず (first/to begin)
- 次に (next)
- 最後に (finally)

**Purpose:**
- 〜ために (for the purpose of)
- 〜ように (so that)

### Authentic Text Sources at N3 Level

**NHK News Web Easy (nhk.or.jp/news/easy):** The primary recommended authentic source for N3 reading practice [tofugu.com, migaku.com, jlptsamurai.com, tcj-education.com]. NHK rewrites real news stories in simplified Japanese — shorter sentences, more common vocabulary, furigana over all kanji. Key features:
- Free, updated daily with new articles
- Furigana toggle (can turn off for challenge)
- Audio recording accompanies each article
- Pop-up dictionary gloss for underlined words
- Links to the original full-speed NHK article for comparison
- Topics: politics, disasters, science, sports, culture — the same categories as JLPT reading passages

Tofugu's assessment: appropriate for "pretty much anyone" past the absolute beginner stage; roughly N4–N3 level. The original NHK articles (non-Easy) are N2–N1 [tofugu.com/japanese-learning-resources-database/nhk-news-web-easy/].

**Shin Kanzen Master N3 Reading (新完全マスター N3 読解):** The most commonly recommended exam preparation reading workbook. Provides passages calibrated to N3 level with question formats matching the exam. The answer-key explanations are detailed enough for self-study.

**N3 読解スピードマスター:** A speed-training companion that focuses on reading rate as well as accuracy. Addresses the time management problem that causes exam failure.

**Graded Readers:** Publishers like White Rabbit Press produce Japanese graded readers at N4–N3 levels that provide authentic-style narrative texts (short stories, daily life narratives) with glossaries.

**Dokushoclub.com (N3 free resources):** Aggregates free reading resources specifically for N3 level, including news summaries, graded content, and links to external sources.

## Key Definitions

**Dokkai (読解):** The reading comprehension section of the JLPT. Literally "reading and comprehending." Tests the ability to extract meaning from written text, not just to recognize vocabulary.

**Referential expression:** A word or phrase that refers back to something mentioned earlier in the text: これ (this), それ (that), そこ (there), その (that [adj.]), etc. Identifying what these refer to is a recurring question type in N3 reading.

**Information retrieval question:** A question that asks you to locate specific data in a functional document (timetable, notice, Q&A). Strategy: don't read the whole document — scan for the specific piece requested.

**Skimming:** Reading quickly to get the gist of a text, focusing on first/last sentences of paragraphs and key connectives. Distinguished from scanning (searching for a specific word or fact).

**I+1 reading text:** A reading passage that contains only one or two unknown words per sentence. Students can infer the unknown words from context. NHK Web Easy approximates this for N4–N3 learners.

**Trap answers:** In multiple-choice comprehension questions, distractors designed to mislead:
- *Half-truths*: Correct about part of the passage but miss an important contrast
- *Extreme words*: Answers using 絶対に, 全然〜ない — test false absolutes
- *Irrelevant correctness*: Factually true statements that don't answer the question asked

## Process / Mechanics

### The Skim-Scan-Dive Reading Strategy

The recommended three-step approach for N3 passages under time pressure [jlptsamurai.com]:

**Skim (60 seconds for medium passage):**
- Read the title/heading
- Read the first sentence of each paragraph
- Read the last sentence of the passage
- Goal: Identify the author's purpose and the main argument direction

**Scan (30 seconds):**
- Read the question(s) and answer options
- Identify the key term or concept being asked about
- Goal: Know exactly what you are looking for before diving

**Dive:**
- Return to the passage with the question's key term in mind
- Locate the relevant paragraph or sentence
- Confirm the answer by verifying it matches the passage logic (not just the surface wording)

This order is critical. Students who read the whole passage first, then read the questions, waste time re-reading text they already covered. Reading questions first redirects attention efficiently.

### Handling Unknown Words During Reading

At N3, students will encounter unknown words even in appropriately-leveled texts. The recommended strategies [jlptsamurai.com, rapid-jt.com, tcj-education.com]:

1. **Radical inference:** Break down unknown kanji into components. 再発見 = 再 (again) + 発 (emit/arise) + 見 (see) = "rediscovery." Even without knowing the full compound, the approximate meaning is accessible.

2. **Contextual inference:** Read the sentences surrounding the unknown word. The discourse connectives (しかし, だから, つまり) tell you the logical relationship, which often pins down the rough meaning.

3. **Part-of-speech cue:** If the unknown word is followed by する, it's a verb. If it's followed by な, it's a na-adjective. If it's followed by の, it could be a noun modifier. Part-of-speech inference helps even without knowing the meaning.

4. **Tolerance for uncertainty:** A key metacognitive skill at N3. Students who freeze on an unknown word and try to resolve it lose time. The trained behavior: note the word, estimate meaning from context, continue reading, return if the question directly targets that word.

5. **The 3-minute rule:** Maximum 3 minutes per passage. Flag and move. Return only with remaining time [jlptsamurai.com].

### Question Type Strategies

**Main idea questions:** Look for the last paragraph or concluding sentence. The author's main point is almost always stated explicitly. Watch for つまり, 要するに, というわけで as signals.

**Referential expression questions:** Find the referential expression (これ, その事, etc.) in the passage. Look at the immediately preceding clause or sentence — that is almost always the referent.

**Reason/cause questions:** Look backward from the effect statement. Japanese often places the reason clause before the result: [reason]ので [result]. Also look for なぜなら…からです structures.

**Contrast questions:** Find the contrast marker (しかし, ところが, 一方). The answer to a contrast question is almost always in the clause after the contrast marker.

**Author's attitude/opinion questions:** The author's viewpoint is usually stated in the first or last paragraph. Watch for opinion markers: と思う, と考える, 〜ではないだろうか (rhetorical question suggesting the author's view).

**Information retrieval questions:** Read the question first. Note the specific information requested (date, price, eligibility condition). Scan the document for that specific category. Do not read irrelevantly.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Linear reading without strategy.** Reading every word from start to finish before looking at the questions is the most time-consuming approach. Students who do this run out of time on medium and long passages.

2. **Getting stuck on unknown words.** One unknown word per sentence is acceptable; inference from context is a skill, not a failure. Students who spend 60 seconds trying to decode a single character cannot finish the section.

3. **Choosing answers that "sound right" rather than verifying in the text.** Trap answers are designed to sound plausible based on world knowledge or partial passage comprehension. Every answer choice should be verifiable with a specific line in the passage.

4. **Missing referential expressions.** Questions like "What does 'それ' in line 8 refer to?" are N3 exam staples. Students who read for content meaning but ignore discourse structure miss these. Practice: every time you encounter これ/それ/あれ/その+noun in authentic text, explicitly identify the referent.

5. **Ignoring conjunctions.** Students who skip discourse markers (しかし, つまり, なぜなら) lose the logical architecture of the text. The exam specifically tests whether students understood the logical relationship between clauses, not just the surface content.

6. **Reading NHK Web Easy with grammar look-up enabled and counting it as comprehension practice.** Looking up every unknown word teaches vocabulary but not reading fluency. Authentic reading practice should be timed, with dictionary use limited to post-reading review.

## Hands-On Elements

- **Daily NHK Web Easy reading:** Assign 1–2 articles per week. First pass: read without dictionary (furigana on). Note words you don't know. Second pass: check the original NHK article (more difficult). Log new vocabulary in SRS deck.
- **Skim-Scan-Dive timed drill:** Give a medium passage (300–400 characters) with 3 questions. Set a 3-minute timer. Apply the skim-scan-dive strategy. Compare results when same students read linearly vs. strategically.
- **Discourse marker map:** Take a medium NHK Web Easy article. Students underline all connectives and draw arrows showing the logical flow (cause→effect, contrast, addition). Discuss what the author is arguing.
- **Referential expression tracking:** Take a paragraph with multiple referential expressions. Students must explicitly identify the referent of each これ/それ/その/etc. before answering any content questions.
- **Trap answer analysis:** Provide 5 multiple-choice questions from N3 practice materials. Students must identify which answer is the trap (half-truth, extreme word, irrelevant correctness) before selecting the correct answer.
- **Information retrieval race:** Provide a realistic functional document (event flyer, train schedule) with 3 specific questions. Students race to find all three answers in under 90 seconds. Discuss what scanning strategy they used.

## Expert Debates / Open Questions

**NHK Web Easy level dispute:** Some practitioners assess NHK Web Easy at N4–N3 (appropriate for N3 preparation); others find it slightly below N3 and recommend supplementing with original NHK articles after a few months. For this course, the consensus is: use NHK Web Easy as the primary authentic text source, with original articles as stretch material.

**Should N3 reading practice include a dictionary or not?** Practitioner opinions vary. The majority view: timed reading practice should be done without dictionary (to build inference skills); subsequent vocabulary review should use jisho.org or Anki. Always using a dictionary builds passive recognition; never using one creates frustration.

**Graded readers vs. authentic texts for intermediate learners:** Graded readers are calibrated to level but are not authentic Japanese. Authentic texts (NHK Web Easy, actual notices, real emails) expose learners to genuine discourse patterns but may contain occasional vocabulary above level. The current practitioner consensus at N3 is to use both, prioritizing authentic texts as skills develop.

## Connections to Adjacent Topics

- **Research-01 (Vocabulary acquisition):** Reading comprehension is impossible without vocabulary. The N3 reading level requires the approximately 3,750-word base to be functional. Spaced repetition of vocabulary directly feeds reading fluency.
- **Research-02 (Kanji expansion):** Kanji recognition is the bottleneck for reading speed. A student who must sound out or skip N3 kanji compounds cannot read at exam speed.
- **Research-04 (Near-synonyms):** The exam reading section tests nuance comprehension — distinguishing 理由 from 原因 in a passage, recognizing that 訪れた means a place was visited (not a person), choosing which verb fits the context. Near-synonym knowledge feeds directly into reading question accuracy.
- **JAPN-101 Grammar:** The connective structures from JAPN-101 — から/ので, が/けど, のに, たり〜たりする, ながら — all appear in N3 reading passages. Grammatical understanding of clause connections is prerequisite to logical comprehension.
