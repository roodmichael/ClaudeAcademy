# Session 01: The N4 to N3 Leap
**Course:** JAPN-102
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to describe what qualitatively changes at N3 — the abstraction shift, the register dimension, and the compound vocabulary structure — and design a study approach for vocabulary acquisition at this level.

---

## Opening

Here is a thought experiment: imagine you have learned ~1,500 Japanese words and ~300 kanji. You pick up an NHK news article. You recognize most of the particles. You recognize the grammar patterns from JAPN-101. But the *content words* — the nouns and verbs that carry the actual meaning — are almost all unknown to you.

This is the N4 wall. It is not a grammar problem. It is a vocabulary problem.

JAPN-101 gave you the grammar skeleton of N3 Japanese. This course builds the vocabulary and kanji to fill it in. The question for this session: what exactly are you missing, and what kind of learning will actually close that gap?

---

## The Scale of the Jump

The numbers first. JLPT N4 requires approximately 1,500 words. JLPT N3 requires approximately 3,750 words. That is roughly **2,200 new words** you need to acquire — more than you have learned in all of N5 and N4 combined.

But the number is the least important thing. The important thing is what kind of words they are.

At N4, almost all vocabulary is **concrete and situational**: body parts, household objects, common verbs of daily routine, family members, colors, numbers, basic adjectives. The words refer to things you can point to or actions you can demonstrate. 水 (water). 食べる (to eat). 電車 (train). 高い (expensive/tall).

At N3, the vocabulary shifts. The new words are predominantly:

1. **Abstract** — 影響 (えいきょう, influence), 可能性 (かのうせい, possibility), 原因 (げんいん, cause), 改善 (かいぜん, improvement). These are not things you can point to.

2. **Two-kanji compounds (熟語, jukugo)** — Two or more kanji paired together, each read in its Chinese-derived pronunciation. The dominant vocabulary form at N3 and above. 経験 (けいけん, experience), 解決 (かいけつ, solution), 連絡 (れんらく, contact).

3. **Register-sensitive** — The same concept often has a casual and a formal variant at N3. 使う vs. 利用する (to use). 友達 vs. 友人 (friend). 先生 vs. 教師 (teacher). At N4, choosing the "wrong" register is forgivable. At N3, it signals that you don't quite belong in the level.

## The Thematic Shape of N3 Vocabulary

N3 vocabulary spans 18+ thematic domains. Some domains are familiar from N4 (daily life, travel, family). Others are new at N3:

- **Business and economy:** 取引 (transaction), 契約 (contract), 利益 (profit)
- **Government and law:** 政府 (government), 権利 (rights), 法律 (law)
- **Science and technology:** 技術 (technology), 開発 (development), 機能 (function)
- **Nature and environment:** 気候 (climate), 資源 (resources), 環境 (environment)
- **Health and medicine:** 症状 (symptoms), 治療 (treatment), 予防 (prevention)
- **Emotions and psychology:** 感情 (emotion), 不安 (anxiety), 信頼 (trust)
- **Change and causation:** 変化 (change), 原因 (cause), 結果 (result)

You will not study all 18 domains in this course. Sessions 02 and 03 focus on the highest-frequency N3 vocabulary — the words that appear most often in JLPT reading passages and in everyday Japanese. You will extend from there using the strategies this session introduces.

## How to Actually Acquire 2,200 Words

There is an important difference between *knowing* a word and *knowing* a word.

You might recognize 影響 if you see it on a flashcard. But if you cannot recall it within half a second while reading, it is not truly available to you. JLPT reading questions give you roughly three minutes per passage. There is no time to "almost remember" a word.

Research on vocabulary acquisition consistently finds that a word needs to be encountered **10–20 times in varied contexts** before it enters reliable long-term memory. A single flashcard encounter counts for almost nothing compared to seeing the word in a sentence, then in a reading passage, then using it yourself.

This course recommends a four-stage acquisition funnel:

**Stage 1 — First encounter:** Learn the word's core meaning and reading. A themed vocabulary list or Anki deck deck gets you here.

**Stage 2 — Sentence encounters (repetitions 2–5):** See the word used in example sentences. The Bunpro and Jisho.org entries for most N3 vocabulary include multiple example sentences. This is where form-meaning links are reinforced in context.

**Stage 3 — Authentic reading (repetitions 6–10):** Encounter the word in NHK Web Easy articles. This is where the word becomes functional — you stop "finding" it and start simply *reading* it.

**Stage 4 — Production (repetitions 10+):** Use the word in your own sentences. Written exercises and practice tests. This cements active recall.

Sessions 02 and 03 start you at Stage 1. The exercises in those sessions push you toward Stage 2. The reading sessions (10 and 11) bring you to Stage 3. The final project is Stage 4.

## The SRS Strategy Pivot

At N4, simple word-to-English flashcards (front: 電車, back: "train") are adequate. At N3, they are not — because N3 vocabulary is too abstract and too collocation-dependent for isolated word memorization.

The recommended N3 upgrade is the **sentence card**:

- **Front:** A Japanese sentence with the target word used naturally. Ideally, all other words in the sentence are already known (this is called "I+1" — input plus one unknown item).
- **Back:** The sentence with the meaning of the target word highlighted, plus its reading.
- **No English translation of the whole sentence** — just the meaning of the target word.

Why sentence cards? Because N3 vocabulary often carries hidden information that a word-card cannot capture:

- 影響 collocates with 与える: 影響を与える (to have an influence on, literally "give influence"). You won't know this from a word card.
- 可能性 collocates with がある: 可能性がある (there is a possibility). Knowing the word without the collocation leaves you unable to use it.

We will practice sentence card construction in Session 02. For now: if you are not already using Anki, download it and set up a Japanese deck. You will need it from Session 02 onward.

## A First Encounter: Three Defining N3 Near-Synonym Pairs

N3 vocabulary includes many pairs of words that look similar in English translation but differ in Japanese usage. Two pairs are so important they appear constantly in JLPT exam questions. Introducing them now, before they appear in dedicated sessions, gives your brain more time to process them.

**理由 (りゆう) vs. 原因 (げんいん)**
Both translate loosely as "reason" or "cause." But:
- 理由 = a stated reason or justification. It answers "why did you do that?" It belongs to human decisions.
- 原因 = a physical or causal cause. It answers "what caused this to happen?" It belongs to events and phenomena.

You give a 理由 for being late to class. A car accident has a 原因.

**経験 (けいけん) vs. 体験 (たいけん)**
Both translate loosely as "experience." But:
- 経験 = accumulated experience built up over time. You 経験 teaching after 10 years in the classroom.
- 体験 = a specific lived event, often first-time or one-off. You 体験 skydiving.

We will drill these in Session 09. Exposure now primes the distinction.

## Why This Matters

You already have the grammar. JAPN-101 gave you the structures. What keeps N3 reading passages opaque is not that the grammar is wrong — it is that too many content words are unknown. Every N3 word you acquire makes every reading passage a little more transparent. This course is about closing that gap systematically and permanently, not by cramming before an exam but by building a vocabulary that is actually available to you in real time.

---

## Key Takeaways

- N3 requires approximately 2,200 new words — a bigger jump than N5 to N4, and qualitatively different: abstract, compound, and register-sensitive.
- The majority of new N3 vocabulary is **jukugo** — two-kanji compounds read in on'yomi. This connects vocabulary acquisition directly to the kanji expansion you will build in Sessions 04–06.
- Vocabulary acquisition requires multiple encounters in varied contexts: flashcard → sentence → authentic reading → production. This course is designed to move you through that funnel.
- Switch from word cards to **sentence cards** in Anki at N3. The collocation context matters.
- Start noticing 理由 vs. 原因 and 経験 vs. 体験 in anything you read. You will meet them properly in Session 09.
