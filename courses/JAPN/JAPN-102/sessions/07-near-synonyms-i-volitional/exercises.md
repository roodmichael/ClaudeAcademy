# Session 07 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

---

## Exercise 1: Volitional Test — Verb Choice
**Type:** Analysis

For each situation, decide whether 見る or 見える is correct, and which particle goes with it. Write the complete verb phrase (particle + verb).

1. You are standing at a bus stop and notice the bus approaching in the distance.
   → バスが/をが __________。

2. You deliberately look at a photograph your friend sent you.
   → 写真を/が __________。

3. You are in a tall building and the city is visible below.
   → 都市が/を __________。

4. A doctor asks a patient to look at a specific point.
   → あの点を/が __________ください。

5. The stars aren't visible tonight because of clouds.
   → 雲で星が/を __________ません。

---

## Exercise 2: 聞く vs. 聞こえる — Particle and Verb
**Type:** Analysis

Same task for hearing. For each situation, decide which verb is correct and write the complete verb phrase.

1. You put on headphones and deliberately play your favorite album.
   → 音楽を/が __________。

2. You are studying and you suddenly notice someone shouting in the street.
   → 外から声が/を __________。

3. A teacher says to a distracted student: "Please listen to what I'm saying."
   → 私の話を/が __________ください。

4. The lecture hall's acoustics are terrible — the professor's words aren't reaching you.
   → 先生の声が/を よく __________ません。

5. You want to hear how someone's audition went.
   → オーディションの話を/が __________たい。

## Exercise 3: Error Analysis
**Type:** Analysis

Each sentence below contains an error in verb choice or particle. Identify the error and rewrite the sentence correctly.

1. 窓から山を見えます。(I can see the mountain from the window.)
2. 昨日、コンサートで音楽が聞きました。(Yesterday I listened to music at the concert.)
3. 遠くから彼女の声を聞こえた。(Her voice reached me from far away.)
4. この映画を見えたことがありますか。(Have you seen this movie?)

For each corrected sentence, explain in one sentence why the original was wrong.

## Exercise 4: Broader Pattern Connection
**Type:** Thought experiment

The lesson explained that わかる (to understand) and できる (to be capable) also take が, not を. They are non-volitional: understanding and ability exist or don't, you cannot "act upon" them.

**Task:** Think of one more Japanese verb you know that might be non-volitional — a verb describing a perception or state that happens to you rather than an action you choose to take. Write a sentence using it with が, and explain why you think it is non-volitional.

If you can't think of one, try these candidates and analyze whether they are volitional or non-volitional: 感じる (to feel), 気づく (to notice), 思う (to think/feel).

**What to observe:** 感じる is interesting — it is arguably more volitional than 見える but less so than 見る. This is the gray zone in the distinction. That gray zone is worth noticing.
