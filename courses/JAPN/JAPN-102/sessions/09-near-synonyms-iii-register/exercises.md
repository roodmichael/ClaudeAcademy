## Exercise 1: Visiting Verb Selection
**Type:** Analysis

For each situation, choose the most appropriate visiting verb from the ladder: 行く、訪ねる、訪れる、訪問する、伺う

1. You are texting a friend about dropping by their apartment tonight.
2. You are a tourist visiting a famous temple in Kyoto.
3. A diplomat is making an official state visit to a foreign government.
4. You are a junior employee calling your senior colleague's company: 明日、御社に____________います。
5. You are writing a poem about autumn arriving as if it were a visitor.
6. You want to ask directions to a place — which verb do you use for "asking"? (Hint: it's not in the visiting ladder but was in the lesson.)

## Exercise 2: 理由 vs. 原因 — Discrimination Drill
**Type:** Analysis

For each scenario, decide whether 理由 or 原因 fits. Write a full sentence using the correct word.

1. You are explaining to your teacher why you missed class.
2. Scientists are investigating what caused an outbreak of illness in a city.
3. A job applicant is asked why they want to work at this company.
4. A news article reports what caused the bridge to collapse.
5. A friend asks why you've been looking worried lately.
6. Engineers are analyzing the factor that caused the engine to fail.

## Exercise 4: 経験 vs. 体験 — Application
**Type:** Thought experiment

Decide which word fits each context. Then explain why in one sentence.

1. A job posting asks for 5年以上の教育___ (5+ years of education ___)
2. A travel company advertises: 本物の和食を___ しよう！(Experience authentic Japanese food!)
3. A curriculum vitae line: 海外___3年 (3 years of overseas ___)
4. An events page: スカイダイビング___ツアー (Skydiving ___ tour)
5. An expert introduces themselves: 20年の___を持つエンジニア (An engineer with 20 years of ___)

**What to observe:** Does the distinction feel intuitive at this point, or are there borderline cases? Scenario 3 (海外___3年) is a genuine borderline — either word appears in natural Japanese. Note why.
