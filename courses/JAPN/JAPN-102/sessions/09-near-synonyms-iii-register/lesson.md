# Session 09: Near-Synonyms III — Register, Collocations, and Nuance
**Course:** JAPN-102
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to discriminate between register-based near-synonym clusters (speaking verbs, visiting verbs, getting verbs), correctly apply 理由 vs. 原因 and 経験 vs. 体験, and choose register-appropriate adjectives and nouns for formal vs. casual contexts.

---

## Opening

Here is a test. You need to tell a colleague's superior that you are going to visit their office tomorrow. Which verb do you use?

- 行く (go)
- 来る (come)
- 訪ねる (visit a person)
- 訪れる (visit a place)
- 訪問する (formal visit)
- 伺う (humble visit to a superior)

All six of these could describe the act of going to an office. But only one is appropriate in this social context. That is what register-sensitive vocabulary means at N3: knowing the word is not enough. You need to know when it fits.

---

## The Visiting Verb Ladder

Five verbs cover the semantic territory of "visiting" in Japanese, arranged by formality and object type:

**行く** — to go (generic)
No visiting nuance. 明日行きます just means "I'll go tomorrow."

**訪ねる (たずねる)** — to visit a person
The object is always a person (or their home as proxy). Casual to neutral register.
- 友人を訪ねる (visit a friend)
- 先生の家を訪ねる (visit the teacher's home — the home stands in for the teacher)
Takes を particle.

**訪れる (おとずれる)** — to visit a place; literary arrival of abstract things
The object is typically a location. More literary than 訪ねる. Extended meaning: abstract arrivals.
- 神社を訪れる (visit a shrine)
- 春が訪れた (spring has arrived — poetic; spring is treated as a visitor)
Takes を particle.

**訪問する (ほうもんする)** — formal visit
The formal compound verb. Used in business contexts, official language, news articles.
- 大使が首相を訪問した (The ambassador paid an official visit to the prime minister)

**伺う (うかがう)** — humble visit
The humble speech (謙譲語) form. Used when visiting a superior — you lower yourself, raise them.
- 明日、御社に伺います (I will visit your esteemed company tomorrow — business context)
- 先生のご自宅に伺ってもよろしいですか (May I visit your home, sensei?)

**The register ladder for "visiting a superior's office":**
Plain → 行く → 訪ねる → 訪問する → 伺う (most respectful)

Also overlapping: **尋ねる (たずねる)** — to ask/inquire (same pronunciation as 訪ねる, different kanji)
- 道を尋ねる (ask for directions)
- 先生に名前を尋ねる (ask the teacher someone's name)
Takes を for the thing asked about; に for the person asked.

## Speaking Verb Register

Three verbs cover "saying/speaking" at different registers:

**言う (いう)** — to say [casual/neutral, any context]
Short utterances, direct speech, reporting what someone said.
- 何を言うんですか (What are you saying?)
- 彼は「行く」と言った (He said "I'll go")

**話す (はなす)** — to talk/speak [conversational, extended interaction]
Emphasizes the communicative process rather than the output.
- 日本語で話す (speak in Japanese)
- 友達と話す (talk with a friend)
- 先生と話し合う (discuss with the teacher)

**述べる (のべる)** — to state, to express [formal, written or official]
Used in presentations, reports, academic writing, official statements.
- 意見を述べる (state an opinion)
- 感想を述べる (express impressions)
- 報告を述べる (present a report)

**Register test:** Would "I stated my opinion" sound right in casual conversation? No — it sounds stiff. That is 述べる. Use 言う or 話す casually. 述べる in a business email or essay.

## Getting and Receiving Verbs

Three verbs cover "obtaining/receiving" with different nuances:

**受ける (うける)** — to receive something directed at you [passive reception]
Something comes to you; you are the recipient of what was initiated elsewhere.
- サービスを受ける (receive service)
- 治療を受ける (receive treatment)
- 批判を受ける (receive criticism)

**得る (える/うる)** — to achieve, to gain [earned acquisition]
Something you worked toward, earned, or sought out.
- 知識を得る (gain knowledge)
- 許可を得る (obtain permission)
- 信頼を得る (earn trust)

**手に入れる (てにいれる)** — to get one's hands on [active acquisition, concrete]
Effort-and-success framing; most colloquial; often for tangible objects or hard-to-get things.
- チケットを手に入れる (get tickets)
- 情報を手に入れる (get one's hands on information)

## Experience and Reason Pairs (Revisited)

You were introduced to these in Session 01. Now we drill them:

**経験 (けいけん) vs. 体験 (たいけん)**

Both translate as "experience." The distinction:
- 経験 = accumulated experience over time, built through repeated exposure
  - 教師の経験 (teaching experience — years of it)
  - 海外経験 (overseas experience — having lived/worked abroad)
  - 経験が豊富な (experienced, with rich experience)
- 体験 = a specific lived event, often first-time or one-off
  - スカイダイビングを体験する (experience skydiving)
  - 体験談 (personal experience story)
  - 体験入学 (trial class/enrollment — a one-time sample)

**The test:** Is this the result of doing something many times over time (経験) or a specific event you participated in (体験)?

**理由 (りゆう) vs. 原因 (げんいん)**

Both translate as "reason/cause." The distinction:
- 理由 = a stated reason or justification given by a human for a human decision/choice
  - 仕事を辞めた理由 (reason for quitting work)
  - 遅れた理由 (reason for being late)
  - 理由を説明する (explain one's reason)
- 原因 = a physical or causal cause — what made an event happen
  - 事故の原因 (cause of the accident)
  - 病気の原因 (cause of the illness)
  - 原因を調べる (investigate the cause)

**The test:** Is this a human's stated justification (理由) or a causal factor that made an event occur (原因)?

**Exam tip:** 「事故の___は何ですか」→ 原因. 「遅れた___は何ですか」→ 理由.

## Adjective Register Pairs

**すごい vs. 素晴らしい**
- すごい (sugoi) = casual amazement ("Wow, that's incredible!")
- 素晴らしい (subarashii) = formal admiration ("Truly wonderful, impressive")
- Using すごい in a formal speech sounds immature. 素晴らしい in casual conversation sounds stiff.

**美しい vs. 綺麗な**
- 美しい (うつくしい) = aesthetic or moral beauty — art, nature, character
- 綺麗な (きれいな) = attractive, clean, orderly — includes cleanliness
- 綺麗な水 = "clean water" (not necessarily aesthetically beautiful)
- 美しい水 = "beautiful water" (purely aesthetic, suggests visual wonder)

---

## Why This Matters

Register errors are N3-level errors. They reveal not just vocabulary knowledge gaps but cultural-social awareness gaps. JLPT N3 reading passages test register knowledge through vocabulary questions that present four near-synonyms and ask which one fits the given context. Getting these right requires knowing not just what a word means but *when* it belongs.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The visiting verb ladder: 行く → 訪ねる → 訪れる → 訪問する → 伺う (ascending formality/humility)
- Speaking verbs by register: 言う (casual/neutral) → 話す (conversational) → 述べる (formal)
- Getting/receiving: 受ける (receive what comes to you) / 得る (earn/achieve) / 手に入れる (actively acquire)
- 経験 = accumulated experience over time; 体験 = a specific lived event
- 理由 = human's stated justification; 原因 = physical/causal cause of an event
- Adjective register: すごい (casual) vs. 素晴らしい (formal); 綺麗な (clean/attractive) vs. 美しい (aesthetic beauty)
