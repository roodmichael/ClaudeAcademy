# Session 05 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: Inference Drill — Unknown Compounds
**Type:** Analysis

Apply the decoding toolkit from this session to each of the following compounds. You may not know all of these words yet — that is the point. For each:
1. Identify the meaning of each individual kanji (use jisho.org if needed)
2. Make your best inference about the compound meaning
3. Check your inference on jisho.org
4. Note: was your inference correct, partially correct, or off? Why?

Compounds: 再発見、記念、連続、対応、不可能、様子、現実、最高

**What to observe:** Which kanji gave you the strongest inference anchors? Which compounds were misleading (where kanji meanings don't obviously predict the compound meaning)?

## Exercise 2: Jisho.org Compound Exploration
**Type:** Hands-on

Choose ONE of these kanji: 最、現、連、対

Go to jisho.org and search for that kanji. Find 8 vocabulary compounds using it. List them with readings and meanings.

Then: Sort your 8 words into two groups:
- Words I will likely see in N3 reading (everyday topics, news, formal writing)
- Words that seem more specialized or rare at this level

Add the "will likely see at N3" group to your Anki deck as sentence cards.

## Exercise 3: Kanji Triage — Under Time Pressure
**Type:** Thought experiment

You are reading this N3-level paragraph. Set a timer for 90 seconds. Read it and answer the two comprehension questions without looking anything up.

「最近、日本の若者は将来に対して不安を感じる人が増えている。政府は対策として教育支援の拡大を検討しているが、専門家は経済状況の改善が最も重要だと述べている。」

Questions:
1. What is the main concern among young people in Japan?
2. What do experts say is most important?

**After the 90 seconds:** Check any words you weren't sure about. How many unknown kanji/words did you encounter? Could you answer the questions despite them?

**What to observe:** This is a preview of the exam reading experience. Note which unknown words you needed for the questions and which you could safely ignore.

## Exercise 4: The Weekly Kanji Exploration Habit — Trial Run
**Type:** Hands-on

Find one kanji you encountered this week in any Japanese you encountered (Anki, reading, exercise sentences — anything). If you haven't encountered any new kanji this week, choose: 変

Perform a full jisho.org compound exploration:
1. Find the radical
2. Find the JLPT level
3. List all compounds at N3 or below
4. Find the most common compound and note its collocation pattern (what verb, particle, or noun does it typically appear with?)

**What to observe:** This is the habit the lesson recommends doing once a week indefinitely. Try it now so you have a workflow established before the reading sessions.
