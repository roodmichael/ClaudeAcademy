**Course:** JAPN-102

## Opening

Here is a real N3 reading challenge: you are reading an article and encounter 再発見. You don't recognize the compound. But you know 再 (again, re-), 発 (emit, arise), and 見 (see). "Re-arise-see." A reasonable guess: "rediscovery." That is exactly what it means.

This session is about developing that inference reflex. You have the method from Session 04 (radicals and mnemonics). Now you practice applying it in the kind of reading environments where you will actually encounter N3 kanji.

---

## The Decoding Toolkit

When you encounter an unknown kanji compound in reading, you have four tools:

**Tool 1: Radical inference**
Break the compound into individual kanji. Identify the radical of each. Use the radical meanings to make an educated guess at the compound meaning.

再 (again) + 発 (arise/emit) + 見 (see) = 再発見 → "see arise again" = rediscovery
記 (record) + 念 (thought/feeling) = 記念 → "recorded thought" = commemoration/memory
連 (connect) + 絡 (connect) = 連絡 → "connect + connect" = contact, communication

**Tool 2: One-kanji anchor**
If you know one kanji in a compound confidently, use it to narrow down the meaning. If you see ___経___ and you know 経 relates to passage/management, you are probably in the economy, experience, or route semantic field.

**Tool 3: Context inference**
Even without decoding the compound, the surrounding text constrains its meaning. If a passage is about environmental policy and you encounter an unknown compound, it is almost certainly related to government, environment, or policy — not cooking or sports.

**Tool 4: Part-of-speech cue**
If the unknown compound is followed by する → it is a verb. If followed by な → it is a na-adjective. If followed by の → it is a noun modifier. Knowing part of speech before you know meaning is already useful.

## High-Frequency N3 Kanji in Compounds: A Worked Set

Let's work through 12 high-frequency N3 kanji using these tools. For each, we examine multiple compounds:

**連 (REN, つ・れる — connect, join)**
- 連絡 (れんらく) — contact, communication
- 連続 (れんぞく) — continuation, series
- 関連 (かんれん) — connection, relevance
- 連日 (れんじつ) — day after day, consecutive days

**最 (SAI, もっと・も — utmost, most)**
- 最近 (さいきん) — recently
- 最初 (さいしょ) — the first, the beginning
- 最後 (さいご) — the last, the end
- 最高 (さいこう) — the highest, the best
Note: 最 is a superlative prefix. Any compound with 最 first is describing the extreme of whatever follows.

**現 (GEN, あらわ・れる — present, actual, appear)**
- 現在 (げんざい) — the present, currently
- 現実 (げんじつ) — reality
- 現代 (げんだい) — modern era
- 表現 (ひょうげん) — expression, representation
- 実現 (じつげん) — realization, actualization

**対 (TAI/TSUI — compare, versus, against)**
- 対応 (たいおう) — response, correspondence
- 対策 (たいさく) — countermeasure
- 反対 (はんたい) — opposition, reverse
- 対象 (たいしょう) — subject, target (of an action)

**可 (KA — possible, can)**
- 可能 (かのう) — possible
- 可能性 (かのうせい) — possibility
- 不可能 (ふかのう) — impossible
Note: 可 is a prefix meaning "possible/permissible." When you see 不可, you can immediately infer negation of possibility.

**様 (YOU, さま — appearance, manner, formal suffix)**
- 様子 (ようす) — appearance, condition, manner
- 同様 (どうよう) — similar, in the same way
- 様々な (さまざまな) — various, diverse
Note: 〜様 as a polite suffix on names (田中様) is a separate but related use.

## Reading N3 Kanji Under Time Pressure

Session 10 covers reading strategy in detail, but one point matters now: N3 reading is a time-limited activity. The JLPT reading section gives you roughly 3 minutes per passage. You cannot stop to decode every unknown kanji carefully.

The trained reading behavior for unknown kanji is:
1. Apply the radical/context inference toolkit (above) — spend 3–5 seconds maximum
2. If the guess is good enough to continue, continue
3. Mark the word and return if a question directly targets it
4. If the unknown word is clearly pivotal (it is what the main idea turns on), invest 10 more seconds

This is not the same as ignoring unknown words — it is triage. Your recognition of the 80% of known kanji must be fast enough to carry the passage. For that, recognition speed matters as much as recognition accuracy.

## Jisho.org as a Kanji Exploration Tool

Between sessions, jisho.org is your most powerful independent kanji study tool. Here is how to use it beyond basic lookup:

**Explore a kanji's compounds:** Enter just the kanji (e.g., 経) and click through to the kanji entry. You see: all readings, all tagged JLPT levels, the radical, stroke order animation, and a list of vocabulary using this kanji sorted by JLPT level.

**Filter by JLPT level:** When searching vocabulary, jisho.org tags words with N5 through N1. Search 「経」and you can quickly find which compounds are N3 and which are N2 — focus on N3 and below for now.

**Audio:** Many jisho.org entries include audio pronunciation. For vocabulary you cannot hear in your head clearly, play the audio once.

**The exploration habit:** Once a week, take one kanji you encountered in reading that week. Do a jisho.org compound exploration — find 5 words using that kanji. Add the N3-tagged ones to your Anki deck.

---

## Why This Matters

N3 is the first level where kanji recognition is a genuine reading bottleneck. A student who must sound out or skip kanji compounds is reading below their actual grammar and vocabulary knowledge, because the character layer is slowing them down. The goal of Sessions 04 and 05 together is to make kanji recognition fast enough that it stops being the constraint on reading — so that grammar and vocabulary understanding can shine through.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The decoding toolkit (radical inference, one-kanji anchor, context inference, part-of-speech cue) lets you navigate unknown N3 compounds in real reading without a dictionary.
- High-frequency N3 kanji like 連, 最, 現, 対, 可, and 様 appear in many compounds — knowing them unlocks clusters of vocabulary.
- Reading speed, not just accuracy, is the goal at N3. Triage unknown kanji rather than stopping for each one.
- Jisho.org compound exploration is the best between-session habit for independent kanji expansion.
