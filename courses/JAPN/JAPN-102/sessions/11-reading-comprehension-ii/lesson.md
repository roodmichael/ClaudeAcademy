## Opening

Everything in this course has been building toward this: picking up an actual Japanese text — one written for native readers, not constructed for language learners — and making sense of it.

NHK Web Easy (nhk.or.jp/news/easy) is a free, daily-updated resource where NHK journalists rewrite real news stories in simplified Japanese. Furigana over every kanji, shorter sentences, more common vocabulary — but authentic structure, authentic discourse patterns, authentic topics. It is the ideal bridge between textbook Japanese and the full-speed NHK articles.

This session is a workshop. We work through the reading skills from Session 10 on real material, and we add the last two skills you need: handling unknown words under time pressure, and the weekly review habit that makes this sustainable.

## The NHK Web Easy Workflow

When you open an NHK Web Easy article, here is a structured workflow that builds all the skills at once:

**Step 1 — First pass (no dictionary, furigana on)**
Read the whole article once, understanding what you can. Do not stop at unknown words. Estimate their meaning from context if possible. Note (on paper or mentally) any words that stopped you.

**Step 2 — Apply skim-scan-dive if returning for comprehension questions**
If you are using the article for exam practice: read the questions first, then skim, then dive.

**Step 3 — Second pass (dictionary permitted)**
Go back and look up the words you noted. Add new vocabulary to your Anki deck as sentence cards.

**Step 4 — Compare to the original article (optional stretch)**
NHK Web Easy links to the original full-speed NHK article. Try reading the first paragraph of the original. Note what additional vocabulary or kanji appear. This is N2-level material — do not worry if it's hard. Just notice the gap.

**Time investment:** 20–30 minutes per article, 2–3 articles per week. This is the practice that builds N3 reading fluency over time.

## Handling Unknown Words: The Inference Protocol

At N3, you will encounter unknown words in every authentic text. This is not a failure — it is normal. The trained N3 reader does not freeze; they triage.

**Protocol for unknown words during timed reading:**

1. **Radical inference (3–5 seconds):** Break the compound into kanji. Apply what you know from Sessions 04–06. 経過 → 経 (passage) + 過 (excess/pass) → "passage of [excess] time" → sounds like the passage of time. Close enough to continue.

2. **Context inference (3–5 seconds):** Read the surrounding sentences. If the passage is about environmental policy and the unknown word appears in a sentence about government action, it probably describes a government measure or result.

3. **Part-of-speech cue (1–2 seconds):** Followed by する → verb. Followed by な → adjective. Followed by に → adverb or particle. Knowing what grammatical role the word plays helps even before knowing its meaning.

4. **Tolerance decision:** Is this word:
   - Directly targeted by a question? → Invest more time, look it up if you must
   - Peripheral to the main point? → Note it, continue, look up after

**The 3-minute rule:** Maximum 3 minutes per passage. If you hit 3 minutes, mark where you are, move to the next passage, and return with remaining time. An incomplete passage answered partially is better than a missed passage answered not at all.

## Common Reading Errors — And How to Avoid Them

**Error 1: Reading linearly under time pressure**
The student reads every word from start to finish before looking at questions. Result: out of time on medium and long passages.
Fix: Questions first, always.

**Error 2: Freezing on unknown words**
The student spends 45–60 seconds trying to decode a single kanji compound. Result: the rest of the passage is unread.
Fix: Apply the inference protocol (max 10 seconds) and move on.

**Error 3: Choosing answers that "sound right"**
The student selects an answer based on world knowledge or partial reading, not passage verification.
Fix: Every answer choice must be verifiable with a specific line in the passage. If you cannot find the line, the answer is probably wrong.

**Error 4: Missing referential expressions**
The student reads for content but ignores これ, それ, この事, etc. Questions about referential expressions are N3 exam staples.
Fix: Every time you encounter これ/それ/その+noun in a passage, briefly ask: "What does this refer to?" Make it a reading habit, not just an exam habit.

**Error 5: Reading NHK Web Easy with look-up enabled and calling it reading practice**
Looking up every unknown word builds vocabulary but not reading fluency. The skills are different.
Fix: First pass with no dictionary (fluency practice). Second pass with dictionary (vocabulary building). Keep them separate.

## The Independent Reading Habit: Sustaining Progress

After this course, reading fluency develops through volume. The research is clear: vocabulary acquired through authentic reading is retained longer and integrated better than vocabulary from lists alone.

**The sustainable weekly habit:**
- 2–3 NHK Web Easy articles per week (20–30 minutes each)
- 10–15 minutes of daily Anki review
- 1 weekly kanji exploration (jisho.org compound dive — from Session 05)

This is approximately 1.5–2 hours of Japanese study per week, structured for retention rather than coverage. More is fine; less slows acquisition significantly.

**When to graduate from NHK Web Easy:**
When you can read an NHK Web Easy article in under 5 minutes with no dictionary and answer comprehension questions correctly, you are ready for the original NHK articles (N2 level) and for Shin Kanzen Master N3 Reading for exam-specific practice.

## Why This Matters

Reading comprehension is the skill that makes Japanese real. Grammar lets you construct sentences. Vocabulary gives you content. Kanji lets you decode characters. But reading is where all three come together in service of understanding actual human communication. NHK Web Easy is real — written by journalists, describing real events, for real readers. The moment you can read it fluently, Japanese has crossed from "something you study" to "something you use."

## Key Takeaways

- The NHK Web Easy workflow: first pass (no dictionary, fluency), second pass (dictionary, vocabulary building), optional original article comparison.
- The inference protocol for unknown words: radical → context → part-of-speech → tolerance decision. Max 10 seconds per word during timed reading.
- Five common reading errors: linear reading, freezing on unknowns, unverified answer choices, ignoring referential expressions, using look-up during fluency practice.
- The sustainable weekly habit: 2–3 articles + daily Anki + weekly kanji exploration = approximately 1.5 hours/week for continued N3 acquisition.
