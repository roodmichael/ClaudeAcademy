# Session 04: Kanji Expansion — The 370 New Characters
**Course:** JAPN-102
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to apply the radical-mnemonic learning method to approach new N3 kanji systematically, and explain how knowing key N3 kanji unlocks multiple vocabulary words through jukugo compounding.

---

## Opening

You know about 300 kanji. There are about 370 more between you and N3. That sounds like an obstacle — but here is the thing: those 370 kanji are not 370 isolated symbols to memorize one at a time. They are building blocks. Once you know 経 and 済, you can read 経済 (economy), 経験 (experience), 経路 (route), and 決済 (payment settlement) — four N3 vocabulary words, one investment.

The question in this session is not "how do I memorize 370 characters?" It is "how do I build a system for learning characters so they stay learned?"

---

## The Numbers

At N5: ~80 kanji. At N4: ~170 kanji (cumulative ~250). At N3: ~370 new kanji (cumulative ~650). This is where character study starts to feel like it has mass to it.

There is no single officially published JLPT kanji list. The commonly referenced community-derived lists (JLPTSensei, Jonathan Waller's JLPT Resources) show some variation, but converge on roughly the same ~370 characters. This course uses JLPTSensei's N3 list as its reference standard — it is the most widely available and cross-checked.

## Radicals: The Building Blocks of Kanji

A **radical (部首, bushu)** is a smaller stroke pattern that appears as a component of many kanji. Japanese dictionaries are organized by radical. But beyond dictionary use, radicals serve as mnemonic anchors — if you know a radical's name and visual meaning, you can construct a story that connects a new kanji's appearance to its meaning.

There are 214 official radicals. For JLPT study, about 50 are high-frequency and worth learning by name. Here are the most useful for N3:

| Radical | Shape | Meaning hint | Example kanji |
|---------|-------|-------------|---------------|
| 言 (ごんべん) | 言 on left | speech, language | 語、話、読、説 |
| 手/扌(てへん) | 扌on left | hand, manual action | 持、打、拾、押 |
| 水/氵(さんずい) | 氵on left | water, liquid | 泳、海、洗、湖 |
| 木 (きへん) | 木 on left | tree, wood | 材、板、植、棚 |
| 日 (にちへん) | 日 | sun, day, time | 明、時、晴、昨 |
| 人/亻(にんべん) | 亻on left | person | 作、休、使、住 |
| 田 (たへん) | 田 | rice paddy, field | 男、町、界 |
| 心/忄(こころ) | 心 or 忄 | heart, feelings | 感、思、怖、悩 |
| 口 (くちへん) | 口 | mouth, speech, opening | 問、命、品 |
| 土 (つちへん) | 土 | earth, ground | 地、場、基 |

**Practical approach:** When you encounter a new kanji, identify its dominant radical. Ask: what story connects this radical's meaning to the kanji's meaning? The story doesn't need to be logical — it needs to be memorable.

## The Mnemonic Method in Practice

Let's build mnemonic stories for five representative N3 kanji:

**政 (SEI, まつりごと — politics, government)**
Radical: 攵 (strike/action). Components: 正 (correct/upright) + 攵 (action). Story: "Correct actions [by leaders] are what make government."
Compounds: 政府 (government), 政治 (politics), 政策 (policy)

**議 (GI — deliberation, debate)**
Radical: 言 (speech). Components: 言 (speech) + 義 (righteousness). Story: "Righteous speech — that's deliberation."
Compounds: 議会 (parliament/assembly), 会議 (meeting), 協議 (deliberation)

**経 (KEI, へる — pass through, passage of time, manage)**
Radical: 糸 (thread/silk). Components: 糸 (thread) + 巠 (loom). Story: "Thread passing through a loom — the passage of process."
Compounds: 経済 (economy), 経験 (experience), 経路 (route), 経過 (passage of time)

**決 (KETSU, き(める) — decide, settle)**
Radical: 氵 (water). Components: 氵 (water) + 夬 (snap/break). Story: "Water breaking through — a decisive moment."
Compounds: 決める (decide), 決定 (decision), 決済 (payment/settlement), 解決 (resolution)

**現 (GEN, あらわ(れる) — present, actual, appear)**
Radical: 王/玉 (jewel/king). Components: 王 (king) + 見 (see). Story: "What the king sees is what is real and present."
Compounds: 現在 (the present/currently), 現実 (reality), 現代 (modern era), 表現 (expression)

## Jukugo: How Kanji Unlock Vocabulary in Clusters

The payoff of learning kanji systematically is the **combinatorial effect**. Once you know 経 and 済:

- 経済 (けいざい) — economy (pass through + settle/complete)
- 経験 (けいけん) — experience (pass through + test/verify)
- 経路 (けいろ) — route (pass through + road)
- 経過 (けいか) — passage of time (pass through + exceed/pass)
- 決済 (けっさい) — payment settlement (decide + settle)

Five words, two kanji. This is the return on investment of kanji study at N3.

Once you know 政 and 治:
- 政治 (せいじ) — politics
- 行政 (ぎょうせい) — administration
- 政府 (せいふ) — government
- 自治 (じち) — self-governance

Compare this to learning 政治, 行政, 政府, and 自治 as four separate unrelated vocabulary items. The second approach requires four times the memorization for the same reading proficiency.

## N3 Kanji by Thematic Cluster

The ~370 N3 kanji are not randomly distributed. They cluster around the same thematic domains as the vocabulary in Sessions 02–03. Here are key clusters:

**Governance and civic life (政, 議, 権, 民, 府)**
- 政策 (policy), 議会 (parliament), 権利 (rights), 国民 (citizens), 政府 (government)

**Economic and business (経, 済, 利, 益, 費)**
- 経済 (economy), 決済 (settlement), 利益 (profit), 費用 (cost)

**Change and causation (変, 化, 因, 果, 改)**
- 変化 (change), 原因 (cause), 結果 (result), 改善 (improvement)

**Emotional and relational (感, 信, 望, 悩, 頼)**
- 感情 (emotion), 信頼 (trust), 希望 (hope), 悩み (worry), 頼む (ask/rely)

**Scientific and technological (技, 術, 機, 能, 開)**
- 技術 (technology), 機能 (function), 可能性 (possibility), 開発 (development)

Learning kanji in these thematic clusters means you learn the kanji and the vocabulary simultaneously — each kanji appears in multiple words you encounter in the same session.

## Distinguishing Similar N3 Kanji

N3 introduces characters that are visually close to N4 characters you already know. Three common confusion groups:

**待/持/時** — all three share a similar structure:
- 待つ (まつ, to wait) — radical 彳 (walk/step), meaning "stand still and let time walk past"
- 持つ (もつ, to hold) — radical 扌 (hand), meaning "hold with the hand"
- 時 (とき, time) — radical 日 (sun), meaning "the sun's position marks time"

The radical is the disambiguation key every time.

**見/貝** — almost identical at a glance:
- 見 (see) — bottom component is ル (legs), running to see
- 貝 (shellfish) — bottom component is 目 (eye) alone, no legs

**己/已/巳** — one of the classic confusion triples:
- 己 (self) — open at the top
- 已 (already/stop) — open in the middle
- 巳 (snake/zodiac) — closed at the top

## Why This Matters

At N4, you could get away with learning vocabulary in hiragana and treating kanji as optional extras. At N3, this strategy collapses. News articles, JLPT reading passages, business documents — they all use kanji compounds with no furigana help. If you cannot recognize N3 kanji automatically, you cannot read at N3 speed.

The mnemonic-radical method is not the only way to learn kanji. But it is the method best suited to adult learners who need to scale quickly to ~650 characters without rote repetition of stroke order for all of them.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The ~370 N3 kanji are best approached through radicals (部首) and mnemonic stories, not rote memorization of all readings.
- Knowing one kanji's meaning and one reading unlocks multiple jukugo vocabulary words through compounding.
- N3 kanji cluster by thematic domain — learning them in clusters reinforces the vocabulary from Sessions 02–03.
- Use the radical to distinguish visually similar kanji (待/持/時, 見/貝, 己/已/巳).
- Learn one reading per kanji first (usually the on'yomi, since jukugo compounds dominate). Acquire secondary readings through vocabulary study.
