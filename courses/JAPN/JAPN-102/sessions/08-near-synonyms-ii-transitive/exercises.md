## Exercise 1: Pair Identification — Agency Choice
**Type:** Analysis

For each situation below, decide which verb from the pair is correct. Write the complete sentence with the correct particle.

**Pair: 開ける / 開く**
1. Situation: You push open a door. Sentence: ___ が/を 開け/開きました。
2. Situation: The store's sliding door opens automatically as you approach.

**Pair: 変える / 変わる**
3. Situation: The schedule has changed (you don't know who changed it).
4. Situation: The manager decided to change the meeting time.

**Pair: 決める / 決まる**
5. Situation: A policy was decided (passive framing, agent unimportant).
6. Situation: You have not yet made your decision about which university to attend.

**Pair: 消す / 消える**
7. Situation: You turn off the classroom lights.
8. Situation: The candle went out in the wind.

## Exercise 2: Error Detection and Correction
**Type:** Analysis

Each sentence contains an error in transitivity, particle, or both. Identify and fix each one. Explain the error in one sentence.

1. 電気が消してください。(Please turn off the lights.)
2. 子どもを育ちました。(I raised a child.)
3. 財布を落ちました。(I dropped my wallet.)
4. 電車がまだ止めていません。(The train hasn't stopped yet.)
5. 会社の方針を変わりました。(The company's policy changed.)

## Exercise 3: The Agency Distinction — Reading Comprehension
**Type:** Analysis

Read each pair of sentences. Explain the different meaning or implication of each sentence in the pair.

**Pair 1:**
A. 都市の人口が減った。
B. 政府が都市の人口を減らした。

**Pair 2:**
A. プロジェクトが終わった。
B. チームがプロジェクトを終えた。

**Pair 3:**
A. ドアが閉まっている。
B. 誰かがドアを閉めた。

**What to observe:** How does the transitive/intransitive choice change the implied cause and the implied responsibility? This is a reading comprehension skill, not just a vocabulary skill.

## Exercise 4: Pattern Testing
**Type:** Thought experiment

The lesson warns against using morphological patterns (ending in -eru = transitive, etc.) to predict transitivity.

**Task:** Test the patterns yourself with these 6 verbs. For each, note what the pattern predicts and whether the pattern is correct.

Verbs: 育てる、育つ、落とす、落ちる、止める、止まる

Pattern hypothesis A: "Verbs ending in -eru are transitive"
Pattern hypothesis B: "Verbs ending in -su are transitive"

How many times do each hypothesis correctly predict the transitivity? How many times do they fail?

**What to observe:** What is the failure rate? Does this confirm the lesson's warning? What does this tell you about how to study these pairs?
