# Session 08: Near-Synonyms II — Transitive and Intransitive Verb Pairs
**Course:** JAPN-102
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to correctly identify and use the 10 key transitive/intransitive N3 verb pairs, choosing the correct verb and particle (を vs. が) for each context.

---

## Opening

Here is a verb you probably know: 開ける (あける). It means "to open." But here is another verb: 開く (あく). It also means "to open." So which one do you use when the door opens by itself, and which one do you use when you open the door?

This is the transitive/intransitive puzzle. It is one of the most systematic near-synonym categories in Japanese, and it trips up intermediate learners constantly — not because the concept is hard, but because the pattern-guessing instinct leads students in the wrong direction.

---

## Transitive vs. Intransitive: The Basic Distinction

**Transitive verbs** take a direct object — something you act upon. They use **を**.
→ ドアを開ける (I open the door — I am the agent acting on the door)

**Intransitive verbs** describe things happening on their own — no external agent. They use **が**.
→ ドアが開く (The door opens — it opens by itself, or its own accord)

This might sound like a small distinction, but it carries real meaning. Japanese is highly sensitive to agency and causality. If the door opened because you pushed it: を + transitive. If the door slid open automatically: が + intransitive. If you leave an automatic door out of the picture and report only the event: が + intransitive.

---

## The 10 Key Transitive/Intransitive Pairs at N3

Learn these as **paired sets**, not as individual vocabulary items:

| Transitive (agent acts, takes を) | Intransitive (event happens, takes が) |
|----------------------------------|---------------------------------------|
| 開ける (あける) — to open [it] | 開く (あく) — to open [itself] |
| 閉める (しめる) — to close [it] | 閉まる (しまる) — to close [itself] |
| 入れる (いれる) — to put in | 入る (はいる) — to enter/go in |
| 出す (だす) — to take out, send | 出る (でる) — to come out, leave |
| 落とす (おとす) — to drop [it] | 落ちる (おちる) — to fall |
| 変える (かえる) — to change [it] | 変わる (かわる) — to change [itself] |
| 決める (きめる) — to decide | 決まる (きまる) — to be decided |
| 止める (とめる) — to stop [it] | 止まる (とまる) — to stop [itself] |
| 育てる (そだてる) — to raise/rear | 育つ (そだつ) — to grow up |
| 消す (けす) — to turn off/erase | 消える (きえる) — to disappear/go out |

---

## Pattern Analysis: Why Morphological Guessing Fails

You might notice patterns in the verb pairs: -える vs. -う, or -す vs. -る. Some learners try to use these patterns as rules:

- "If the verb ends in -eru, it's transitive; -u means intransitive"
- "If the verb ends in -su, it's transitive"

**Do not rely on these patterns.** Research shows they are correct only about 20% of the time. The actual morphological patterns are inconsistent:

- 開ける (-eru) is transitive ✓, but 開く (-ku) is intransitive ✓ — this pair works
- 入れる (-eru) is transitive ✓, but 入る (-ru) is intransitive ✓ — also works
- 落とす (-su) is transitive ✓, but 出す (-su) is also transitive ✓ — pattern fails for intransitive
- 消す (-su) is transitive, 消える (-eru) is intransitive — wait, -eru here is INtransitive

The pattern is messy. Learn each pair explicitly.

---

## Using the Pairs in Context

Each pair creates a set of sentences that show agency:

**変える / 変わる:**
- 会社が方針を変えた。(The company changed its policy — agent: 会社、object: 方針)
- 方針が変わった。(The policy changed. — event, no agent stated)
- 世界が変わった。(The world changed.)
- 私が状況を変えます。(I will change the situation.)

**決める / 決まる:**
- 委員会が日程を決めた。(The committee decided the schedule — agent: 委員会)
- 日程が決まった。(The schedule was decided / has been decided — no agent stated)
- まだ決まっていません。(It hasn't been decided yet.)
- 自分で決めてください。(Please decide for yourself.)

**消す / 消える:**
- 電気を消してください。(Please turn off the lights — you act on the lights)
- 電気が消えた。(The lights went out — they disappeared, no agent)
- ファイルを消した。(I deleted the file.)
- データが消えてしまった。(The data disappeared. — てしまう for unexpected/unfortunate completion)

---

## The Agency Clue in Context

When reading N3 passages, the transitive/intransitive choice tells you whether an agent caused something or something happened on its own. This matters for comprehension:

「会社の利益が大幅に減った」— profits fell (intransitive — reported as an event)
「会社が費用を大幅に減らした」— the company cut costs (transitive — the company was the agent)

These sentences have different implications for cause and responsibility. Japanese uses the transitive/intransitive distinction to carry that meaning.

---

## Connections Back to JAPN-101

You studied passive voice in JAPN-101. Here is the connection: **only transitive verbs can be passivized in the standard passive form**. 

- ドアを開ける (transitive) → ドアが開けられた (passive: the door was opened)
- ドアが開く (intransitive) → *ドアが開かれた is ungrammatical in the standard passive sense

The intransitive form already expresses "the door opened" without an agent — you do not need a passive to achieve that meaning. This is why understanding transitive/intransitive helps you understand when and why passive voice is used.

---

## Why This Matters

JLPT N3 vocabulary questions frequently test transitive/intransitive pairs with a sentence blank. JLPT reading passages use them constantly — especially the change verbs (変える/変わる, 決める/決まる) in news reporting. And conversational Japanese uses them constantly: 止まれ！ (Stop! — intransitive command to the thing), 止めて！(Stop it! — transitive command to a person).

---

## Key Takeaways

- Transitive verbs have an agent acting on an object (を). Intransitive verbs describe an event happening on its own (が).
- Learn the 10 key N3 pairs as pairs, not as individual vocabulary items.
- Do not rely on morphological patterns to predict transitivity — they fail ~80% of the time.
- The transitive/intransitive distinction is not just vocabulary — it encodes agency and causality in Japanese, affecting the meaning of sentences in reading comprehension.
- Only transitive verbs can be passivized in the standard passive form (connection to JAPN-101).
