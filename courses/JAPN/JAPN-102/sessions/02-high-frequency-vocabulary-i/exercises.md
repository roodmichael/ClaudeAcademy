Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: Sentence Card Construction — Social Vocabulary
**Type:** Hands-on

Choose 8 words from this session's vocabulary list. For each word, write an original Japanese sentence using it correctly. Rules:
- All other words in your sentence should be words you already know
- Include the correct collocation partner (e.g., 信頼を**得る**, not 信頼を*持つ*)
- Write the reading for any kanji you aren't 100% sure of

When you're done, add these 8 words as sentence cards in Anki (Japanese sentence on front, meaning of target word on back).

**What to observe:** Which words were easy to put in a sentence and which felt awkward? The awkward ones are where you need more example sentences — look up 3 more on Jisho.org.

## Exercise 2: Collocation Fill-In
**Type:** Analysis

Fill in the blank with the most natural verb from the list below. Each verb is used once.

Verbs: 感じる / 得る / 受ける / かける / 乗る / 述べる

1. 批判を________ → to receive criticism
2. 不安を________ → to feel anxious
3. 意見を________ → to state an opinion (formal)
4. 相談に________ → to listen to someone's problem
5. 心配を________ → to cause someone worry
6. 信頼を________ → to earn trust

**What to observe:** Do any of the natural verb choices surprise you? (かける in 心配をかける is idiomatic — "put worry on someone.") Note which collocations feel non-obvious.

## Exercise 4: Thematic Word Association
**Type:** Thought experiment

Look at this cluster of emotional/social words from the session:
不安、悩む、落ち込む、心配する

These all relate to negative emotional states, but they're not the same thing. Write a short scenario (2–3 sentences) in which a person experiences all four in sequence — something causes 不安, which leads to 悩む, then something happens and they 落ち込む, and someone else 心配する about them.

You can write in English if your Japanese isn't ready for it, but try to include the Japanese words in your English sentences (e.g., "She began to feel 不安 about...").

**What to observe:** Trying to use words in sequence reveals whether you understand their differences. 不安 is more of a background state; 悩む is active agonizing; 落ち込む is the aftermath of something going wrong. Did your scenario capture these distinctions?
