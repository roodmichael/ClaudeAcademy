# JAPN-102: Japanese Language — N4 to N3 Vocabulary and Kanji

## Course Description

JAPN-101 gave you the grammar scaffolding. This course fills in the walls. JAPN-102 builds the vocabulary and kanji knowledge that N3 demands — roughly 2,000 new words, 370 new characters, and the reading strategies you need to make sense of authentic Japanese text under real time pressure. This is not the course where you learn rules. This is the course where Japanese starts to feel like something you actually read, not decode.

## Prerequisites

JAPN-101 (Japanese Language — N3 Grammar Patterns), or equivalent JLPT N4-level grammar competency.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

- Recognize and read approximately 370 new N3 kanji in compound and standalone contexts
- Apply the four on'yomi/kun'yomi prediction rules to decode unknown compound words at reading speed
- Use radicals as mnemonic anchors to learn new kanji and distinguish visually similar characters
- Demonstrate vocabulary knowledge of approximately 2,000 new N3 words across 18 thematic categories
- Discriminate between near-synonym pairs — choosing the correct verb, noun, or adjective and the correct particle for the context
- Read and comprehend N3-level authentic texts using the skim-scan-dive strategy within a 3-minute-per-passage budget
- Trace the logical structure of an N3 reading passage using discourse connectives

## Required Materials

- **Anki** (free spaced repetition app) — download at apps.ankiweb.net
- **Jisho.org** — free online Japanese dictionary, used in nearly every session
- **NHK News Web Easy** — nhk.or.jp/news/easy — free daily N3-appropriate reading practice
- **Recommended (optional):** Shin Kanzen Master N3 読解 — the best exam-prep reading workbook for N3. Not required; NHK Web Easy substitutes for the reading practice.
- **Recommended (optional):** A notebook for kanji grid practice. JLPT does not test handwriting, but writing kanji by hand 5 times accelerates recognition.

## Assessment Plan

- **Session quizzes:** After each session, 4–6 questions. Low stakes — they reveal what needs reinforcement, not whether you pass.
- **Midterm:** After Session 06. A two-part vocabulary and kanji applied challenge: you will decode a set of compound words using the on'yomi/kun'yomi system and demonstrate vocabulary knowledge by writing original sentences for N3 words in context.
- **Final project:** After Session 11. A reading comprehension portfolio — five authentic NHK Web Easy articles annotated with your discourse analysis, unknown word strategies, and self-assessment. You will also write a 300-word personal reflection in Japanese on one of the articles.

## A Note on Pacing

Each session in this course builds on the last, but the payoff is cumulative. In the early sessions, words and kanji may feel isolated. By Session 07, you will notice that your kanji knowledge, your on'yomi/kun'yomi prediction skills, and your vocabulary are all reinforcing each other. That moment is worth working toward.

Spend 30–45 minutes on each lesson, then 20–30 minutes on the exercises before taking the quiz. If something is not sticking, the exercise is more valuable than the quiz — return to it. Use Jisho.org freely during exercises; use it sparingly during timed reading practice. Between sessions, 10–15 minutes of daily Anki review compounds faster than a three-hour weekly cram session. The research is unambiguous on this.
