# Glossary — JAPN-103: Japanese Language — N4 to N3 Conversation and Natural Speech

All key terms are listed alphabetically. Terms are defined as used in this course.

---

## A

**aizuchi (相槌):** Back-channel responses produced by the listener during the speaker's turn to signal active listening and engagement. Examples include うん, なるほど, へえ, そうですか. Frequency of aizuchi is higher in Japanese conversation than in many Western conversational styles; absence signals disengagement.

**atamadaka (頭高型):** A pitch accent pattern in Tokyo/standard Japanese where the first mora is high and the pitch drops immediately after, remaining low. Example: 雨 (ame, rain) is H-L.

---

## B

**バイト敬語 (baito keigo):** "Part-time worker keigo" — a non-standard honorific register developed in service contexts, characterized by forms like ～になります for simple statements of fact. Disputed by prescriptivists but widely used.

**美化語 (bikago):** "Beautification language" — the use of お or ご prefixes to make speech more elegant and refined, without encoding social hierarchy. Distinct from sonkeigo and kenjōgo. Examples: お酒, ご飯, お料理.

**母音の無声化 (boin no mushōka):** Vowel devoicing — the vowels い (i) and う (u) become whispered or nearly inaudible when appearing between two voiceless consonants, or at word end after a voiceless consonant. Example: です sounds like "des."

---

## C

**縮約形 (shukuyaku-kei):** Contracted forms — shortened casual speech forms generated primarily by dropping the mora い from common verb endings. The umbrella term for contractions like てる, ちゃう, とく.

---

## D

**だろう / でしょう:** Expressions of reasoned probability — the speaker has a basis for their opinion but is not certain. だろう is casual; でしょう is formal. Used to express "probably" with a reasoning basis.

---

## E

**証拠性 (shōkosei):** Evidentiality — grammatical marking of the source of information (direct observation, hearsay, inference, reasoning). Japanese encodes evidentiality grammatically through expressions like らしい, ようだ, はずだ, and そうだ.

---

## H

**はずだ (hazu da):** A high-certainty evidential expression meaning "should be / supposed to be / must be by logic." Requires an objective factual basis for the inference. Frustrated はず: はずだったのに expresses that a logical expectation was violated.

**heiban (平板型):** A flat pitch accent pattern — the pitch rises after the first mora and stays high, never dropping. Approximately 80% of Japanese vocabulary is heiban.

**ヘッジング (hejjingu):** Hedging — the use of linguistic devices to soften assertions, signal uncertainty, or protect the speaker from being held fully accountable for a claim.

**拝見する (haiken suru):** The kenjōgo (humble) form of 見る (to see/look). Used by the speaker to humbly refer to their own act of seeing or reading something. Example: こちらを拝見します (I will look at this).

---

## I

**いただく (itadaku):** A kenjōgo (humble) verb with three distinct uses: (1) humble form of 食べる/飲む (to eat/drink); (2) humble form of もらう (to receive); (3) used in the structure ～ていただく for humble requests of another's action.

**いらっしゃる (irassharu):** The sonkeigo (honorific) form for three verbs: いる, 行く, and 来る. Context determines which meaning is active.

---

## J

**じゃあ (jā):** A versatile transition marker meaning "well then / OK then / so." Signals conclusions, transitions to action, topic changes, and conversation closings. The slightly more formal equivalent is では.

---

## K

**か (ka):** Question particle. In polite speech, used with falling intonation for yes/no questions. In casual speech, the particle is often dropped and rising intonation alone marks the question.

**かな (kana):** A gentle wondering particle meaning "I wonder if…" — the lowest-certainty position in the spectrum. Gender-neutral; universally safe.

**感情 (kanjō):** Emotion. Used in the course in discussing how Japanese encodes emotional stance grammatically through particles, evidential expressions, and prosody.

**かもしれない (kamoshirenai):** The lowest-certainty inferential expression — pure possibility with no evidence required. Casual colloquial form: かも. "It might be / maybe."

**間投詞 (kantoushi):** Interjections — standalone sounds or words that signal listening, reaction, or thinking state. Includes えーと, あの, うん, へえ.

**気がする (ki ga suru):** "I have a feeling / it seems to me (intuitively)" — based on gut feeling or intuition, no external evidence.

**敬語 (keigo):** The collective term for all forms of Japanese honorific language — including teineigo, sonkeigo, and kenjōgo.

**謙譲語 (kenjōgo):** Humble language that lowers the speaker's own actions (or the speaker's in-group) to show deference. Uses special vocabulary (参る, いただく, etc.) or the productive pattern お〜いたす.

**ちょっと (chotto):** Literally "a little, a bit." Functions as: (1) softener of requests; (2) qualifier of statements; (3) indirect refusal signal when followed by pause or vague difficulty expression.

**ちなみに (chinami ni):** "By the way / incidentally / for reference" — adds related supplementary information. Neutral-to-slightly-formal register.

---

## M

**参る (mairu):** The kenjōgo (humble) form of both 行く (to go) and 来る (to come). Context determines which meaning is active.

**まあ (maa):** A softening/moderating word meaning "well / I suppose / sort of." Functions include moderation of claims, resigned acceptance (まあいいか), and gentle openers. まあまあ = "so-so."

**みたいだ (mitai da):** Casual equivalent of ようだ. Based on situational reading or resemblance. More flexible attachment than ようだ; attaches directly to any word type.

**もん / もの (mon/mono):** A sentence-final particle providing justification or explanation with a somewhat childlike, vulnerable, or endearing tone. "But it's because…!" Traditionally associated with feminine or childlike speech; usage is broadening among young speakers.

**モーラ (mora):** The basic timing unit of Japanese phonology. One mora = one beat. ん, っ, and both halves of long vowels each count as one mora. Natural Japanese speech runs at 5-7 morae per second.

---

## N

**なんか (nanka):** A casual vagueness marker and hedge meaning "something, somehow, kind of." Frequent in young casual speech. Strictly casual register. Polite equivalent: 何か (nani ka).

**nasal allophony (鼻子音の異音):** The phenomenon by which the moraic nasal ん is pronounced differently depending on the following consonant: [m] before p/b/m; [n] before t/d/n; [ŋ] before k/g; nasalized vowel before vowels.

**なるほど (naruhodo):** An aizuchi meaning "I see / that makes sense / indeed" — signals comprehension of an explanation. Slightly more formal than うん or そうか.

**ね (ne):** A sentence-final particle seeking agreement or creating shared ground. Assumes information is shared between speaker and listener. "Isn't it / right." Contrast: よ (new information).

**に違いない (ni chigai nai):** The highest-certainty evidential expression — strong subjective conviction. "There's no doubt / it must be." Formal and emphatic; not for casual conversation.

**認識モダリティ (ninshiki modariti):** Epistemic modality — grammatical marking of the speaker's degree of certainty about propositional content.

---

## O

**おいでになる (oide ni naru):** An alternative sonkeigo form for 行く/来る (いらっしゃる is more common; おいでになる is more formal).

**おっしゃる (ossharu):** Sonkeigo form of 言う (to say). "You/he/she says/said (respectfully)."

**外 (soto):** "Outside" — the out-group; people outside the speaker's circle (other companies, customers). Contrast with 内 (uchi).

---

## P

**ピッチアクセント (pitch accent):** A system where each mora in a word is either high (H) or low (L) in pitch. Japanese uses pitch accent rather than stress accent. Four types in Tokyo dialect: heiban, atamadaka, nakadaka, odaka.

---

## R

**ら抜き言葉 (ra-nuki kotoba):** "Words with ら removed" — the casual form of the potential of Group 2 verbs that drops ら (食べられる → 食べれる). Increasingly pervasive in casual speech; still marked as incorrect in formal contexts and JLPT writing.

**らしい (rashii):** An evidential expression meaning "apparently / I heard that (hearsay)" or "that's so typical of (typicality)." Attaches directly to any word type. Medium-high certainty based on information from others.

**連濁 (rendaku):** Sequential voicing — a morphophonological rule where the first consonant of the second element in a compound sometimes voices: 手紙 (te-gami), 赤ちゃん (aka-chan).

---

## S

**さ (sa):** A casual assertion and acknowledgment-seeking particle. "You know / like." Mild masculine lean; widely used. Can appear mid-sentence as a filler/attention marker.

**シャドーイング (shadōingu):** Shadowing — the technique of speaking along with or immediately after audio, repeating what was said while the next utterance is already playing. The most effective technique for closing the listening gap between textbook and natural speed Japanese.

**し (shi):** A sentence-final particle that lists reasons or qualities non-exhaustively, implying "and there's more." Sentence-final し alone implies "among other things."

**終助詞 (shūjoshi):** Sentence-final particles — grammatical morphemes attached to the end of utterances that express the speaker's attitude, seek information, or manage conversational interaction.

**尊敬語 (sonkeigo):** Honorific language that elevates others' actions. Uses special vocabulary (いらっしゃる, 召し上がる) or the productive pattern お〜になる. Never used for the speaker's own actions.

**そうか / そっか (sō ka / sokka):** An aizuchi expressing sudden understanding or realization. "Oh, I see / got it." Casual.

**そうだ (appearance):** Evidential expression based on direct sensory observation. Formation: い-adj stem + そう; な-adj + そう; verb ます-stem + そう. Cannot describe past events.

**そうだ (hearsay):** Evidential expression reporting information received from others. Formation: complete plain-form sentence + そうだ. Can describe past events.

**そういえば (sō ieba):** "Come to think of it / speaking of which" — a transition marker signaling an associative topic pivot, as if something in the current conversation triggered a related memory.

---

## T

**タメ口 (tameguchi):** Casual speech register — the plain/casual style used with close friends and peers. Contrasted with teineigo (polite speech). Characterized by contractions and use of rough sentence-final particles.

**丁寧語 (teineigo):** Polite language — the ます/です register. The baseline polite register, not technically sonkeigo/kenjōgo (no special vocabulary substitution). The first and most essential tier of formal Japanese.

**ところで (tokoro de):** "By the way" — a transition marker signaling a deliberate topic change. Neutral-to-formal register.

**って (tte) sentence-final:** Two distinct functions: (1) hearsay marker — reports information from another source ("I heard that…"); (2) casual topic marker replacing は.

**つもり (tsumori):** Expresses intention (東京に行くつもり = I intend to go to Tokyo) or the speaker's self-conception, which may differ from reality (上手に話しているつもりだった = I thought I was speaking well).

**つなぎ言葉 (tsunagi kotoba):** Linking words / discourse connectives used to transition between thoughts, topics, or narrative steps.

---

## U

**内 (uchi):** "Inside" — the in-group; the speaker's own company, family, or social circle. When speaking to outsiders (soto), the uchi group uses humble (kenjōgo) forms even when describing superiors.

---

## W

**わ (wa):** In standard Tokyo dialect, a traditionally feminine sentence-final particle expressing soft assertion or conclusion. Increasingly archaic among urban women under 40. Gender-neutral in Kansai dialect.

---

## Y

**ようだ (yōda):** An evidential expression meaning "it seems like (from indirect evidence or context)" — the speaker is reading a situation rather than directly observing or receiving hearsay. More formal register; casual equivalent is みたいだ.

**よ (yo):** A sentence-final particle signaling that the speaker is providing information the listener may not have. "I'm telling you / (new info)." Contrast: ね (shared information).

**よね (yone):** A sentence-final particle combining よ's assertion with ね's confirmation-seeking. "I'm fairly confident of this, and I think you'd agree."

---

## Z

**ぞ (zo):** A strongly masculine, casual sentence-final particle expressing strong assertion, declaration, or warning. Inappropriate in formal or mixed-gender professional contexts.

**ぜ (ze):** A masculine, casual sentence-final particle inviting shared action or making casual assertions. Slightly softer than ぞ. Common in fiction; declining in everyday speech.

**存じる / 存じております (zonjiru / zonjite orimasu):** Kenjōgo form of 知っている (to know). Used when humbly referring to your own knowledge in formal contexts.
