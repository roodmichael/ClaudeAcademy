## Core Concepts

### The Gap Between Textbook Japanese and Natural Spoken Japanese

Textbook Japanese teaches the formal polite register (丁寧体, teineigo) — the ます/です forms — and, in grammar explanations, the full plain forms. Natural spoken Japanese is neither. Speakers of casual Japanese compress and contract constantly. A learner who has studied thoroughly through N3 but has only used textbook materials will find real conversation — in drama, film, podcasts, or with native speakers — significantly harder to parse than their grammar knowledge suggests it should be. The contractions in this document represent the primary mechanism behind that gap.

The general principle: the more contracted a form is, the more informal it is. Contraction correlates inversely with register. For learners, this means building two competencies separately — recognition (passive) and production (active) — with different thresholds for when each contracted form is appropriate to use.

### The Rule Underlying Most Contractions

The majority of spoken contractions involve dropping the mora い (i) from ている, doing so across multiple positions in a chain. Once い drops, surrounding sounds often assimilate. This single process — い-deletion — generates most of the core contractions a learner needs to know.

## Key Definitions

**縮約形 (shukuyaku-kei):** Contracted forms used in casual spoken Japanese. Not considered errors in speech, but inappropriate in formal writing or standard JLPT exam Japanese.

**タメ口 (tameguchi) / 普通体 (futsūtai):** Plain/casual style speech contrasted with the polite ます/です style. The register in which most contractions occur.

**ら抜き言葉 (ra-nuki kotoba):** "ら-removed speech" — dropping ら from the potential form of ichidan (Group 2) verbs, e.g., 食べられる → 食べれる. Extremely common in casual speech; considered incorrect on formal exams but functionally accepted in everyday conversation. Has the functional advantage of disambiguating potential from passive.

## Process / Mechanics — The Contraction Table

The following table organizes contractions by category. The "Safety" column indicates whether the form is safe for learners to **produce** (P) or should be understood but used with caution (U).

### Core ている-Family Contractions

| Full Form | Contracted | Example | Notes | Safety |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------|--------|
| ている | てる | 見ている → 見てる | Most common contraction in Japanese. Universal in casual speech. | P |
| でいる | でる | 読んでいる → 読んでる | Applies to godan verbs whose て-form ends in で | P |
| ていた | てた | 食べていた → 食べてた | Past progressive: "was eating" | P |
| ていく | てく | 変わっていく → 変わってく | Direction/change over time | P |
| ておく | とく | 覚えておく → 覚えとく | Preparation/advance action contraction: て+お assimilation | P |
| ておいた | といた | 調べておいた → 調べといた | Past preparation form | P |
| ていた+の | てたの | 知っていたのか → 知ってたのか | Stacked contraction | U |
| ている+の | てんの | わかっているのか → わかってんのか | Double contraction; very casual | U |
| ている+の+だ | てんだ | 何をやっているのだ → 何やってんだ | Extremely casual; exasperation tone | U |
| ておる | とる | 〔関西〕おる → とる | Dialectal (Kansai); older style | U |

### てしまう Contractions

| Full Form | Contracted | Example | Notes | Safety |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------|--------|
| てしまう | ちゃう | 終わってしまう → 終わっちゃう | When て-form ends in て; completion/regret nuance | P |
| でしまう | じゃう | 死んでしまう → 死んじゃう | When て-form ends in で | P |
| てしまった | ちゃった | 忘れてしまった → 忘れちゃった | Past tense; "oops, I did it" | P |
| でしまった | じゃった | 飲んでしまった → 飲んじゃった | Past; で-type verb | P |

**Semantic note on ちゃう:** Like てしまう, ちゃう carries three layered meanings — (1) unintentional action done by accident, (2) complete/total completion, (3) a sense of regret or that the result cannot be undone. In casual speech among younger speakers, the regret nuance has faded and ちゃう often simply marks completion: 食べちゃった can just mean "I ate it all up" without strong emotional coloring.

### Nominalizer の → ん

| Full Form | Contracted | Example | Notes | Safety |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------|--------|
| のだ | んだ | 疲れたのだ → 疲れたんだ | Explanatory/assertive; extremely common | P |
| のです | んです | どこへ行くのですか → どこへ行くんですか | Polite explanatory; equally common | P |
| るの | んの | やるのか → やんのか | Verb + nominalizer; very casual | U |
| ない | ん | 知らない → 知らん | Negative shortened; rough but common | U |

### では → じゃ and Related

| Full Form | Contracted | Example | Notes | Safety |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------|--------|
| では | じゃ | これでは → これじゃ | Casual copula/contrast particle | P |
| ではない | じゃない | 違うのではない → 違うんじゃない | Negative; very frequent | P |
| という | って | 田中って人 | Quotative/topic marker; extremely common | P |
| という | っつ | 無理っつ | Even more compressed; rough casual | U |

### Conditional and Obligatory Contractions

| Full Form | Contracted | Example | Notes | Safety |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------|--------|
| れば | りゃ | 逃げれば → 逃げりゃ | Conditional shortening; rough | U |
| ければ | きゃ/けりゃ | 可愛ければ → 可愛きゃ | Adjective conditional; rough | U |
| なければ | なきゃ | やらなければ → やんなきゃ | Obligation "must do"; very common | P |
| なくては | なくちゃ | 行かなくては → 行かなくちゃ | Soft obligation; very common | P |
| らない | んない | 分からない → 分かんない | Godan negative; casual | U |
| ては | ちゃ | 見ては → 見ちゃ | "If [do X]…"; casual te-wa | U |

### Potential Form Contraction (ら抜き言葉)

| Full Form | Contracted | Example | Function Gained | Safety |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-----------------|--------|
| 食べられる | 食べれる | Can eat (only) vs. can eat OR be eaten | Disambiguates potential from passive | U (recognize) |
| 見られる | 見れる | Can see (only) | Same disambiguation advantage | U |
| 起きられる | 起きれる | Can wake up (only) | — | U |

**Pedagogical note:** ら抜き言葉 is grammatically incorrect on JLPT exams and formal writing. It is pervasive in casual speech and should be recognized. Learners who use it in casual conversation will sound natural; those who use it in formal contexts (writing, presentation, business) will be corrected.

### I-Adjective Vowel Compression

These are rougher contractions characteristic of very casual or emphatic speech:

| Full Form | Contracted | Example | Register |
|-----------|-----------|---------|----------|
| ~ai い | ~ee ぇ | 高い → 高ぇ | Very rough; masculine casual |
| ~oi い | ~ee | 強い → 強ぇ | Same |
| 凄い | すご | すごい → すご | Common, especially in compounds |

## Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Using contracted forms in formal contexts.** ちゃう, てる, and じゃない are native-sounding in casual speech and jarringly out of place in business situations, formal emails, or JLPT writing sections. The register boundary must be learned, not just the forms.

2. **Confusing でる (contraction of でいる) with 出る (to exit/come out).** Both sound identical in casual speech: 読んでる (reading) vs. 外に出る (go outside). Context disambiguates.

3. **Over-applying ちゃう to all てしまう meanings.** In formal apologies or serious contexts (謝罪, business), the contracted form sounds flippant. Use てしまいました in formal situations.

4. **Assuming all contractions are interchangeable across dialects.** とる for ておる is a Kansai form. とく for ておく is standard casual. They sound similar but come from different sources.

5. **Expecting written Japanese to use these forms.** With the exception of manga dialogue and messaging/texting, written Japanese does not use casual contractions even when the content is informal.

## Hands-On Elements

- **Contraction drill:** Provide sentences in full form; students convert to casual contracted form, then back. Focus first on ている→てる, ておく→とく, てしまう→ちゃう, のだ→んだ, ではない→じゃない.
- **Register-sorting exercise:** Give students a list of sentences; identify which would work with a friend, which would work with a boss, which with neither.
- **Listening transcription:** Play short casual speech audio (anime, drama) at natural speed; students transcribe then identify each contraction type.
- **ら抜き言葉 recognition:** Given a list of potential forms, identify which are standard, which are ら抜き, and write the full standard form alongside.

## Expert Debates / Open Questions

- **ら抜き言葉 acceptability is an ongoing debate in Japanese society.** The Agency for Cultural Affairs surveys show that ら抜き言葉 usage has steadily increased each decade and is now a majority pattern among younger speakers. Prescriptivists maintain it is an error; descriptivists argue it has become standard colloquial usage. The JLPT treats it as incorrect. Teachers disagree on whether to teach it actively or only as a recognition item.

- **When does comprehension become production?** No clear threshold exists in the research for which casual contractions a learner at N3 level should be producing. The general guidance from practitioner sources is that learners should produce the safest, most universal contractions (てる, じゃない, んだ, なきゃ) and aim to recognize the rougher ones without necessarily producing them.
