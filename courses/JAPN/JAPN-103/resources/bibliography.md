## Tier 2 — Academic / Peer-Reviewed

**Saigo, H. (2011).** *The Japanese Sentence-Final Particles in Talk-in-Interaction.* John Benjamins Publishing. URL: https://benjamins.com/catalog/pbns.205
*Used for: Academic treatment of sentence-final particles as social action management devices. Core academic support for Sessions 03-04.*

**Konnelly, L., et al. (2023).** "Unconventional Usage of Gender-Based Japanese Sentence-Final Particles (wa and no)." *MDPI Languages*, 8(3), 222. URL: https://www.mdpi.com/2226-471X/8/3/222
*Used for: Modern gendered particle usage — confirming that young urban Japanese speakers use traditionally gendered particles more flexibly than textbooks describe. Session 04.*

**Hamada, Y. (2016).** "Shadowing: Who benefits and how? Uncovering a hidden challenge for EFL learners." *International Journal of Applied Linguistics.* (Cited through NihongoKnow; not web-fetchable directly)
*Used for: Academic support for the efficacy of shadowing for phonological development and listening comprehension. Session 09.*

**ResearchGate (2024).** "An Exploration of Japanese Cultural Dynamics and Communication Practices through Social Pragmatics." URL: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/379931914_An_Exploration_of_Japanese_Cultural_Dynamics_Communication_Practices_through_Social_Pragmatics
*Used for: Indirect communication, ちょっと as social signal, agreement/disagreement systems. Session 11.*

## Tier 4 — Well-Known Books (Cited; Not Web-Fetchable)

**Chino, Naoko.** *All About Particles.* Kodansha, 2001.
*A comprehensive reference for Japanese particles — scope, nuance, and usage patterns. Supplementary reference for Sessions 03-05.*

**Makino, Seiichi and Michio Tsutsui.** *A Dictionary of Intermediate Japanese Grammar.* Japan Times, 1995.
*The standard academic grammar dictionary for intermediate learners. Supplementary reference for the certainty/evidential expressions in Sessions 10-11, including the らしい function distinction.*

**Rubin, Jay.** *Making Sense of Japanese: What the Textbooks Don't Tell You.* Kodansha, 2002.
*A short, excellent book on what Japanese textbooks get wrong. Particularly useful for understanding the gap between formal and casual registers.*

**Saito, Hitoshi.** *Shadowing: Let's Speak Japanese.* Kinseido, 2012.
*The standard academic shadowing curriculum for Japanese learners. Graded from slow to natural speed with transcripts. Directly cited in Session 09 as a recommended resource.*

**AJALT.** *Japanese for Busy People* series. Kodansha International.
*Covers workplace keigo scripts in depth, including telephone answering, business email, and client greeting scenarios. Recommended supplementary resource for Sessions 06-07.*

## Recommended Further Reading (Not Directly Cited)

**NHK Pitch Accent Dictionary (NHKアクセント辞典).** Japan Broadcasting Publishing, 1998.
The authoritative print reference for standard Japanese pitch accent. Not web-fetchable but indispensable for serious pitch accent study.

**Vance, Timothy J.** *The Sounds of Japanese.* Cambridge University Press, 2008.
Academic phonology reference covering vowel devoicing, nasal allophony, and connected speech phenomena in depth.

**Kubozono, Haruo (ed.).** *Handbook of Japanese Phonetics and Phonology.* De Gruyter Mouton, 2015.
Comprehensive academic reference for the phonological phenomena covered in Sessions 08-09.
