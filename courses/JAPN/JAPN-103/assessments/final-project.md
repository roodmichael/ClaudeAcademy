# Final Project — JAPN-103
**Covers:** All Sessions (01–11)

---

## Overview

This course has trained you in the full conversational layer of N3 Japanese: contractions, particles, discourse markers, keigo, listening mechanics, and evidential grammar. The final project asks you to synthesize this knowledge into a real, functioning piece of Japanese that demonstrates you can work in both casual and formal registers — not just define them.

This is a performance project, not an exam. You will produce two short pieces: a casual conversation and a formal exchange, both on related topics, then write a brief analysis of your own choices. The goal is to show you can produce natural Japanese, not just classify it.

---

## The Task

### Part 1: The Casual Conversation (Original Production)

Write a casual dialogue of at least 15 exchanges (a total of 30 turns: 15 for Speaker A, 15 for Speaker B) between two close friends. The topic is your choice, but the conversation must feel like a real conversation — with natural flow, not a grammar exercise.

**Required elements (check each off when included):**

☐ At least three different contractions from Sessions 01-02 used naturally (not forced)
☐ At least four different sentence-final particles from Sessions 03-04 used correctly
☐ At least two hesitation fillers and one transition marker from Session 05
☐ At least two different aizuchi exchanges between speakers
☐ At least one indirect refusal using ちょっと
☐ At least one evidential/uncertainty expression chosen for the correct evidence basis (Session 10)
☐ One discovery moment (なるほど, そうか, へえ, or similar) with appropriate context
☐ A natural conversation close (not just an abrupt ending)

### Part 2: The Formal Exchange (Original Production)

Write a formal/professional exchange of at least 10 exchanges (20 total turns). This can be:
- A job-related conversation between a new employee and their boss
- A customer service interaction
- A business call scenario
- A formal introduction in a professional setting

**Required elements:**

☐ At least two sonkeigo verb forms (for the superior or customer's actions) used correctly
☐ At least two kenjōgo forms (for your own or your in-group's actions) used correctly
☐ Correct uchi/soto treatment (if the scenario involves an outside party)
☐ At least one appropriate keigo request or response phrase (ご確認いただけますか, お送りいたします, etc.)
☐ Natural teineigo throughout as the baseline

### Part 3: Analysis (300–500 words)

After writing your dialogues, write a brief analysis in English (or Japanese — your choice) addressing:

1. **Register decisions:** Pick two specific moments where you made a deliberate register choice (casual vs. formal; one particle vs. another). Explain why you chose what you chose.

2. **Keigo direction:** In your formal exchange, identify one sonkeigo choice and one kenjōgo choice. Confirm you have the direction right: sonkeigo for whose action? Kenjōgo for whose?

3. **Your hardest challenge:** What was the single hardest thing to write naturally in either dialogue? What made it hard?

4. **Shadowing connection:** How has (or how would) shadowing practice help you *hear* the patterns you wrote in Part 1?

---

## What to Submit

Submit all three parts together as one document to the Tutor. The Tutor will:
- Read both dialogues for naturalness, not just grammatical correctness
- Verify the keigo direction in Part 2
- Respond to your analysis in Part 3 with specific observations

---

## Evaluation Criteria

- **Naturalness:** Does the casual dialogue sound like real people, or like a textbook exercise? Are particles used where they actually belong, or just appended randomly?
- **Keigo accuracy:** Are sonkeigo and kenjōgo applied to the correct subjects? No mixing of direction.
- **Evidence basis:** The evidential expression used in Part 1 should match the stated or implied evidence situation — not just a random かもしれない
- **Discourse management:** Does the casual conversation flow? Are there natural entrances, pivots, and an exit?
- **Analysis depth:** Part 3 should show genuine reflection, not just summary

## Tutor Scoring Guide

**Strong submissions:**
- Part 1 dialogue reads naturally when read aloud — particles appear at utterance-final positions, contractions are not forced in formal speech contexts within the dialogue, aizuchi comes at reasonable pacing
- Part 2 keigo direction is consistently correct — the most common failure is kenjōgo for others or sonkeigo for self
- Part 3 analysis shows meta-linguistic awareness: the student knows *why* they made choices, not just that they made them
- The hardest challenge described in Part 3 usually reveals the student's actual gap: students with strong listening say particles; students with strong reading say contractions in production; students from formal grammar backgrounds say aizuchi timing

**What to push back on:**
- Casual dialogues that contain no particles at all (flat, textbook-formal sentences in a "casual" setting)
- Keigo forms used for the wrong subject — address specifically which verb and which direction
- Analysis that just summarizes the dialogue rather than analyzing decisions
- Shadowing reflection that is vague ("it would help with listening") — ask for specific phonological features from Session 08 that the student wrote and would hear differently after shadowing
