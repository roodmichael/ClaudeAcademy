# Midterm Assessment — JAPN-103
**Covers:** Sessions 01–06

---

## Overview

Sessions 01–06 of JAPN-103 have given you two distinct skill sets: the casual speech layer (contractions, sentence-final particles, discourse markers) and the formal speech system (keigo). The midterm asks you to demonstrate that you understand both layers as systems — not just isolated facts — by analyzing a real Japanese dialogue for the choices its speakers are making.

This is a written analytical exercise. There is no right or wrong register choice to make yourself; the task is to identify and explain what is happening in someone else's Japanese.

---

## The Task

Below is a Japanese dialogue between two people. Read it carefully.

---

**A:** えーと、田中課長、少しよろしいでしょうか。
**B:** あ、どうぞ。何かな？
**A:** 実は、明日のプレゼンについてなんですが、ちょっと変更したい部分がありまして。
**B:** そう？どの辺？
**A:** スライドの3枚目なんですが、数字がちょっと古いかもしれなくて…
**B:** あー、そうか。じゃあ、更新しといて。
**A:** はい、かしこまりました。あと、資料をご確認いただけますか？
**B:** うん、後でね。ちょっと今忙しいから。
**A:** もちろんです。では、後ほどお送りいたします。
**B:** ありがとう。お疲れ。

---

## What to Submit

Answer each of the following questions in writing. You can answer in English or Japanese — use whichever allows you to be more precise.

**Part 1: Register Analysis**

1. Who is Speaker A and who is Speaker B (in terms of their work relationship)? How does the language in the dialogue tell you this?

2. Find two places where Speaker A uses keigo (sonkeigo or kenjōgo). Identify the specific verb or expression and explain which tier it belongs to and whose action it refers to.

3. Speaker B uses much more casual speech than A. Find two examples of casual speech markers in B's lines (contractions, casual particles, or casual discourse markers) and explain what they signal about the relationship.

**Part 2: Particle and Discourse Marker Analysis**

4. Speaker A says ちょっと変更したい部分がありまして and then ちょっと古いかもしれなくて. Are these uses of ちょっと functioning as: softening, qualifying, or indirect refusal? Explain your reasoning.

5. Speaker B says そうか at line 7. What emotional function does そうか serve here? How is it different from そうですか?

6. Speaker B says ちょっと今忙しいから at line 9. Is this a literal "a little busy" or a signal? What does it mean for Speaker A's request to review the materials?

**Part 3: Uncertainty and Evidentiality**

7. Speaker A says 数字がちょっと古いかもしれなくて. Why is かもしれない the appropriate uncertainty expression here? What evidence basis is Speaker A claiming?

**Part 4: Your Own Assessment**

8. What is one thing Speaker A does well in this conversation in terms of natural spoken Japanese? What is one thing that still sounds slightly textbook-formal that a more advanced speaker might soften?

---

## Evaluation Criteria

- Correct identification of keigo tier with specific evidence (not just "it sounds formal")
- Accurate analysis of ちょっと in its different functions — especially recognizing the indirect signal
- Ability to connect register observations to the social relationship being described
- Thoughtful engagement with Part 4 — showing you can evaluate, not just identify

---

## Tutor Scoring Guide

**Strong response characteristics:**
- Identifies B as a superior based on linguistic evidence (casual particles, giving instructions rather than requesting, ending with just ありがとう and お疲れ without ございます)
- Correctly identifies ご確認いただけますか (kenjōgo — humble request for B's action) and お送りいたします (kenjōgo — humble for A's own action)
- Notes B's casual contractions: しといて (from しておいて), じゃあ as casual transition, the casual particle ね in 後でね
- Distinguishes ちょっと忙しいから as a soft "not now / please don't push this" rather than literal
- Recognizes かもしれない as appropriate because A is speculating, not certain — checking in rather than asserting a fact

**Common weaknesses to watch for:**
- Confusing sonkeigo and kenjōgo direction
- Missing that ちょっと忙しいから is social hedging, not a literal description of B's busyness level
- Treating B's casual speech as "incorrect" rather than as an appropriate register signal from a superior
- Part 4 answers that are too generic — push the student toward specific linguistic observations
