## Exercise 1: ちょっと Function Identification
**Type:** Analysis / Written

For each use of ちょっと, identify which function it serves: (A) softening a request, (B) qualifying a statement, or (C) indirect refusal signal.

1. A: 週末一緒に飲みに行かない？B: うーん、ちょっと… (pause)
2. ちょっと待ってもらえますか？
3. この計画はちょっと無理かもしれません。
4. A: これ、頼んでいいですか？B: それはちょっと難しいですね。
5. ちょっといいですか、聞きたいことがあって。

**For each refusal signal (C):** How would you know for sure that ちょっと means "no" in this context rather than "a little"?

## Exercise 2: Discovery Prosody Practice
**Type:** Speaking / Oral

Say each discovery expression aloud with appropriate prosody. Then describe in one sentence the emotional state it conveys.

1. あ！（when you suddenly remember something you forgot）
2. へえ〜…（when you learn something mildly interesting but not shocking）
3. へえー！！（when you hear something genuinely surprising）
4. なるほど。（when something that was unclear has now been explained）
5. そっか…（when you quietly realize something disappointing or bittersweet）

**What to observe:** The same word with different prosody (pitch, length, speed) conveys entirely different emotional content. Record yourself saying each one and listen back — do they sound distinct?

## Exercise 3: Indirect Disagreement
**Type:** Written Production

For each situation, write a natural Japanese response that disagrees indirectly without saying いいえ or 違います directly.

1. A colleague proposes an unrealistic deadline: このプロジェクト、来週までに終わりますよね？

2. Someone suggests a restaurant you don't want to go to: あのイタリアンレストランにしない？

3. A classmate claims something incorrect about Japanese grammar: んですか is the same as のですか, right?

4. Your Japanese friend invites you to a late-night event when you have work the next morning: 今夜12時まで一緒にいようよ！

Write each response and identify which indirect disagreement strategy you used.

## Exercise 4: Full Conversation — All Layers
**Type:** Written Production

Write a 15-line casual conversation between two friends (call them A and B) that incorporates each of the following (check each off when used):

☐ At least one hesitation filler (えーと, あの, うーん)
☐ At least one discourse marker / transition (じゃあ, そういえば, ところで)
☐ At least two different aizuchi from the listener
☐ One use of ちょっと as indirect refusal
☐ One uncertainty/evidential expression (らしい, かもしれない, そうだ, ようだ, or はずだ)
☐ One use of なんか or まあ as a hedge
☐ One discovery expression (なるほど, へえ, そうか, あ！)
☐ A natural conversation closing

Topic: A is asking B about a rumor they heard and B is explaining what they know.

After writing the conversation, read it aloud. Where does it sound natural? Where does it still feel scripted?
