# Session 09 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz. This session's exercises require audio sources. Use the recommended materials from the lesson.

## Exercise 1: Shadowing Start — Phase 1 Protocol
**Type:** Hands-on / Speaking (Required audio)

Select a piece of audio from the recommended materials list (NHK Web Easy, Nihongo con Teppei for Beginners, or equivalent). Choose something 2–3 minutes long with an available transcript.

Follow the Phase 1 protocol:
1. Pre-listen 2-3x without transcript (note: roughly how much did you follow?)
2. Read transcript aloud at audio pace
3. Listen with transcript — track where you are
4. Shadow with transcript visible for one full pass at 0.8x speed
5. Shadow once more at 1.0x speed

**Write:**
- Source you used:
- Estimated comprehension before shadowing (% of words you followed):
- One specific sound or contraction that tripped you up during shadowing:
- One phrase that felt more natural after the third pass than the first:

## Exercise 2: Shadowing Speed Experiment
**Type:** Hands-on / Speaking (Required audio)

Take the same audio from Exercise 1. Shadow the same 60-second segment at three different speeds: 0.7x, 1.0x, and 1.2x.

Write a brief comparison:
- At 0.7x: where was your attention? Did you focus on sounds or meaning?
- At 1.0x: what was hardest to keep up with?
- At 1.2x: what happened to your comprehension vs. your production ability?

**What to observe:** Most learners find that at 1.2x speed, they can still *say* much of the audio even when meaning comprehension drops. This decoupling (sound before meaning) is the goal of Phase 2 shadowing.

## Exercise 3: Dictation Challenge
**Type:** Listening / Written

Find a new 30-second clip of natural casual Japanese (not the same source as Exercise 1). Listen and transcribe as completely as you can.

After your best attempt at transcription, find or request the transcript from the tutor. Compare your transcription to the source.

For each error, classify it as:
- **V** (vocabulary — unknown word)
- **P** (phonological — knew the word, missed the sound)
- **C** (contraction — recognized in writing, missed in speech)
- **A** (attention — processing fell behind)

Total errors by category: V___ P___ C___ A___

**What to observe:** Which category dominated? This tells you where to direct your practice energy.

## Exercise 4: Materials Evaluation
**Type:** Research / Written

Spend 15 minutes sampling three different listening sources from the list below (or others you know). For each, note:
1. Approximate speed (slow/medium/fast compared to what you're comfortable with)
2. Formality level (casual speech? news? designed-for-learners?)
3. Transcript available? (Yes/No/Where)
4. Your comprehension on first pass (%)

**Sources to sample:**
- NHK Web Easy (nhk.or.jp/lesson/)
- Nihongo con Teppei for Beginners (podcast — search on podcast app)
- Any slice-of-life anime scene with Japanese subtitles

**What to observe:** Where does each source fall in the progression from Phase 1 to Phase 3 material? What does your current 70% comprehension level look like across these sources?

## Exercise 5: Design Your Weekly Schedule
**Type:** Planning / Written

Design a realistic 7-day listening practice schedule based on your actual life. Use the minimum of 75 minutes / week target.

- Day 1: ___ min — Type (shadowing / extensive / intensive)
- Day 2: ___ min — Type
- Day 3: ___ min — Type
- Day 4: ___ min — Type
- Day 5: ___ min — Type
- Day 6: ___ min — Type
- Day 7: ___ min — Type

Total: ___ min

**Identify:**
- Which day is your anchor (most reliable daily shadowing slot)?
- What content will you use for extensive listening? (Commute? Exercise? Background while doing something else?)
- What's the minimum you'd do on a bad week?

**What to observe:** The schedule that works is the one you actually do, not the most ambitious one. Design for your real week, not your ideal week.
