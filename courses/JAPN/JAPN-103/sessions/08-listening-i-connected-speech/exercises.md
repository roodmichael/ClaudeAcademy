## Exercise 1: Mora Counting Drill
**Type:** Analysis / Written

Count the morae in each word. Remember: ん, っ, and both halves of long vowels (ー, おう, えい) each count as one mora.

1. ビール (bi-i-ru) = ___
2. テレビ (te-re-bi) = ___
3. 切手 (ki-t-te) = ___
4. コーヒー (ko-o-hi-i) = ___
5. 雑誌 (zas-shi) = ___
6. 新聞 (shin-bun) = ___
7. 東京 (to-u-kyo-u) = ___
8. えっと (et-to) = ___
9. 飛行機 (hi-ko-u-ki) = ___
10. ありがとうございます = ___

**What to observe:** Which ones surprised you? Long vowel halves (コーヒーの ー) and small っ are the most common miscounts.

## Exercise 2: Vowel Devoicing Map
**Type:** Analysis / Written

For each word below, identify: (A) which vowel devoices, and (B) between which two voiceless consonants it appears.

1. です (desu) — devoiced vowel: ___ between: ___ and ___
2. ます (masu) — devoiced vowel: ___ between: ___ and ___
3. 机 (tsukue, tsu-ku-e) — devoiced vowel: ___
4. 切符 (kippu, ki-p-pu) — devoiced vowel: ___
5. 聞きます (kikimasu) — list ALL devoiced vowels

**What to observe:** Say each word aloud with the devoiced vowel whispered or silent. Does it start to sound more natural? This is what native speakers actually produce.

## Exercise 3: Pitch Accent Awareness
**Type:** Analysis / Research

Using jisho.org (which displays pitch accent for many entries), look up the following words and note their pitch accent type (heiban, atamadaka, nakadaka, or odaka). If jisho.org doesn't show it, note that.

1. 雨 (ame) — rain
2. 飴 (ame) — candy
3. 橋 (hashi) — bridge
4. 箸 (hashi) — chopsticks
5. 花 (hana) — flower
6. 鼻 (hana) — nose
7. 日本語 (nihongo) — Japanese language
8. 勉強 (benkyou) — study

For the minimal pairs (1/2, 3/4, 5/6): Say both words in a sentence. Can you hear the pitch difference? Even approximate awareness of the distinction trains your ear over time.

## Exercise 4: ん Allophony
**Type:** Analysis / Speaking

Say each compound word aloud and identify how ん sounds in each (like 'm', 'n', 'ng', or a nasalized vowel):

1. 新聞 (shimbun) — ん before b: sounds like ___
2. 三月 (sangatsu) — ん before g: sounds like ___
3. 本棚 (hondana) — ん before d: sounds like ___
4. 安心 (anshin) — ん before sh: sounds like ___
5. 漫画 (manga) — ん before g: sounds like ___

**What to observe:** 新聞 is the clearest example — the ん before b clearly becomes an [m] sound (shin-bun → shim-bun). Once you hear this one, the pattern extends to the others.

## Exercise 5: Natural Speed Analysis
**Type:** Listening / Written

Find a 30-second clip of natural casual Japanese conversation (suggestions: a Japanese YouTube vlog, a Nihongo con Teppei podcast episode, or a slice-of-life anime scene without extreme speech styles). Listen three times:

**Listen 1:** Without any goal — just exposure. How much can you follow?
**Listen 2:** Count how many times you hear a word you know but couldn't initially parse. What was happening phonologically?
**Listen 3:** Focus specifically on sentence endings — what particles and sounds appear there?

Write brief notes:
- Estimated pace: ___ mora/second (guess — is it around 5, 6, or 7?)
- At least 2 words you recognized on listen 3 that you missed on listen 1
- 1 pattern (devoicing, contraction, etc.) you can now identify in the clip
