# Session 04 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

---

## Exercise 1: Register and Context Sorting
**Type:** Analysis / Written

For each sentence below, decide: is this particle choice appropriate for the context described? If not, suggest a better alternative.

1. **Context:** A job interview. The candidate says: 御社で働きたいぞ。(I want to work at your company.)
   - Appropriate? Why or why not? Better option?

2. **Context:** A male high school student texting a close friend: やろうぜ！今日サッカー。(Let's do it! Soccer today.)
   - Appropriate? Why or why not?

3. **Context:** A woman in her 50s speaking to her daughter: 大丈夫よ、心配しなくていいわ。(It's fine, don't worry.)
   - Appropriate? Why or why not?

4. **Context:** A 22-year-old student explaining to a classmate why they didn't finish homework: だって、バイトが遅くまであったんだもん。
   - Appropriate? What does もん signal about the speaker's emotional tone?

**What to observe:** In which cases did the particle carry social information beyond its literal meaning? What would a native speaker infer about the speaker from each choice?

## Exercise 2: Hearsay って vs. Topic って
**Type:** Analysis / Written

Classify each use of って as (A) hearsay or (B) casual topic marker. Then write the more formal equivalent.

1. あの人って誰？
2. 明日、学校休みだって。
3. このアプリって使いやすい？
4. 彼女、転職するって聞いた。
5. この映画って有名なの？

**What to observe:** In sentences 3 and 5, what word does って replace? In sentences 2 and 4, what is the source of the information?

## Exercise 3: し — Non-Exhaustive Listing Practice
**Type:** Written Production

Write three sentences using し that list reasons for a feeling or decision. Use し at least twice in each sentence, and end the last one with sentence-final し (trailing off).

Example: 美味しいし、量も多いし、また行きたいな。

1. Why you like (or don't like) a particular type of exercise. (Use し to list 2 reasons)
2. Why a friend should visit Japan. (Use し to list 3 qualities)
3. Why you're tired today. (End with sentence-final し trailing off — "...you know how it is.")

## Exercise 4: Media Audit
**Type:** Analysis / Thought Experiment

Think of a Japanese anime, manga, or drama you've encountered. (If none, use the examples below.)

**If you have a source:** Pick two characters of different genders or personality types. Write down any sentence-final particles you remember or can look up from their speech patterns. What do the particle choices tell you about how those characters are positioned?

**If using provided examples, analyze these character types:**
- Character A: A tough male protagonist in a sports anime who often says 行くぞ！and 絶対勝つぜ！
- Character B: A soft-spoken male supporting character who says そうだね and そうかもしれないし
- Character C: An older female teacher who says そうですわ and よく頑張りましたわ

For each character: What does their particle use tell you about their social register, personality, and intended audience impression?

**What to observe:** Are any of these particles ones you'd use in your own Japanese? Which ones, and in what situations?

## Exercise 5: Oral Drill — Produce the Particles
**Type:** Speaking

Say each sentence out loud five times, focusing on the particle at the end. Make it confident and audible.

1. そんなのさ、全然気にしなくていいよ。(That stuff — don't worry about it at all.)
2. 美味しいし、雰囲気もいいし、また来たいな。(It's good, the vibe is nice too — I want to come back.)
3. だって、難しかったんだもん。(But it was hard!)
4. 明日、雨だって。(Apparently it'll rain tomorrow.)
5. 絶対やるぞ。(I'm absolutely doing this. [masculine, emphatic])

**What to observe:** Which particle felt most unnatural or uncomfortable to produce? That's the one your ear hasn't calibrated to yet — seek it out in media this week.
