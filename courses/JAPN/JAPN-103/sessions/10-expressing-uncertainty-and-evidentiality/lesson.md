# Session 10: Expressing Uncertainty and Evidentiality
**Course:** JAPN-103
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to select the correct expression from the certainty spectrum (かな through に違いない) based on your actual evidence basis — not just meaning, but what kind of knowledge you're claiming to have.

---

## Opening

Here is a moment every Japanese learner eventually faces. You want to say "I think it might rain." You know several Japanese expressions that seem to fit. かもしれない. らしい. ようだ. そうだ. はずだ. You pick one — maybe かもしれない because it feels most "uncertainty-ish" — and the sentence sounds fine. But a Japanese speaker would have chosen a different one. Not because you were wrong in meaning, but because each of these expressions encodes something different: not just how certain you are, but *how you know what you know.*

Japanese grammar encodes epistemic stance — the source of your knowledge — in a way English mostly doesn't. This session teaches you to pick the right expression not by feeling but by asking: "What is my evidence basis?"

---

## The Certainty-Evidence Framework

The following spectrum maps the major expressions from lowest to highest speaker certainty. More important than the certainty level is the **evidence column** — what kind of knowledge each expression encodes.

| Expression | Certainty | Evidence Basis | Colloquial Form | Register |
|-----------|-----------|----------------|-----------------|---------|
| かな | Very low | Pure wondering; no evidence | — | Casual-neutral |
| かもしれない | Low | Possibility; no concrete basis | かも | All registers |
| 〜っぽい | Low-medium | Observable characteristics (informal) | — | Casual only |
| 気がする | Low-medium | Gut feeling / intuition | — | Casual-neutral |
| みたいだ | Medium | Situational reading; resemblance | みたい | Casual |
| そうだ (appearance) | Medium | Direct visual/sensory observation | そう | Casual-neutral |
| ようだ | Medium-high | Indirect context / situational inference | — | Formal; writing |
| らしい (hearsay) | Medium-high | Information from others; indirect sources | — | Casual-neutral |
| と思う | Variable | Speaker's own assessment / intuition | — | All registers |
| だろう / でしょう | Medium-high | Reasoning with justification | だろ | だろ casual; でしょ formal |
| はずだ | High | Logical inference from objective facts | — | Neutral |
| に違いない | Very high | Strong subjective conviction | — | Formal; emphatic |

## The Most Critical Distinction: Hearsay vs. Appearance そうだ

This is the single most tested N3 nuance, and the one where learners make the most errors.

**Two verbs, one word, one mora of difference in formation:**

### Appearance そうだ — What you directly observe
**Formation:** い-adj: drop い, add そう (高い → 高そう); な-adj: plain stem + そう; verb: ます-stem + そう

- 雨が降り**そうだ**。(It looks like it's going to rain — I can see the dark sky right now.)
- 美味し**そう**！(Looks delicious! — direct visual impression)
- 彼、疲れ**そう**だね。(He looks tired — you can see it on his face)

Key property: **You are the observer, right now.** Appearance そうだ cannot describe the past (you can't visually observe something that already happened).

### Hearsay そうだ — What you were told
**Formation:** Complete plain-form sentence (including plain だ for nouns/na-adjectives) + そうだ

- 雨が降る**そうだ**。(I heard it's going to rain — weather report, someone told me)
- あの映画、面白い**そうだ**。(Apparently that movie is good — I heard/read it)
- 田中さんは来ない**そうだ**。(I heard that Tanaka-san won't come)

Key property: **You received this information.** Hearsay そうだ can describe past events (you can hear about what happened).

**The one-word test:**
- 高**そう** (adjective stem + そう) = "looks expensive" [appearance]
- 高い**そうだ** (complete adjective + そうだ) = "I heard it's expensive" [hearsay]

## The Four You Need to Distinguish Most

These four cause the most confusion because they all express something like "it seems":

### かもしれない — Pure Possibility (No Evidence Required)
*Claim: "It is possible."*

Evidence basis: None required. You're expressing that something isn't ruled out.

- 彼は来るかもしれない。(He might come — you have no particular reason to think so)
- 間違えたかもしれない。(I might have made a mistake — just a thought)
- Colloquial: かも alone (sentence-final): 雨かも。(Maybe rain.)

**When NOT to use:** If you have evidence (you saw dark clouds, you heard something), use a higher-certainty expression. かもしれない undersells what you know.

### らしい — Hearsay / Indirect Evidence
*Claim: "I have heard that" / "Evidence from sources points to this."*

Evidence basis: Information from others, things you've read, reports, rumor. Also: typicality ("that's so like him").

- あの映画、面白いらしいよ。(Apparently that movie is interesting — I heard from someone)
- 田中さんは来ないらしい。(It seems Tanaka-san won't come — indirect evidence)
- 彼らしい行動だ。(That's so typical of him — the typicality function)

**The flexibility advantage:** らしい attaches directly to any word type without special conjugation.

### ようだ — Situational Inference (Indirect)
*Claim: "Based on the surrounding evidence, I infer."*

Evidence basis: You are reading a situation — not direct observation (that's そうだ), not hearsay (that's らしい), but indirect inference from context.

- 彼は来ないようだ。(It seems he's not coming — phone not answered, no message, context suggests)
- 誰かいるようだ。(Someone seems to be here — lights on, sounds, indirect evidence)
- Register: More formal; preferred in writing; casual equivalent is みたいだ

### はずだ — Logical Expectation
*Claim: "Based on facts I know, X should logically be the case."*

Evidence basis: Objective, knowable facts that lead to a logical inference. Not speculation, not hearsay — reasoning.

- 電気がついているから、誰かいるはずだ。(The lights are on, so someone must be here — logical inference)
- 練習したから、上手になるはずだ。(I practiced, so I should be getting better — logical expectation)
- はずがない: (There's no way that can be true) — the negative is emphatic denial

**Frustrated はず:** はずだったのに expresses that a logical expectation was violated — disappointment.
- 来るはずだったのに、来なかった。(They were supposed to come but didn't.)

## The Expressions That Cause Grammar Mismatches

### と思う — The Speaker's Own Assessment
と思う is uniquely flexible because it encodes *the speaker's thinking* — it can accompany almost any certainty level.

- 彼は来ると思う。(I think he'll come — personal assessment)
- 間違っていると思うんですが… (I think this might be wrong, but… — softened disagreement)
- In hedged form: と思うんですが is one of the most useful expressions for softening potentially difficult statements

### つもり — Intention and Self-Conception
Two distinct uses:
1. **Intention:** 東京に行くつもりです。(I intend to go to Tokyo.)
2. **Self-conception (possibly mistaken):** 上手に話しているつもりだった。(I thought I was speaking well — but apparently wasn't.)

The second use is the nuanced one. つもり describes how the speaker *conceives of themselves* or their action — which may differ from reality. It is not the same as はずだ (objective inference) — it is subjective self-assessment.

## Two More to Know for N3

### だろう / でしょう — Reasoned Opinion with Probability
- **だろう** (casual): 明日は晴れるだろう。(It will probably be sunny tomorrow — reasoned opinion)
- **でしょう** (formal/seeking agreement): 明日、来られるでしょうか。(Will you be able to come tomorrow, do you think?)

Used when the speaker has a basis for their opinion but it is still a probability, not certainty.

### に違いない — Subjective Conviction
High-certainty expression; more emphatic than はずだ but based on the speaker's own conviction rather than objective logic.

- あんなに練習したんだから、うまくなったに違いない。(After all that practice, he must have gotten good.)
- Register: Formal and somewhat literary. Using it in casual conversation sounds overwrought. For emphatic certainty in casual speech, 絶対〜だよ sounds more natural.

## Production Checklist

Before choosing an uncertainty expression, ask yourself these three questions:

1. **What is my evidence basis?**
   - Nothing (just a thought) → かもしれない or かな
   - I heard / read it → らしい or hearsay そうだ
   - I can see / observe it right now → appearance そうだ
   - I'm reading context / indirect signs → ようだ or みたいだ
   - Logical inference from facts → はずだ
   - Strong personal conviction → に違いない

2. **What register am I in?**
   - Casual: みたいだ, らしい, だろ, かも
   - Formal: ようだ, はずだ, に違いない, でしょう

3. **Am I talking about the past, present, or future?**
   - Appearance そうだ cannot describe the past (you can't observe what already happened)
   - All others work in any tense

## Why This Matters

English speakers often underspecify their evidence source — "I think it might rain" works whether you saw clouds, heard a forecast, or have a gut feeling. Japanese requires you to specify. This is not harder; it is more precise. Once you internalize the framework, it feels natural — you stop saying かもしれない for everything and start picking the expression that actually encodes what you know.

---

## Key Takeaways

- The certainty spectrum encodes not just how certain you are but what kind of knowledge you're claiming
- The most-tested distinction: appearance そうだ (ます-stem + そう, direct observation) vs. hearsay そうだ (complete plain form + そうだ, information received)
- かもしれない needs no evidence; らしい needs a source; ようだ needs indirect context; はずだ needs objective facts
- に違いない is formal and emphatic — not for casual speech; use 絶対〜だよ for casual conviction
- つもり is about self-conception / intention, not logical expectation — that's はずだ
