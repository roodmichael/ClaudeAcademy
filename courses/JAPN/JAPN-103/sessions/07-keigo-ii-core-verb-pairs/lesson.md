# Session 07: Keigo II — Core Verb Pairs and Productive Patterns
**Course:** JAPN-103
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to produce the 15 core irregular keigo verb pairs (plain → sonkeigo → kenjōgo) correctly, apply the productive お〜になる and お〜いたす patterns to unlisted verbs, and avoid the most common mixing errors.

---

## Opening

You understand the keigo system from Session 06. Now comes the practical part — the part that actually requires memorization. Japanese has about 15 irregular keigo verbs where the entire verb changes form (like English "go → went"). These are not predictable from any rule. They have to be learned as pairs.

The good news: once you know these 15 pairs, you can handle the vast majority of keigo situations you'll encounter at N3 level. And for any verb not on the list, there's a productive pattern that works reliably.

---

## The 15 Core Irregular Keigo Verbs

Study this table systematically. The structure of each column matters as much as the content.

| Plain Form | Meaning | Teineigo | Sonkeigo (elevating OTHERS) | Kenjōgo (humbling SELF) |
|-----------|---------|----------|----------------------------|------------------------|
| いる | to be/exist | います | いらっしゃる | おる / おります |
| 行く | to go | 行きます | いらっしゃる / おいでになる | 参る / 参ります |
| 来る | to come | 来ます | いらっしゃる / おいでになる | 参る / 参ります |
| 言う | to say | 言います | おっしゃる | 申す / 申します / 申し上げる |
| する | to do | します | なさる | いたす / いたします |
| 食べる | to eat | 食べます | 召し上がる | いただく / いただきます |
| 飲む | to drink | 飲みます | 召し上がる | いただく / いただきます |
| もらう | to receive | もらいます | — | いただく / いただきます |
| あげる | to give (to others) | あげます | — | 差し上げる |

## Four Critical Overlaps to Memorize

### 1. いらっしゃる — Triple Duty Sonkeigo

**いらっしゃる** is the sonkeigo form for three different verbs: いる, 行く, and 来る. Context always disambiguates:

- 社長はいらっしゃいますか。(Is the president here? — sonkeigo of いる)
- 先生はどちらにいらっしゃいますか。(Where has the teacher gone? — sonkeigo of 行く)
- 田中様がいらっしゃいました。(Tanaka-sama has arrived. — sonkeigo of 来る)

The verb form is identical. The situation tells you which action is being elevated.

### 2. 参る — Double Duty Kenjōgo

**参る** is the kenjōgo form for both 行く (to go) and 来る (to come). Again, context disambiguates:

- 明日伺います / 明日参ります。(I will visit / I will come tomorrow. — kenjōgo of 行く/来る)
- 只今参ります。(I'll be right there / I'm coming now. — kenjōgo of 来る)

### 3. いただく — Three Meanings

**いただく** is the kenjōgo form for two different verbs AND a mealtime expression:

- いただく as "to eat/drink" (kenjōgo of 食べる/飲む): いただきます before meals
- いただく as "to receive" (kenjōgo of もらう): プレゼントをいただきました
- いただく in request form: ～ていただけますか (May I ask you to do X?) — kenjōgo construction for requesting someone's action

### 4. 伺う — Double Duty Kenjōgo

**伺う** serves as kenjōgo for both 聞く/尋ねる (to ask) and 訪問する (to visit):

- 明日お伺いします。(I will visit tomorrow.)
- 少し伺ってもよろしいですか。(May I ask you something?)

## The Productive Patterns

For verbs not on the irregular list, two productive patterns generate keigo forms:

### Sonkeigo: お + [ます stem] + になる

- 使う → お使いになる (to use — respectful)
- 読む → お読みになる (to read — respectful)
- 書く → お書きになる (to write — respectful)

**Important:** Do not use this pattern with the irregular sonkeigo verbs above (don't say お見になる when ご覧になる is the correct form).

### Kenjōgo: お + [ます stem] + いたす / する

- 届ける → お届けいたします (I will deliver.)
- 送る → お送りいたします (I will send it.)
- 電話する → お電話いたします / ご連絡いたします (I will call you.)

### The お vs. ご Rule

This trips up many learners:

- **お** (o) — native Japanese words (和語, wago): お名前, お電話, お時間
- **ご** (go) — Sino-Japanese/Chinese-origin words (漢語, kango): ご住所, ご連絡, ご説明

When in doubt, check the word's origin. Common exceptions you'll encounter: お電話 is standard despite 電話 being kango (the exception is well-established).

## The Most Common Mixing Error

The single most consequential keigo error is mixing the direction — using sonkeigo for yourself or kenjōgo for others:

**Wrong:** 私が召し上がりました。(I ate — using sonkeigo for yourself. Sounds bizarre.)
**Right:** 私がいただきました。(I ate — kenjōgo for yourself.)

**Wrong:** 田中部長が参られました。(Section Chief Tanaka came — kenjōgo for a superior. Sounds insulting.)
**Right:** 田中部長がいらっしゃいました。(Section Chief Tanaka came — sonkeigo for superior.)

The drill: **Before using any keigo verb, ask "whose action is this?" If it's yours → kenjōgo. If it's theirs → sonkeigo.**

## Keigo in Practice: Example Scenarios

**Scenario 1: You call a company and ask if the person you're looking for is available.**

山田様はいらっしゃいますか。(Is Yamada-sama available? — sonkeigo for their presence)

**Scenario 2: You leave a message saying you'll call back.**

後ほどまたお電話いたします。(I will call again later — kenjōgo お〜いたします for your own action)

**Scenario 3: You offer to show someone something.**

こちらをご覧ください。(Please look at this — sonkeigo: ご覧 for their seeing)

**Scenario 4: You receive something from a customer.**

ありがとうございます。確かにいただきました。(Thank you. I have received it. — kenjōgo for your own receiving)

## バイト敬語 — The Disputed Service Register

You will hear this in convenience stores and restaurants: ご注文はXXになります (Your order is XX). Prescriptivists call this incorrect — になる implies a process of becoming, not a statement of fact. But this "part-time worker keigo" (バイト敬語) is so pervasive in service Japanese that even the Agency for Cultural Affairs has acknowledged it as a recognized usage conflict rather than simply wrong.

For your own production: don't use it. For recognition: you'll hear it everywhere.

## Why This Matters

The verb table in this session is one of the few things in conversational Japanese that requires rote memorization. These forms cannot be derived from any rule — they are historical irregular verb substitutions. The good news is the set is finite and stable. Once you can produce these 15 pairs correctly and apply the productive patterns, you have the mechanical foundation to navigate keigo in most professional situations at N3 level.

---

## Key Takeaways

- いらっしゃる is sonkeigo for いる, 行く, and 来る — context determines which
- 参る is kenjōgo for both 行く and 来る
- いただく has three distinct uses: receiving, eating/drinking (kenjōgo), and the request construction ～ていただく
- The direction rule: sonkeigo for others' actions; kenjōgo for your own — never reversed
- For unlisted verbs: お〜になる (sonkeigo) and お〜いたす (kenjōgo) are the productive patterns
- お (native words) vs. ご (Chinese-origin words) is the prefix rule, with exceptions
