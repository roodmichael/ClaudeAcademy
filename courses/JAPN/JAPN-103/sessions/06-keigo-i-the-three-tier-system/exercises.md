# Session 06 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: Register Identification
**Type:** Analysis / Written

Identify the keigo tier of each underlined verb/expression as: (A) teineigo, (B) sonkeigo, (C) kenjōgo, or (D) plain form.

1. 先生は今日いらっしゃいますか。
2. 明日また来ます。
3. では、私が参ります。
4. 田中様はもうご覧になりましたか。
5. 少々お待ちください。（お待ちください — whose action is this referring to? What tier is it from?）
6. 私どもはご説明いたします。

**What to observe:** In sentence 6, 私ども is the humble/collective "we" (used in business Japanese). Does the kenjōgo marker on the verb match the subject?

## Exercise 2: Uchi/Soto Sorting
**Type:** Analysis / Written

In each scenario, decide: should you use sonkeigo or kenjōgo for the person/action described? Write the keigo principle being applied.

1. A client calls your company. You need to say "My boss (部長 Bucho) will come to the meeting."
   - Tier: ___ . Why?

2. In a meeting, your boss Yamamoto-san says "Our department (our uchi group) will deliver the report by Friday." How does Yamamoto-san refer to himself/the department's action?
   - Tier for department's action: ___ . Why?

3. You are introducing yourself to a new client: "I will visit your company tomorrow."
   - Tier for your own action: ___ . Why?

4. You want to say "Has the customer (お客様) already eaten?" to a colleague.
   - Tier for お客様's action: ___ . Why?

## Exercise 3: Error Spotting
**Type:** Analysis / Written

Each sentence below contains a keigo usage error. Identify the error and rewrite the sentence correctly.

1. 私がいらっしゃいました。(I arrived.)
2. 田中先生が申しております。(Professor Tanaka is saying/speaking.) [Caller telling someone Tanaka is on the phone]
3. お客様、食べていただきますか。(Will you eat, customer?) [Inviting a customer to eat at an event]
4. 課長が参られました。(The section chief arrived.) [Said to a client]

## Exercise 4: Context Map
**Type:** Thought Experiment / Written

For each situation, decide which keigo level is appropriate and why. There may be more than one defensible answer — explain your reasoning.

1. First day at a new job; a new colleague about the same age greets you in the hallway.
2. You call a company to make an inquiry.
3. Your boss's boss (部長) sends you an email.
4. You are shopping at a department store and a staff member assists you.
5. You are meeting your Japanese girlfriend's parents for the first time.
6. After a month at the same job, you and your colleagues have become quite friendly.

## Exercise 5: Oral Production
**Type:** Speaking

Say each sentence pair out loud. The first is plain/polite form; the second uses appropriate keigo for the context indicated. Notice how the social weight of the sentence changes.

1. A: 先生、今日来ますか。→ B (sonkeigo): 先生、今日いらっしゃいますか。
2. A: 私は明日行きます。→ B (kenjōgo): 私は明日参ります。
3. A: 見ましたか？(informal) → B (sonkeigo): ご覧になりましたか？
4. A: 受け取りました。(plain) → B (kenjōgo): いただきました。

**What to observe:** The kenjōgo forms in sentences 2 and 4 — do they feel natural yet? If not, return to these before Session 07.
