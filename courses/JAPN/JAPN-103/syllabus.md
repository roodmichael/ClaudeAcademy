# JAPN-103: Japanese Language — N4 to N3 Conversation and Natural Speech

## Course Description

You have already done the hard work: N3 grammar patterns from JAPN-101, and N3 vocabulary and kanji from JAPN-102. But there is a specific frustration that comes after that foundation is in place — you pick up a podcast, watch a drama, or talk with a native speaker, and it sounds like a different language. That gap is real, and it has a name: the gap between textbook Japanese and natural spoken Japanese. This course closes it. Students train the skills that written study cannot give you — casual speech contractions, sentence-final particles, keigo, listening at speed, and the evidential vocabulary that encodes how a speaker knows what they know.

## Prerequisites

- JAPN-102: Japanese Language — N4 to N3 Vocabulary and Kanji (required)
- JAPN-101: Japanese Language — N3 Grammar Patterns (indirect prerequisite via JAPN-102)

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

- Recognize and produce the core casual speech contractions (てる, とく, ちゃう, んだ, じゃない, なきゃ) in appropriate contexts without thinking about them
- Use sentence-final particles (ね, よ, よね, な, かな, ぞ, ぜ, わ, さ, し, もん) with correct register and gender awareness
- Deploy discourse markers and filler words (えーと, あの, まあ, なんか) and aizuchi naturally to manage conversation flow
- Navigate the three-tier keigo system — teineigo, sonkeigo, kenjōgo — and produce the 15 core irregular keigo verb pairs correctly
- Hear and parse natural Japanese speech at 5–7 mora per second using shadowing and intensive listening techniques
- Recognize connected speech phenomena (vowel devoicing, mora compression, sentence-final truncation) so familiar vocabulary is not lost at natural speed
- Choose the correct evidential expression from the certainty spectrum: かもしれない, らしい, ようだ, みたいだ, そうだ (appearance vs. hearsay), はずだ, に違いない
- Use softeners (ちょっと, なんか, まあ), discovery expressions (なるほど, へえ), and indirect refusal strategies as fluent conversation management

## Required Materials

**For shadowing practice (Sessions 8–9 and ongoing):**
- Audio source at N3 level — recommended: NHK Web Easy audio articles (free, nhk.or.jp/lesson/), Nihongo con Teppei for Beginners (podcast, episodes ~5 min), or Let's Talk in Japanese (podcast)
- Playback app that supports variable speed: VLC (free), Language Reactor (browser extension for Netflix), or Audacity (free)

**For keigo study (Sessions 6–7):**
- Access to jisho.org (free — look up formal verb forms)
- Optional but excellent: *Japanese for Busy People* II (AJALT) — covers workplace keigo scripts in depth

**For broader reference:**
- *Making Sense of Japanese* by Jay Rubin (short, excellent — clarifies what textbooks get wrong)
- *All About Particles* by Naoko Chino (reference for particle nuance)
- A dictionary that includes pitch accent data: jisho.org shows pitch accent for many entries; the Kenkyusha dictionary (paid) is the authoritative reference

**What you do NOT need:**
- Any new grammar reference — JAPN-101 covered all N3 grammar patterns already
- New kanji or vocabulary materials — JAPN-102 covered those

## Assessment Plan

- **Session quizzes:** After each session — 4–6 questions, low-stakes, immediate feedback
- **Midterm:** After Session 06 — a structured conversation audit (written) where you analyze a real Japanese dialogue for register, particle choices, and contraction use
- **Final project:** A recorded or written multi-turn conversation in two registers (casual and polite) demonstrating mastery of the full course skill set

## A Note on Pacing

This course is more about training than studying. Vocabulary and grammar courses reward careful reading and review; conversation courses reward repetition and practice volume. For Sessions 1–5 (contractions and particles), do the exercises out loud, not in your head — say the contracted forms until they feel automatic, not until you understand them. For Sessions 8–9 (shadowing), ten minutes of daily shadowing beats a two-hour weekend session every time; build the habit before the midterm. For Sessions 10–11 (uncertainty expressions), the key is choosing among similar options — if you find yourself writing らしい where you mean ようだ, the quiz will flag it. Use the Tutor to practice saying complete sentences with each expression, not just to learn the definitions.
