# JAPN-101: Japanese Language — N3 Grammar Patterns

## Course Description

This course bridges JLPT N4 and N3 competence by systematically teaching the grammar patterns that make Japanese sentences feel natural and specific rather than bare and approximate. Students arriving with solid N4 foundations — basic te-form, masu/desu, particles, plain form — will leave able to express aspect, agency, probability, causation, and register through precise grammatical choice. The course covers te-form extensions, all four conditional forms, passive and causative constructions, noun modification, and the clause connectors that produce fluent multi-clause speech.

## Prerequisites

JAPN-100 or demonstrated JLPT N4 proficiency. Students must be comfortable with: te-form formation, plain form conjugation for all verb classes, basic particle use (は, が, を, に, で, と), and masu/desu politeness forms. Students who struggle with these will find Session 1 difficult.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:
- Use ている, てある, てしまう, ておく, and てみる to express aspect precisely
- Choose correctly among てあげる, てくれる, and てもらう based on perspective and social relationship
- Apply all four conditional forms (と, ば, たら, なら) in appropriate contexts and avoid cross-form substitution errors
- Form passive, causative, and causative-passive constructions across all verb classes
- Disambiguate the four meanings of られる using context and particle cues
- Build Japanese relative clauses to modify nouns with full embedded predicates
- Use こと and の as nominalizers with correct context-dependent selection
- Connect clauses naturally using から/ので, が/けど, のに, たり〜たり, and ながら with appropriate register and emotional nuance

## Required Materials

- A Japanese dictionary with example sentences: [Jisho.org](https://jisho.org) (free, excellent)
- Reference grammar: *A Dictionary of Basic Japanese Grammar* by Shibatani & Makino (Tier 4 reference — not required, but highly recommended for deeper explanations)
- Practice tool: [Bunpro.jp](https://bunpro.jp) — grammar SRS platform; a free account supports all grammar points in this course
- A notebook for handwriting practice; working through conjugation tables by hand is more effective than typing them

## Schedule

| Session | Title | Key Concept |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| 01 | ている and てある — Aspect and Deliberate State | Ongoing action vs. resultant state; deliberate vs. natural state |
| 02 | てしまう, ておく, てみる — Completion, Preparation, Trial | Aspect extensions: finality, preparation, experimentation |
| 03 | てあげる, てくれる, てもらう — Giving and Receiving Actions | Action-benefactives; perspective and social direction of favors |
| 04 | Conditionals I — と and ば | Natural consequences and conditional emphasis |
| 05 | Conditionals II — たら and なら | General conditionals and contextual response |
| 06 | Passive Voice — Direct and Adversative | れる/られる passive; adversarial passive (迷惑の受け身) |
| 07 | Causative Voice — Letting and Making | させる/せる; に vs. を for permission vs. compulsion |
| 08 | Causative-Passive and the Four Faces of られる | させられる; disambiguation of passive/potential/spontaneous/respectful |
| 09 | Potential Form and Relative Clauses | できる/られる potential; embedding verb phrases before nouns |
| 10 | Nominalizers — こと and の | Converting verb phrases to noun phrases; when each is required |
| 11 | Connecting Clauses — から/ので, が/けど, のに, たり, ながら | Reason, contrast, listing, and simultaneous action connectors |

## Assessment Plan

- **Session quizzes:** After each session — 5 questions mixing multiple choice, fill-in, and application. Low stakes. Use quiz scores to identify what to review before the next session.
- **Midterm:** After Session 06 — an applied translation and construction exercise covering te-form extensions, all four conditionals, and direct passive. You will be given scenarios and must produce Japanese sentences using the correct forms with explanations of your choices.
- **Final project:** After Session 11 — a short written composition (two paragraphs, ~200 characters) on a topic of your choice, followed by a self-analysis where you identify every grammar pattern used and explain why you chose it. The goal is not just to produce correct Japanese but to demonstrate that you know *why* each form is the right one.

## A Note on Pacing

This is a grammar-intensive course at the intermediate level. Expect each session to take 60-90 minutes the first time through, plus 20-30 minutes of practice with the exercises. Do not rush the early sessions — ている and てある appear constantly in natural Japanese, and any shakiness here will compound in later sessions. If a session's quiz comes back with more than two errors, revisit the lesson before proceeding. The Tutor is available between sessions for any grammar point that isn't clicking. Sessions 04 and 05 (the four conditionals) are the most nuanced material in the course; budget extra time, and return to them after Session 11 to see how much more natural they feel once you have the full picture.
