# Midterm Assessment — JAPN-101
**Covers:** Sessions 01–06

## Overview

This midterm tests your command of the grammar patterns from the first half of JAPN-101: the full te-form extension family (Sessions 01-03), all four conditional forms (Sessions 04-05), and direct and adversative passive voice (Session 06). Rather than isolated drill questions, this assessment presents realistic scenarios and asks you to construct, transform, and explain sentences — demonstrating that you understand *why* each form works, not just *what* it looks like.

There is no time limit. Work through each section carefully. Submit your responses to the Tutor for evaluation.

---

## The Task

### Part 1: Te-Form Extensions in Context (Sessions 01-03)

**Scenario:** You planned a big dinner for your friend's birthday tonight. Read this account and answer the questions that follow.

> Today was exhausting. I prepared everything in advance — I bought ingredients at the market and set them out this morning. I accidentally burned the vegetables while I was checking email. My friend tried the soup and said it was actually good! I also made a cake yesterday and it's been in the refrigerator waiting.

**Task 1A:** Write this account in Japanese using the correct te-form constructions. Aim for 4-6 sentences. Target constructions:
- ておく (prepared in advance)
- てしまう (accidentally burned)
- てみる (tried the soup)
- てある (the cake waiting in the fridge)
- ている or てある for the state of the ingredients

**Task 1B:** In English, explain your choice of てある vs. ている for the cake in the refrigerator. Why is てある more precise than ている here?

**Task 1C:** Write two sentences using てくれる and てもらう to describe how a friend helped you during the dinner preparation. They are different sentences describing the same helpful act — explain the perspective difference.

### Part 2: Conditional Forms (Sessions 04-05)

**Task 2A:** Complete each sentence with the most appropriate conditional (と, ば, たら, なら). You may use each more than once. Explain your choice for at least three of your answers.

1. この薬を飲む\_\_\_、よく効くと思います。(If you take this medicine, I think it will work well.)
2. 春に\_\_\_、桜が咲く。(When spring comes, the cherry blossoms bloom.)
3. もし宝くじに当たっ\_\_\_、世界旅行をしたい。(If I won the lottery, I'd want to travel the world.)
4. 大阪に行く\_\_\_、たこ焼きを食べてみて。(If you're going to Osaka [as mentioned], try the takoyaki.)
5. 一生懸命練習すれ\_\_\_、必ず上手になる。→ Rewrite this correctly if there is an issue.
6. 疲れ\_\_\_、早く寝た方がいいよ。(Advice: If you're tired, you should go to bed early.)

**Task 2B:** Write a short dialogue (4-6 lines) between two people discussing travel plans. Use all four conditionals naturally — label each one.

### Part 3: Passive Voice (Session 06)

**Task 3A:** Transform these active sentences into passive:

1. 田中監督がその映画を撮った。(Director Tanaka filmed that movie.) — Use によって
2. 上司が私に残業を頼んだ。(My boss asked me to work overtime.)
3. 隣の人が夜中に音楽をかけた。(My neighbor played music in the middle of the night.) → Write this as an adversative passive from your perspective.

**Task 3B:** Identify whether each sentence uses direct passive (D) or adversative passive (A):

1. 昨日、財布を盗まれた。
2. 子供に泣かれて、眠れなかった。
3. 先生に論文を褒められた。
4. 電車で隣の人に話しかけられた。(The person next to me talked to me — was it pleasant or unpleasant? Can you tell from grammar alone?)

## Evaluation Criteria

- **Grammatical accuracy:** Are te-form constructions, conditionals, and passive formations correct?
- **Semantic precision:** Is the chosen form the right one for the meaning (e.g., てある vs. ている, たら vs. と, adversative vs. direct passive)?
- **Register awareness:** Are politeness levels consistent within each piece? Does the choice of connector (ので vs. から) match the social context?
- **Explanation quality:** Can you articulate *why* you chose each form, or are you guessing? The explanations matter as much as the sentences.
- **Completeness:** All parts submitted; the dialogue uses all four conditionals.

## Tutor Scoring Guide

**Strong responses will:**
- Use てある correctly for the cake (deliberately placed, agent implied) rather than ている (which would only describe its state without the deliberate implication)
- In Part 2A, correctly identify: と for the natural blooming phenomenon (spring → blossoms), たら for the lottery hypothetical (volitional desire follows), なら for the Osaka advice (responding to stated plans), たら or ば for the medicine advice (condition-focused)
- Sentence #5 has ば with same-subject sequential volitional actions — the student should note this and offer たら as the correction
- Correctly classify sentence #4 in Part 3B as direct passive (physically addressed by neighbor) and discuss that the adversative/pleasant distinction requires context — the grammar alone does not encode valence

**Common weaknesses to watch for:**
- Confusing てある (transitive + deliberate) with ている (state only) for the cake
- Using と where たら is needed (volitional desire after the conditional)
- Missing the difference in Part 1C: くれる takes the friend as subject; もらう takes the speaker as subject
- Failing to note the ば problem in Part 2A sentence #5

**Feedback approach:** Correct errors with explanation of the rule. Celebrate any sentence that correctly produces adversative passive or navigates the くれる/もらう distinction — these are genuine N3 achievements.
