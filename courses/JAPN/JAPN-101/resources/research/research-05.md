**Course:** JAPN-101
**Level:** 101
**Date:** 2026-06-24
**Sources:** 9 (Tier 4: 0 | Tier 5: 9)

---

## Core Concepts

This research area covers the clause-connecting particles and patterns that allow a speaker to build multi-clause sentences — giving reasons, expressing contrast, describing simultaneous or interleaved actions. These are the "glue" of natural Japanese discourse.

The five connectors covered here fall into three functional groups:

**Reason/cause:** から (because — subjective), ので (because — objective/formal)  
**Contrast/adversative:** が/けど (but — neutral), のに (but — with surprise or complaint)  
**Action patterns:** たり〜たりする (doing things like X and Y), ながら (while doing X)

Beyond their basic translations, each connector carries pragmatic weight — formality level, emotional register, and constraints on what can follow. A learner who masters these connectors can produce sentences that feel natural rather than mechanical.

---

## Key Definitions

**から (kara):** Clause connector meaning "because" or "so." Attaches to the plain or polite form of the preceding clause. Subjective and assertive — the speaker is stating their reason directly and confidently. More casual, emotional, or personal in tone.

**ので (node):** Clause connector meaning "because" or "so." Attaches to plain form + の (and adds な for na-adjectives/nouns). More formal, objective, and indirect than から. The reason feels external or circumstantial rather than personal assertion.

**が (ga) / けど (kedo) / けれど (keredo) / けれども (keredomo):** Adversative conjunctions meaning "but" or "however." All have identical core meaning; they differ only in formality level. Neutral emotional tone — no complaint or surprise implied.

**のに (noni):** Adversative conjunction meaning "despite" or "even though." Always expresses surprise, disappointment, frustration, or complaint. Used only with actual (not hypothetical) conditions. Cannot precede volitional expressions.

**たり〜たりする:** A pattern for listing representative examples of actions without implying exhaustiveness or a fixed order. "Do things like X and Y." The final たり is followed by a form of する.

**ながら (nagara):** Simultaneous action marker — "while doing X." Both actions share the same subject. The first action (before ながら) is subordinate; the second (after ながら) is the main or primary action.

## Process / Mechanics

### から vs. ので: Cause and Reason

**Formation:**

| Preceding element | から | ので |
|-------------------|------|------|
| Verb (plain) | 行くから | 行くので |
| Verb (polite) | 行きますから ✅ | 行きますので ✅ (uncommon but acceptable) |
| い-adjective | 忙しいから | 忙しいので |
| な-adjective | 元気だから | 元気なので |
| Noun | 学生だから | 学生なので |

Key: ので requires な between a na-adjective/noun and ので (same as の nominalizer). から uses だ as normal.

**Pragmatic distinction:**

| Feature | から | ので |
|---------|------|------|
| Register | Casual, conversational | Formal, written, business |
| Emotional stance | Speaker asserts their reason confidently | Speaker presents reason as objective circumstance |
| Social reading | Can sound selfish/assertive if overused in requests | Sounds considerate, apologetic, tactful |
| With requests | Possible but can seem demanding | Preferred — sounds more polite |

**Business/polite context example:**
- ❌ (less appropriate) 用事があるから早退します。 (reason-focused, assertive)
- ✅ (more appropriate) 用事があるので早退します。 (circumstance-focused, deferential)

**What only から can do:**
1. Follow probability expressions (だろう/でしょう):
   - 雨が降るでしょう**から**、傘を持って行って (Because it will probably rain, take an umbrella)
   - ❌ 雨が降るでしょう**ので**
2. Function in the emphasis pattern `〜のは〜からだ`:
   - 成功したのは努力した**からだ** (The reason for success is because I worked hard)
   - ❌ 〜ので だ pattern does not exist
3. Answer "why" questions (なぜ〜ですか):
   - なぜですか → 〜からです (Because...)
   - ❌ 〜のでです (incorrect)
4. Omit the conclusion more naturally:
   - 遅くなる**から**。(Because I'll be late [so don't wait up — implied]) — casual self-assertion
   - のでの can do this too, but から feels more natural with emotional/casual conclusions

### が / けど / けれど / けれども: Neutral Contrast

**Formation:** Attach directly to plain or polite form of the preceding clause. No special particle needed before が/けど even for na-adjectives:
- 元気**だけど** (but I'm fine)
- 学生**だが** (but I'm a student)

**Formality hierarchy:**
- けれども → (most formal/written)
- けれど → (formal)
- が → (neutral/literary; common in written prose)
- けど → (casual, most common in everyday speech)

**Two functions:**

1. **Contrast:** Second clause contradicts or qualifies the first
   - 綺麗だが、バラには棘がある (Beautiful, but roses have thorns)
   - このレストランは高いけど、美味しい (This restaurant is expensive but delicious)

2. **Preliminary remark (softener):** Sets up context before a request, question, or negative information — makes the transition feel less abrupt
   - 来週**だけど**、一緒に行きませんか (About next week — want to come with me?)
   - すみません**が**、ちょっといいですか (Excuse me, but do you have a moment?)

Unlike のに, が/けど can precede any type of expression including volitional, requests, and invitations.

### のに: Emotional Contrast

**Formation:**
- Verb plain form + のに: 行くのに、〜
- い-adjective + のに: 忙しいのに、〜
- な-adjective + な + のに: 元気なのに、〜
- Noun + な + のに: 学生なのに、〜

Note: な is required before のに for na-adjectives and nouns — unlike が/けど which do not require な.

**Core meaning:** The second clause contradicts what the first clause would naturally lead us to expect, and the speaker is surprised, disappointed, or frustrated by this.

- 疲れているのに寝れない (I can't sleep despite being tired — frustration)
- 安いのに美味しい (It's delicious despite being cheap — positive surprise)
- 一生懸命勉強したのに試験に落ちた (I failed the exam despite studying hard — complaint/disappointment)

**Constraints on のに:**
- Cannot precede affirmative volitional: ❌ 高いのに買おう → ✅ 高いけど買おう
- Cannot precede requests/invitations: ❌ 高いのに買ってください → ✅ 高いけど買ってください
- Exception: negative commands are acceptable: 高いのに買わないで (Don't buy it even though it's expensive)
- The condition expressed in the first clause must be actual/real, not hypothetical

**Sentence-initial variant:**
- なのに (at sentence start) = "And yet..." / "Even so..." — often expresses exasperation
- それなのに = more formal/written version

**Comparison with related forms:**
- けど expresses contrast without emotional coloring
- くせに (JLPT N3) expresses a similar contrast but with criticism/accusation toward the subject
- ものの (formal written) expresses contrast without emotional coloring, similar to が

### たり〜たりする: Non-Exhaustive Listing

**Formation:** `[verb ta-form] + り + ... + [verb ta-form] + り + する/します`

The particle り attaches to the ta-form. When the ta-form ends in だ (e.g., 読んだ), the particle becomes だり:
- 読む (ta-form: 読んだ) → 読んだり
- 歌う (ta-form: 歌った) → 歌ったり

The final する conjugates for tense and politeness: した (past), します (polite present), している (doing now).

**Core nuance:** The speaker lists representative examples of a broader set — the listener understands that other actions also occur. This contrasts with て-form chaining, which lists all actions sequentially and exhaustively.

- て-form: 勉強して、ごはんを食べて、シャワーを浴びた (I studied, ate, and showered — complete sequence)
- たり〜たり: 勉強したり、ごはんを食べたりした (I did things like study and eat — non-exhaustive, no fixed order)

**Single-item たり:**
たり can be used with just one verb when the speaker wants to convey "things like X":
- 映画を見たりします (I do things like watch movies)

**Alternating states (oscillation):**
たり〜たり can also describe conditions that alternate:
- 雨が降ったり、止んだりする (It keeps raining and stopping — back and forth)
- 元気だったり、疲れたりする (Sometimes I'm fine, sometimes I'm tired)

**Comparison with や (noun listing):**
- や lists nouns non-exhaustively: りんごやバナナを買った (I bought things like apples and bananas)
- たり〜たり lists verbs/adjectives non-exhaustively — cannot use for nouns alone

### ながら: Simultaneous Actions

**Formation:** `[verb ます-stem] + ながら`
- 聞く → 聞き + ながら → 聞きながら
- 食べる → 食べ + ながら → 食べながら
- 歩く → 歩き + ながら → 歩きながら

**Core rules:**
1. **Same subject required:** Both actions must have the same performer
   - ❌ 私が歩きながら、犬が走った (Different subjects — ungrammatical)
   - ✅ 私は音楽を聴きながら歩いた (I walked while listening to music — same subject)

2. **Action A is subordinate to Action B:** The action before ながら is the background/simultaneous context; the action after ながら is the main focus
   - 音楽を聴きながら勉強する (Study while listening to music — studying is the main activity)

3. **Both actions must be ongoing simultaneously:** ながら implies two actions truly overlap in time
   - ✅ テレビを見ながら食べる (Eat while watching TV — simultaneous)
   - ❌ 学校に着いてながら授業が始まった (Arrival is instantaneous — cannot be ながら background)

**Distinguishing ながら from similar patterns:**

| Pattern | Core difference | Example |
|---------|----------------|---------|
| ながら | True simultaneous actions, same subject | 歌いながら踊る (dance while singing) |
| ているところだ | Emphasizes being in the middle of one action | 今食べているところだ (I'm in the middle of eating) |
| 中 (ちゅう/なか) | Attaches to nouns; no subject restriction | 会議中に電話した (called during the meeting) |
| がてら | Second action is incidental/additional to the first | 散歩がてらコンビニに寄った (stopped at convenience store incidentally while walking) |

## Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Using から in formal requests:** Sounds self-centered. 用事があるから来週休みます (to a supervisor) → Better: 用事があるので来週休みます.

2. **のに with volitional:** ❌ 高いのに買おう. のに cannot precede plans, desires, or invitations. Use けど instead.

3. **な before のに — forgetting it:** ❌ 元気のに → ✅ 元気**な**のに. This follows the same pattern as な-adjective nominalization.

4. **Mixed subjects with ながら:** ❌ 先生が説明しながら、学生がメモを取った. Both subjects must be the same for ながら.

5. **たり without the closing する:** Learners sometimes end a sentence after the last たり without する: ❌ 映画を見たり、音楽を聴いたり。→ ✅ 映画を見たり、音楽を聴いたりした。(The する must close the construction and be conjugated for tense.)

6. **たり with only one item when sequence is intended:** Using たり〜たり to describe actions in a specific sequence produces ambiguity, since たり implies non-ordered listing. Use て-form for strict sequences.

7. **Confusing ので and のに:** Both involve の before で/に, but ので = reason ("because") and のに = contrast despite ("even though"). Students confuse them in reading and writing. Mnemonic: のに contains に which implies "toward/against" — you're going *against* expectations.

- このカフェは遠い\_\_\_、よく行く。(neutral contrast — just a fact)

## Expert Debates / Open Questions

**から vs. ので — is the formality distinction real?**  
Some linguists argue the から/ので distinction is more about assertiveness vs. deference than simple formality. Research by discourse analysts suggests that ので is chosen even in casual speech when a speaker wants to seem considerate, not merely in formal contexts. The practical effect for learners: err toward ので in any situation involving a request or explanation to someone of higher status.

**のに and subjectivity:**  
のに clearly encodes speaker attitude (surprise, complaint). Some grammarians debate whether the positive-surprise use (安いのに美味しい) genuinely involves complaint/frustration, or whether のに simply marks unexpected contrast and emotion is added contextually. The Wasabi/Bunpro treatment treats both positive and negative surprise as valid.

**ながら with instantaneous verbs:**  
The constraint that ながら requires ongoing (durative) actions raises edge cases. Native speakers sometimes use expressions like 着くなり (upon arriving) or 気づきながら (while being aware) that blur the line. The safe rule for N4-N3: only use ながら with actions that can genuinely overlap in time.

**One-item たり:**  
Using たり with a single verb (映画を見たりします) is sometimes questioned in prescriptive grammar contexts since たり historically implied alternation or listing. In contemporary Japanese, single-item たり is widely accepted and common.
