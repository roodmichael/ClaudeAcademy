## Core Concepts

Japanese has a three-way system of verb derivations that English speakers find particularly challenging: passive, causative, and causative-passive. Each is formed by attaching a specific auxiliary to the verb base, and each shifts who performs the action, who is affected, and who has control.

- **Passive (受け身):** The grammatical subject receives an action performed by someone else
- **Causative (使役):** The grammatical subject causes or allows someone else to perform an action
- **Causative-passive (使役受け身):** The grammatical subject is made/forced to perform an action by someone else

A critical complication: られる serves four distinct roles in Japanese (passive, potential, spontaneous, and respectful). Disambiguation requires context.

---

## Key Definitions

**Passive (受け身, うけみ):** A grammatical construction where the person or thing receiving an action becomes the grammatical subject. Agent (doer) is optionally marked with に, から, or によって. Can express direct passive (action physically done to subject) or indirect/adversative passive (subject is negatively affected by an action done nearby or to something close to them).

**Direct passive:** The subject is the physical target of the action.
- お母さんに褒められた (I was praised by my mother)

**Indirect/adversative passive (迷惑の受け身, めいわくのうけみ):** The subject is negatively affected, even though the action was done to something else (or intransitively). Unique to Japanese; has no clean English equivalent.
- 赤ちゃんに泣かれた (I was disturbed by the baby crying — the baby's crying affected me negatively)

**Causative (使役, しえき):** The grammatical subject causes or permits another person to perform an action. Expressed with せる (u-verbs) or させる (ru-verbs and する).

**Causative-passive:** Combines causative + passive. The subject is forced to do something by someone else. Always expresses compulsion, never permission.
- 兄に宿題をさせられた (I was forced to do homework by my older brother)

## Process / Mechanics

### Passive Formation (れる・られる)

**Ru-verbs (ichidan):** Drop る, add られる
- 見る → 見られる (to be seen / can see / is seen by)
- 食べる → 食べられる (to be eaten)

**U-verbs (godan):** Replace final kana with あ-row equivalent, then add れる

| Verb | Base | あ-row | Passive |
|------|------|--------|---------|
| 歌う | 歌 | 歌わ | 歌われる |
| 書く | 書 | 書か | 書かれる |
| 話す | 話 | 話さ | 話される |
| 歩く | 歩 | 歩か | 歩かれる |
| 飲む | 飲 | 飲ま | 飲まれる |
| 打つ | 打 | 打た | 打たれる |

**Irregular:**
- する → される
- 来る → 来られる

**Particle selection for the agent (doer):**

| Particle | When to use | Example |
|----------|------------|---------|
| に | Default agent marker | お父さんに叩かれた (I was slapped by my father) |
| から | When the verb involves transfer and に would be ambiguous (read: both "by" and "for") | 友達からプレゼントをもらわれた |
| によって | When the verb implies creation, invention, or formal attribution — prevents に from being read as "for" | 歌は秋元さんによって書かれた (The song was written by Akimoto) |

Why によって matters: "歌は秋元さんに書かれた" could mean "written for Akimoto" (dative に). によって eliminates that ambiguity.

**The four meanings of られる:**

| Meaning | Japanese Term | Example | Signal |
|---------|--------------|---------|--------|
| Passive | 受け身 | 見られた (was seen) | Agent に present |
| Potential | 可能 | 見られる (can see) | No agent; context of ability |
| Spontaneous | 自発 | 昔が思い出される (the past is recalled) | Feelings/mental states |
| Respectful | 尊敬 | 先生が来られた (the teacher came) | Referent is a superior |

**ら-drop:** In casual speech, younger speakers drop the ら from る-verb potential forms: 見られる → 見れる. This is NOT acceptable for passive, spontaneous, or respectful uses — and causes ambiguity when overused.

### Causative Formation (させる / せる)

**Ru-verbs:** Replace る with させる
- 見る → 見させる; 食べる → 食べさせる

**U-verbs:** Replace final う-sound with あ-row + せる
- 書く → 書かせる; 飲む → 飲ませる; 話す → 話させる

**Irregular:**
- する → させる
- 来る → 来させる

**Letting vs. Making — particle distinction:**

With intransitive verbs, the particle on the person being caused reveals whether permission or compulsion is intended:

| Particle | Nuance | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| に | Letting (person has volition) | 先生は生徒に家へ帰らせた (The teacher let the student go home) |
| を | Making/forcing (doer has no volition implied) | 赤ちゃんを寝させる (I'll get the baby to sleep — compulsion implied) |

With transitive verbs, the pattern is: Subject は person に object を verb-させる
- 社長は社員に残業をさせた (The boss made the employees work overtime)

### Causative-Passive Formation (させられる)

Combines causative (させる) + passive (られる). The causative verb is formed first, then られる is attached.

**Long form (standard):**
- Ru-verbs: 見る → 見させる → 見させられる
- U-verbs: 歌う → 歌わせる → 歌わせられる
- する → させる → させられる

**Short/contracted form (common in speech):**
Only applies to u-verbs (NOT to ru-verbs or irregular verbs):
- 歌わせられる → 歌わされる
- 書かせられる → 書かされる
- 歩かせられる → 歩かされる

Note: する causative-passive (させられる) has no short form. Ru-verb causative-passive cannot be shortened (which would create ambiguity with potential form).

**Sentence pattern:**
Subject は/が + causer に + object を + verb-させられる

- 兄に宿題をさせられた (I was forced to do homework by my older brother)
- 社長に残業をさせられた (I was made to work overtime by the boss)

**Nuance:** Causative-passive always implies unwillingness or compulsion. There is no permissive reading as there is with plain causative. The agent marked with に is the one imposing the action.

## Common Beginner Mistakes

1. **Confusing passive and potential (られる):** 食べられる can mean "can eat" (potential) or "is eaten" (passive). Without an agent (に + person), it defaults to potential in most modern spoken contexts. When writing, context or a preceding に-phrase resolves ambiguity.

2. **Wrong particle choice for passive agent:** Using に when によって is needed (creation/attribution) produces a different meaning. Always check: could に be read as "for"? If yes, use によって or から.

3. **Indirect passive with no English equivalent:** Many English speakers omit the indirect/adversative passive because they look for a direct object being acted upon. In Japanese, 子供に泣かれた is grammatically normal and emotionally natural. English speakers must learn to produce it actively.

4. **Short vs. long causative-passive:** Students attempt to shorten ru-verb causative-passives: ❌ 食べさせられる → ❌ 食べさされる (the short form does not apply to ru-verbs). Only u-verbs have contracted forms.

5. **Using に vs. を in causative:** Students often default to に for all causatives, missing that を signals compulsion with intransitive verbs. This produces sentences that sound like permission when compulsion is meant.

6. **Applying ら-drop to passive:** Because younger speakers drop ら in potential forms, students sometimes produce ❌ 見れた meaning "was seen" — this is incorrect; ら cannot be dropped in passive constructions.

## Hands-On Elements

**Exercise 1 — Passive formation drill:**
Convert these active sentences to passive:
- 先生が学生を褒めた (The teacher praised the student)
- 猫がご飯を食べた (The cat ate the food)
- 田中さんが歌を書いた (Tanaka-san wrote the song) — use によって

**Exercise 2 — Four readings of られる:**
Identify the meaning (passive / potential / spontaneous / respectful) in context:
- 先生が来られました。
- この問題は難しくて解かれません。
- 昔のことが思い出される。
- 私のケーキが食べられた！

**Exercise 3 — Causative scenarios:**
Describe each situation using causative (させる):
- A parent who makes their child eat vegetables
- A teacher who allows students to leave early
- A boss who forces employees to attend a meeting

**Exercise 4 — Causative-passive:**
Transform the causative sentences from Exercise 3 into causative-passive from the perspective of the person being made to do the action.

**Exercise 5 — Particle selection:**
Choose に, から, or によって for each:
- この論文は田中教授\_\_書かれた。(attribution of written work)
- 犬\_\_噛まれた。(I was bitten by a dog)
- 友達\_\_プレゼントをもらった。(received from friend — is passive even needed here?)

## Expert Debates / Open Questions

**Is adversative passive overteaching or undercovering?**  
Wasabi Japanese and Bunpro both present adversative/indirect passive as a core feature, but some textbook series (early Genki editions) introduce it only briefly. Debate exists about whether learners need to produce adversative passive at N4 or only recognize it.

**The ら-drop issue:**  
ら-drop (see 見れる for 見られる) has become extremely common in spoken Japanese to the point that many younger native speakers use it consistently. JLPT testing still marks ら-drop as incorrect in potential contexts. Whether teachers should teach ら-drop as acceptable is contested. The safe guideline: know it exists in speech, avoid it in writing and formal contexts.

**Causative "let" vs. "make" ambiguity:**  
With transitive verbs in causative sentences, the に particle marks the person being caused to act, and the sentence can mean either "let" or "make" depending on context. Some grammarians argue this ambiguity is irresolvable without wider discourse context; others argue pragmatic cues are usually sufficient.
