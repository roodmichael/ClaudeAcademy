# Glossary — JAPN-101

All key terms from all sessions, alphabetized. Where a Japanese term has multiple related English glosses, the most precise translation is given first.

---

**Adversative passive (迷惑の受け身, めいわくのうけみ):** A uniquely Japanese passive construction in which the grammatical subject is negatively affected by an action done by someone else — even if the action was not performed directly on the subject. Can use intransitive verbs. Contrast with direct passive. *Session 06*

**あ-row (ア行):** The first row of the Japanese syllabary (a, i, u, e, o). In u-verb passive and causative formation, the final kana is replaced with its あ-row counterpart before adding れる or せる. *Sessions 06, 07*

**Agent:** The entity that performs the action in a sentence. In passive constructions, the agent is typically marked with に (default), から (transfer), or によって (attribution). *Session 06*

**Aspect:** A grammatical category expressing how an action unfolds in time — whether it is ongoing, completed, resultant, etc. Japanese marks aspect more systematically than English. Contrasts with *tense*. *Session 01*

**Causative (使役, しえき):** A verb derivation expressing that the grammatical subject causes or allows another person to perform an action. Formed with させる (ru-verbs, する, 来る) or せる (u-verbs). *Session 07*

**Causative-passive (使役受け身, しえきうけみ):** A combined derivation expressing that the grammatical subject is forced or compelled to perform an action by someone else. Always expresses compulsion — no permissive reading. Formed by combining causative form + られる. *Session 08*

**Change-of-state verb:** A verb describing an action that happens instantaneously and results in a new state (e.g., 結婚する, 死ぬ, 来る). When combined with ている, these express the resultant state rather than ongoing action. *Session 01*

**Clause connector:** A particle or expression that connects two clauses with a logical, causal, or contrastive relationship. Covered in this course: から, ので, が, けど, のに, たり〜たり, ながら. *Session 11*

**Contextual conditional:** See *なら*. *Session 05*

**Durative verb:** A verb describing an action that takes place over a period of time and can be logically ongoing (e.g., 食べる, 読む, 走る). When combined with ている, these express an ongoing action. Contrast with change-of-state verb. *Session 01*

**え-row (エ行):** The fourth row of the Japanese syllabary (ka→ke, ma→me, etc.). Used in forming u-verb potential forms: replace the final kana with its え-row equivalent and add る (書く → 書ける). *Session 09*

**Gap (relative clause):** The implied grammatical role played by the head noun inside a Japanese relative clause. Not grammatically marked — inferred from context. *Session 09*

**が/けど:** Adversative clause connectors meaning "but" or "however." Neutral emotional tone. Formality ladder: けれども > けれど > が > けど. *Session 11*

**Head noun:** The noun being modified by a relative clause. In Japanese, the head noun comes after the modifying clause. The head noun plays an implied grammatical role inside the embedded clause (the head noun gap). *Session 09*

**Head-final language:** A typological term for languages where modifiers come before the elements they modify. Japanese is head-final: relative clauses come before the noun they modify, not after. *Session 09*

**In-group (内, うち):** The social sphere of the speaker — typically self, close family, and close friends. Relevant to てあげる/くれる/もらう: くれる flows toward the in-group; あげる flows away from it. *Session 03*

**Instantaneous verb:** See *change-of-state verb*. *Session 01*

**から (cause/reason):** A clause connector meaning "because." Attaches to plain or polite form. Assertive, personal, casual. Can follow でしょう/だろう and can anchor のは〜からだ patterns. Contrasts with ので in register and speaker stance. *Session 11*

**こと:** A nominalizer that converts a verb or adjective phrase into a noun phrase. Slightly more formal and abstract than の. Required before だ/です in predicate nominalization, in fixed expressions (ことができる, ことがある, ことにする, etc.), and after thought/knowledge verbs. *Session 10*

**Nominalization:** The process of converting a verb or adjective phrase into a noun phrase using a nominalizer (こと or の in Japanese). *Session 10*

**Nominalizer:** A word that creates a noun phrase from a predicate. Japanese has two: こと and の. *Session 10*

**ながら:** A connector meaning "while doing X." Requires the same subject for both actions; both actions must be durative (genuinely simultaneous). The action before ながら is subordinate; the action after is the main focus. Formation: ます-stem + ながら. *Session 11*

**なら:** A contextual conditional meaning "if that's the case" or "as for X." Requires pre-established context — cannot introduce a hypothetical independently. Often used in conversation to respond to what the other person just said. The result clause can occur before the condition is fully realized. *Session 05*

**のに:** An adversative connector meaning "despite" or "even though," always expressing emotional contrast — surprise, frustration, disappointment, or positive wonder. Requires な before it for na-adjectives/nouns. Cannot precede affirmative volitional expressions or requests. *Session 11*

**の:** (1) As a nominalizer: converts a verb or adjective phrase into a noun phrase. Slightly more conversational and concrete than こと. Required with direct perception verbs (see/hear in real time), with やめる, and with assistance/waiting verbs. (2) As a subject particle inside relative clauses: interchangeable with が. *Sessions 09, 10*

**ので:** A clause connector meaning "because." Attaches to plain form; requires な for na-adjectives and nouns. Objective, deferential, formal. Preferred in requests and explanations to superiors. Cannot follow でしょう/だろう. *Session 11*

**Noun modification (relative clause):** In Japanese, placing a plain-form verb or adjective clause directly before a noun to modify it. No relative pronoun is used. Plain form is required inside the clause; は cannot appear inside the clause. *Session 09*

**Out-group (外, そと):** Anyone outside the speaker's in-group. See also *in-group*. *Session 03*

**Passive (受け身, うけみ):** A verb derivation in which the grammatical subject receives an action performed by someone else. Formed with れる (u-verbs) or られる (ru-verbs, 来る). Agent optionally marked with に, から, or によって. *Session 06*

**Plain form (dictionary form):** The base, non-polite verb form. Required inside relative clauses, conditional clauses, and before nominalizers こと and の. *Sessions 04–10*

**Potential form:** A verb form expressing ability or circumstantial possibility ("can do"). Ru-verbs: drop る + られる. U-verbs: え-row + る. する → できる. Alternative: ことができる (more formal). *Session 09*

**ら-drop:** The colloquial omission of ら from ru-verb potential forms (食べられる → 食べれる). Common in modern spoken Japanese among younger speakers. Not acceptable in formal/written Japanese or on JLPT exams. *Sessions 08, 09*

**Resultant state:** The state of an entity that persists after an action has completed. ている with change-of-state verbs expresses resultant state. てある expresses a resultant state created by deliberate human action. *Session 01*

**Register:** The level of formality and social positioning expressed through language choices. In this course, register distinctions appear in: から vs. ので, けれども vs. けど, てあげる vs. て差し上げる, polite vs. casual contractions. *Sessions 03, 11*

**Spontaneous (自発, じはつ):** One of the four meanings of られる. Expresses a feeling or mental state that arises involuntarily, without a volitional agent. Common verbs: 思い出す, 案じる, 感じる. *Session 08*

**ば:** A conditional form emphasizing the condition clause. Implies the condition is the key variable. Formation: う-verbs → え-row + ば; る-verbs → drop る + れば; い-adj → ければ. Breaks down with same-subject sequential volitional actions. *Session 04*

**たら:** The most versatile Japanese conditional. Formation: ta-form + ら. Handles one-time conditions ("when"), hypotheticals ("if"), and volitional result clauses. The safe default when unsure which conditional to use. Exclusively used in たら〜どうですか (advice) pattern. *Session 05*

**たり〜たりする:** A pattern for listing representative, non-exhaustive actions or alternating states. Formation: ta-form + り + ... + ta-form + り + する (conjugated). Contrasts with te-form chaining, which is exhaustive and sequential. Must close with する. *Session 11*

**て-form:** The conjunctive/connective form of Japanese verbs. Used in all te-form extension constructions covered in this course (ている, てある, てしまう, ておく, てみる, てあげる, てくれる, てもらう). *Sessions 01–03*

**てある:** Te-form of a transitive verb + ある. Expresses a state that was created by a deliberate human action. Contrasts with ている on intransitive verbs, which describes natural states without implying a human agent. *Session 01*

**てあげる:** Te-form + あげる. Performing an action as a favor for someone outside the speaker's in-group. Can sound patronizing toward superiors; use て差し上げる for formal/humble contexts. *Session 03*

**ている:** Te-form + いる. (1) With durative verbs: ongoing action. (2) With change-of-state verbs: resultant state. Colloquial contraction: てる. *Session 01*

**てくれる:** Te-form + くれる. Someone outside the in-group performs an action benefiting the speaker or in-group. Foregrounds the giver's generosity. Subject is the person giving the favor. *Session 03*

**てしまう:** Te-form + しまう. Expresses either (1) complete, definitive completion or (2) regret/accidental action. Colloquial contractions: ちゃう (for て verbs), じゃう (for で verbs). *Session 02*

**てみる:** Te-form + みる. "Try doing X to see what happens." Expresses curiosity or experimentation, not difficulty. Distinct from ようとする (attempting something that might fail). *Session 02*

**てもらう:** Te-form + もらう. The speaker receives a beneficial action from someone. Focuses on what the speaker obtained. Subject is the receiver (speaker). Both に marks the actor. *Session 03*

**ておく:** Te-form + おく. Performing an action in advance for future benefit or as preparation. Emphasizes the preparatory act itself. Colloquial contraction: とく. *Session 02*

**と (conditional):** A conditional form expressing automatic, natural, or habitual results. Formation: plain form + と. Cannot precede volitional expressions, requests, or invitations. Cannot pair with もし. Used for scientific facts, instructions, and "and then" narrative. *Session 04*

**Transitive verb:** A verb that takes a direct object marked with を. Required for てある (the object is the thing that exists in a deliberate state). Contrasts with intransitive verbs, which describe events without a direct object. *Session 01*

**迷惑 (めいわく):** Inconvenience or nuisance caused to others. Culturally significant concept in Japanese social interaction; grammatically encoded in adversative passive (迷惑の受け身). *Session 06*

**もし:** A conditional adverb meaning "if by chance" or "hypothetically speaking." Can precede たら, ば, and なら. Cannot precede と (which implies certainty). *Session 05*

**られる:** A verb suffix with four distinct meanings depending on context: (1) Passive (受け身): receiving an action; (2) Potential (可能): ability to do; (3) Spontaneous (自発): involuntary mental states; (4) Respectful (尊敬): honorific action of a superior. Disambiguation relies on context and particle cues. *Session 08*
