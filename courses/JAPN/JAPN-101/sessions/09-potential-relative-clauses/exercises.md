## Exercise 2: Potential or Automatic Visibility/Audibility?
**Type:** Analysis

Choose the correct verb: conjugated potential form OR the intransitive automatic-perception verb (見える/聞こえる).

1. Can you see the screen from there? (黒板, 見る or 見える)
2. Is the music audible from outside the room? (音楽, 聞く or 聞こえる)
3. I can speak Japanese a little. (日本語, 話す)
4. Can you hear what the teacher is saying? (先生, 聞く or 聞こえる) — *Are these different?*
5. This seafood can be eaten raw. (この海鮮, 食べる — attribute potential)

**What to record:** The correct sentence for each, and a note explaining the distinction where it matters.

## Exercise 3: Building Relative Clauses
**Type:** Hands-on

Combine these elements into noun phrases using a relative clause (plain-form verb + head noun):

1. I bought / book / yesterday
   → 「_____本」

2. Tanaka-san is making / dinner / tonight
   → 「_____夕食」

3. I haven't seen / movie / yet
   → 「まだ_____映画」(use negative plain form)

4. My friend works / restaurant / in that town
   → 「友達が_____レストラン」

5. I lived / apartment / when I was a student
   → 「学生の時に_____アパート」

Then use each noun phrase you built in a complete sentence.

## Exercise 4: Identifying the Error
**Type:** Analysis

Each sentence contains an error in how the relative clause is formed. Identify and correct it.

1. 田中さんは作るご飯は美味しい。
2. 先週読みましたレポートは難しかった。
3. 友達のくれたプレゼントをもらった。(Intended: I received the present that my friend gave me.)
4. 私が昨日会う人は田中さんです。(Intended: The person I met yesterday is Tanaka-san.)
5. 私は住む町は静かだ。

## Exercise 5: Complex Noun Phrases
**Type:** Hands-on (short writing)

Japanese relative clauses can stack. Build complex noun phrases for these descriptions, then use them in a full sentence:

1. "the old coffee shop that I used to go to every Saturday when I was in university"
   (Vocabulary: 大学の時, 毎週土曜日, 行く, 古い, 喫茶店)

2. "the person who taught me how to make beer"
   (Vocabulary: ビールを作る方法, 教える, 人)

3. "the project I was forced to work on last week" (Use causative-passive from Session 08 in the clause)

**What to record:** Your three complex noun phrases, each embedded in a complete sentence, with English translations.
