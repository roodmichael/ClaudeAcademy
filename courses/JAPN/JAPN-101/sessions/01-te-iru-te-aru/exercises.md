## Exercise 1: The "Can It Happen Twice?" Test
**Type:** Analysis

For each verb below, decide whether ている would express (A) ongoing action or (B) resultant state. Use the heuristic from the lesson: can this action happen twice in a row without a reset?

1. 食べる (eat)
2. 死ぬ (die)
3. 読む (read)
4. 結婚する (get married)
5. 走る (run)
6. 着る (put on clothes)
7. 眠る (sleep)
8. 起きる (wake up)

**What to record:** For each verb, write (A) or (B) and one example sentence using ている. Then write the English meaning of your sentence.

## Exercise 2: Spot the Context
**Type:** Analysis

Each sentence below uses ている. Identify whether it expresses (A) ongoing action, (B) resultant state, or (C) habitual action.

1. 田中さんは今、電話をかけている。(Tanaka-san is on the phone right now.)
2. 彼女は東京に住んでいる。(She lives in Tokyo.)
3. 私は毎朝コーヒーを飲んでいる。(I drink coffee every morning.)
4. 子供が庭で遊んでいる。(The children are playing in the yard.)
5. 彼はもう結婚している。(He is already married.)
6. バスが来ている。(The bus has come / is here.)

**What to record:** Write your classification (A/B/C) and explain your reasoning for each.

## Exercise 3: てある vs. ている
**Type:** Thought experiment

These two sentences describe a very similar physical situation but carry different meanings:

- 窓が開いている。(Mado ga aite iru.)
- 窓が開けてある。(Mado ga akete aru.)

1. Write out what each sentence means in English as precisely as you can — not just "the window is open" but what each one implies about *how* it got that way.
2. In which of these situations would each sentence be appropriate?
   - Situation A: You walk into a room and a window is open. There was a storm last night.
   - Situation B: You walk into a room and a window is open. You asked your roommate to air out the room before you arrived.
3. Now create two parallel sentences about a coffee maker (コーヒーメーカー) — one using ている (intransitive verb: 動く, works/is running) and one using てある (transitive verb: 準備する, prepare).

**What to record:** Your English translations of the two window sentences, your situation assignments with reasoning, and your two coffee maker sentences.

## Exercise 4: Transitive or Intransitive?
**Type:** Hands-on

てある only works with transitive verbs. Before you can use てある correctly, you need to identify whether a verb is transitive (takes an object) or intransitive (does not).

Sort these verbs into transitive (can say を + verb) or intransitive:

| Verb | Transitive or Intransitive? |
|------|----------------------------|
| 開ける (akeru — open something) | ? |
| 開く (aku — open [by itself]) | ? |
| 書く (kaku — write) | ? |
| 消える (kieru — disappear) | ? |
| 消す (kesu — turn off/erase) | ? |
| 置く (oku — place/put) | ? |
| 始まる (hajimaru — begin [by itself]) | ? |
| 始める (hajimeru — begin [something]) | ? |

Then write one てある sentence using two of the transitive verbs.

**What to observe:** Which pairs are intransitive/transitive twins? Japanese frequently pairs verbs this way. This pattern will matter throughout the course.

## Exercise 5: Colloquial to Formal
**Type:** Hands-on

In casual speech, ている contracts. Convert these casual forms to their full written forms, then use each in a complete sentence:

1. 食べてる → ?
2. 寝てた → ?
3. 書いてる → ?
4. 笑ってた → ?

Then reverse the direction: convert these formal forms to their casual spoken equivalents:
5. 勉強しています → ?

**What to record:** The full and casual forms, plus which register (casual vs. formal) you would use in: a text message to a friend / a report to your boss / a conversation with a stranger.
