# Session 10: Nominalizers — こと and の
**Course:** JAPN-101
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to convert verb phrases into noun phrases using こと and の, and select the correct nominalizer in contexts where they are not interchangeable.

---

## Opening

Every language needs a way to talk about actions as things. In English, gerunds do this: "Running is good exercise." "I enjoy cooking." "She heard him singing." Japanese does this with **nominalizers** — two small words, こと and の, that attach to a plain-form verb or adjective and turn the entire phrase into a noun.

Most of the time, you can choose either one freely. But in specific contexts, only one is grammatically correct. Learning the rules for those contexts is the point of this session.

---

## Formation — Both Work the Same Way

Both こと and の attach to the **plain form** of the preceding predicate. This is the same plain-form rule from relative clauses.

| Predicate type | こと | の |
|---------------|------|-----|
| Verb (plain present) | 食べる**こと** | 食べる**の** |
| Verb (plain past) | 食べた**こと** | 食べた**の** |
| い-adjective | 忙しい**こと** | 忙しい**の** |
| な-adjective | 元気な**こと** | 元気な**の** |
| Noun (predicate) | 学生であること | 学生なの |

**Critical rule:** Polite forms cannot be nominalized.
- ❌ 食べますこと / 食べますの
- ✅ 食べること / 食べるの

And na-adjectives require な before both nominalizers:
- ❌ 元気のに → ✅ 元気なのに (though のに is a different construction)
- 元気なことが大事だ / 元気なのが大事だ (both: it's important to be healthy)

---

## When Either Works (Most of the Time)

In most contexts, こと and の are freely interchangeable:

- 日本語を話すことは楽しい。= 日本語を話すのは楽しい。(Speaking Japanese is fun.)
- 早く起きることが大切だ。= 早く起きるのが大切だ。(Waking up early is important.)
- ピアノを弾くことが好きだ。= ピアノを弾くのが好きだ。(I like playing the piano.)

When you have a free choice, either is natural. の tends to feel slightly more conversational; こと slightly more formal or abstract.

---

## When ONLY こと Works

### 1. Before だ/です in a predicate nominal

When the nominalized phrase is the predicate of the sentence (equated to the subject using だ/です), only こと works:

- 趣味は寝ること**だ**。(My hobby is sleeping.) ✅
- ❌ 趣味は寝るの**だ**。(Sounds like のだ — explanatory/evidence pattern, not equation)

### 2. Fixed こと expressions

Several high-frequency grammar patterns use こと as a locked-in component:

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
|-----------|---------|---------|
| ことができる | can do | 私は料理することができる。|
| ことがある | there are times when / have experienced | 日本に行ったことがある。|
| ことにする | decide to | 早起きすることにした。|
| ことになる | it has been decided (externally) | 来月転勤することになった。|
| ことにしている | make it a rule to | 毎朝走ることにしている。|

In all of these, の cannot substitute for こと.

### 3. After thought, communication, and knowledge verbs

When the nominalized clause is the object of verbs like 知る (know), 思う (think), 聞く (be told), 言う (say) in the sense of reporting or indirect speech/thought:

- 彼が来ることを知っている。(I know that he is coming.) ✅
- 彼が来ることを聞いた。(I heard that he is coming.) ✅

The の version is possible in some of these contexts but こと is safer and more formal.

---

## When ONLY の Works

### 1. After verbs of direct perception (see, hear)

When you are directly perceiving an event as it happens — witnessing or hearing something in real time:

- 父が歌うのを聞いた。(I heard my father singing.) ✅
- ❌ 父が歌うことを聞いた。(Wrong in the perceptual sense)

- 子供たちが遊んでいるのを見た。(I watched the children playing.) ✅
- ❌ 子供たちが遊んでいることを見た。(Wrong — こと implies reported/abstract awareness)

The distinction: の is used for directly experienced, sensory events. こと is for reported, thought about, or abstract ones.

### 2. With やめる (to stop, quit)

- 勉強するのをやめた。(I stopped studying.) ✅
- ❌ 勉強することをやめた。(Unnatural)

### 3. With action-assistance and waiting verbs

With verbs like 手伝う (help), 待つ (wait for), 忘れる (forget — in the action sense):

- 彼が帰るのを待った。(I waited for him to come home.) ✅
- 荷物を運ぶのを手伝った。(I helped carry the luggage.) ✅

---

## The Deep Principle

If you want a heuristic: **の is for directly experienced, concrete events. こと is for abstract, conceptual, or reported actions.**

- Hearing someone sing right now → の (you are there, experiencing it)
- Knowing that someone will come → こと (abstract knowledge, not sensory)

This principle does not cover every edge case perfectly, but it handles the majority.

---

## Why This Matters

Nominalizers are everywhere in Japanese. The moment you move beyond the simplest sentences — "I eat sushi" — you will encounter nominalization constantly. Describing hobbies, explaining causes, expressing knowledge, making decisions — all of these involve nominalizers. The こと/の distinction is tested at N3 and trips up learners even at advanced levels.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Both こと and の nominalize verb phrases; both require plain form
- In most contexts, either is acceptable; の is slightly conversational, こと slightly formal
- Only こと: before だ/です in predicate nominalization; fixed expressions (ことができる, ことがある, etc.); after knowledge/thought verbs
- Only の: with direct perception verbs (see/hear in real time); with やめる; with hand-assistance and waiting verbs
- The deep principle: の = concrete/sensory; こと = abstract/conceptual
