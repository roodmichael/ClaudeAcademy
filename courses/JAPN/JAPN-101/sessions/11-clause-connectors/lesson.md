# Session 11: Connecting Clauses — から/ので, が/けど, のに, たり〜たり, ながら
**Course:** JAPN-101
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to connect clauses using five connector patterns — choosing between から and ので for register, navigating the contrast connectors (が/けど vs. のに), listing non-exhaustively with たり〜たり, and expressing simultaneous actions with ながら.

---

## Opening

Single-clause Japanese is grammatically correct but socially incomplete. Real conversations, real writing, real thought — all of it flows across multiple clauses. The connectors in this session are the mortar between sentences. They carry not just logical content but emotional charge: why something happened, what surprised you, that two things happened at the same time.

By the end of this session, you will have the last major structural tools for N3-level production.

---

## から and ので — Expressing Reason

Both から and ので mean "because" or "so." Both connect a reason clause to a result or action. The difference is in **register and speaker stance**.

**Formation:**

| Predicate | から | ので |
|----------|------|------|
| Verb plain | 行くから | 行くので |
| Verb polite | 行きますから | 行きますので |
| い-adjective | 寒いから | 寒いので |
| な-adjective | 元気だから | 元気**な**ので |
| Noun | 学生だから | 学生**な**ので |

Note: な is required before ので for na-adjectives and nouns (same pattern as の nominalizer).

**The key distinction:**

**から** is assertive and personal. The speaker is stating their reason directly and confidently. This can sound self-centered or demanding if overused in polite contexts.

**ので** is objective and deferential. The speaker presents the reason as a circumstance rather than a personal assertion. In requests or explanations to superiors, ので sounds more considerate.

- Less appropriate: 用事があるから早退します。(I'm leaving early because I have something to do — assertive)
- More appropriate: 用事があるので早退します。(same meaning, but circumstance-framing, more polite)

**What only から can do:**

1. Follow probability/likelihood expressions (でしょう/だろう):
   - 雨が降るでしょう**から**、傘を持って行って。(Because it will probably rain, take an umbrella.) ✅
   - ❌ 雨が降るでしょう**ので**

2. Answer "why" questions:
   - なぜですか → 〜**からです**。(Because...) ✅
   - ❌ 〜のでです

3. Appear in the emphasis pattern のは〜からだ:
   - 成功したのは努力した**からだ**。(The reason for my success is that I worked hard.) ✅
   - ❌ 成功したのは努力した**のでだ**

## が and けど — Neutral Contrast

**が** and **けど** (and its variants けれど, けれども) all mean "but" or "however." They express neutral contrast between two clauses — no emotional judgment, no surprise, no complaint. Just two facts that pull in different directions.

**Formality ladder (most formal → least formal):**
けれども → けれど → が → けど

- けれども: formal written prose
- が: neutral/literary; common in written and formal speech
- けど: everyday casual speech (by far the most common in conversation)

**Two uses:**

1. **Contrast:** The second clause contradicts or qualifies the first.
   - このカフェは遠い**けど**、よく行く。(This café is far, but I go often.)
   - 高い**が**、品質はいい。(It's expensive, but the quality is good.)

2. **Softening / preliminary remark:** Sets up context before a request or sensitive information.
   - すみません**が**、ちょっといいですか。(Excuse me, but do you have a moment?)
   - 来週のことなんです**けど**、一緒に行きませんか。(About next week — want to come with me?)

Unlike のに, が/けど can precede volitional expressions, requests, and invitations.

---

## のに — Emotional Contrast

**のに** expresses contrast with **emotional weight**: surprise, frustration, disappointment, or occasionally positive wonder. The second clause contradicts what the first clause would lead you to expect, and the speaker feels something about that.

**Formation:**
- Verb plain form + のに
- い-adjective + のに
- な-adjective + **な** + のに (な required, unlike が/けど)
- Noun + **な** + のに

**Core examples:**

- 疲れているのに寝れない。(I can't sleep despite being tired — frustration)
- 安いのに美味しい。(It's delicious despite being cheap — positive surprise)
- 一生懸命勉強したのに試験に落ちた。(I failed despite studying hard — complaint)

**Constraints:**

- Cannot precede affirmative volitional expressions: ❌ 高いのに買おう → ✅ 高いけど買おう
- Cannot precede requests or invitations: ❌ 疲れているのに手伝ってください → ✅ 疲れているけど手伝ってください
- Exception: negative commands work fine: 高いのに買わないでください (even though it's expensive, please don't buy it)

**Sentence-initial なのに:** Used at the start of a second sentence to express "And yet..." or exasperation:
- なのに、また遅刻した。(And yet, he was late again.)

## たり〜たりする — Non-Exhaustive Listing

**たり〜たりする** lists representative examples of actions without implying a fixed order or complete list. "I do things like X and Y" — the listener understands that other things also happen.

**Formation:** Ta-form + り + ... + ta-form + り + する

- 読む → 読んだ → 読んだり
- 歌う → 歌った → 歌ったり
- 食べる → 食べた → 食べたり

The final する is conjugated for tense and politeness: した (past), します (present polite), している (ongoing).

**Compare to te-form chaining:**

- て-form: 勉強して、ごはんを食べて、シャワーを浴びた。(I studied, ate, and showered — exhaustive sequence)
- たり〜たり: 勉強したり、ごはんを食べたりした。(I did things like study and eat — non-exhaustive, any order)

**Two uses:**

1. **Non-exhaustive list of different actions:**
   - 週末は映画を見たり、友達と話したりする。(On weekends I do things like watch movies and talk with friends.)

2. **Alternating states:**
   - 天気が晴れたり曇ったりしている。(The weather keeps alternating between sun and clouds.)

**One-item たり:** Listing a single representative example is also common:
- 映画を見たりします。(I do things like watch movies.)

**Critical rule:** The construction must close with する (conjugated). Leaving it open is an error:
- ❌ 映画を見たり、音楽を聴いたり。
- ✅ 映画を見たり、音楽を聴いたりした。

## ながら — Simultaneous Actions

**ながら** expresses two actions happening at the same time by the same person. The action before ながら is the background/subordinate activity; the action after is the main focus.

**Formation:** ます-stem + ながら
- 聞く → 聞き + ながら → 聞きながら
- 食べる → 食べ + ながら → 食べながら
- 歩く → 歩き + ながら → 歩きながら

**Three rules:**

1. **Same subject:** Both actions must have the same performer.
   - ✅ 私は音楽を聴きながら歩いた。(I walked while listening to music.)
   - ❌ 私が歩きながら、犬が走った。(Different subjects — ungrammatical)

2. **Action A is subordinate:** What comes before ながら is the background activity.
   - 音楽を聴きながら勉強する。(Study while listening to music — studying is the main action)
   - 勉強しながら音楽を聴く。(Listen to music while studying — listening is now the main action)

3. **Both actions must be genuinely simultaneous:** ながら requires overlap in time. Instantaneous events cannot serve as ながら backgrounds.
   - ✅ テレビを見ながら食べる。(Both are durative and genuinely simultaneous)
   - ❌ 着いてながら話し始めた。(Arriving is instantaneous — cannot be ながら)

---

## Why This Matters

These five connectors are not optional accessories — they are the difference between choppy, elementary Japanese and natural intermediate speech. から vs. ので tells your listener whether you are asserting or deferring. のに signals how you feel about a situation. ながら tells them you were doing two things at once. At N3, these should come without deliberate thought.

---

## Key Takeaways

- から = assertive reason; ので = deferential reason; ので is preferred in polite requests and explanations to superiors
- から is exclusive in: でしょうから, なぜですか → からです, のは〜からだ patterns
- が/けど = neutral contrast; formality ladder: けれども > けれど > が > けど
- のに = emotional contrast (surprise, frustration, complaint); needs な before it for na-adj/nouns; cannot precede volitional
- たり〜たり = non-exhaustive listing; must close with する conjugated for tense
- ながら = same-subject simultaneous durative actions; action before ながら is subordinate
