# Session 04: Conditionals I — と and ば
**Course:** JAPN-101
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to form sentences with と and ば, identify what each construction specializes in, and avoid the most common substitution error between them.

---

## Opening

Push a button. The light turns on. Every time. No exceptions. What kind of "if" is that?

It is not really an "if" at all — it is a *when*, a constant result. The light does not sometimes turn on; it always turns on. Japanese has a conditional specifically for this kind of relationship: と. It is the conditional for rules, habits, and the automatic world — and it cannot be used for anything else.

Japanese has four conditional forms where English uses just "if." Understanding why four exist requires understanding what each one specializes in. Sessions 04 and 05 tackle all four.

---

## と — Natural Consequence and Constant Results

**と** (to) attaches to the plain form of a verb, adjective, or noun-plus-だ and expresses results that **automatically, necessarily, or habitually follow** from the condition.

**Formation:**
- Verb (plain form) + と: 押すと (when/if you push)
- い-adjective + と: 寒いと (when it's cold)
- な-adjective + だ + と: 元気だと (when [someone] is healthy)
- Noun + だ + と: 春だと (when it's spring)

**と expresses:**

1. **Natural phenomena and scientific facts:**
   - 雨が降ると、涼しくなる。When it rains, it gets cool.
   - 水を100度まで熱すると沸騰する。When you heat water to 100 degrees, it boils.

2. **Habitual or automatic sequences:**
   - お腹が空くとパンを食べる。When I get hungry, I eat bread (every time).

3. **"And then" — discovering an actual condition (past narrative):**
   - 祭りに行くとボブがいた。I went to the festival and (there) Bob was.
   - 角を曲がると大きな公園があった。I turned the corner and there was a large park.

The "and then" use is interesting: you describe an action in the present (行く) but the result is past (いた). This is a narrative device for "upon doing X, I discovered Y."

**What と cannot do:**

- **No volitional expressions in the result clause:** ❌ 時間があると観光したい (wrong — want is volitional)
- **No requests or invitations:** ❌ 晴れると公園に行きませんか (wrong)
- **No imperatives or advice:** ❌ 雨が降ると傘を持って行って (wrong)
- **Cannot pair with もし:** もし signals hypothesis; と signals certainty. ❌ もし雨が降ると

The reason: と implies the result *must* follow. Adding a volitional expression or request implies the person has a choice — which breaks the "automatic" logic.

---

## ば — Emphasis on the Condition

**ば** (ba) is the conditional that foregrounds the condition clause itself. It carries a nuance of "if *and only if*" this condition is met. Think of ば as saying: the condition is the key variable — satisfy it, and the result follows.

**Formation:**
- **Ru-verbs:** Drop る, add れば → 食べる → 食べれば
- **U-verbs:** Replace final sound with え-row + ば
  - 書く → 書けば
  - 飲む → 飲めば
  - 話す → 話せば
  - 歩く → 歩けば
  - 買う → 買えば
  - 待つ → 待てば
- **Irregular:** する → すれば; 来る → 来れば
- **い-adjectives:** Replace い with ければ → 安い → 安ければ
- **Negative:** Add なければ → 行かない → 行かなければ
- **Na-adjectives / nouns:** Add であれば → 元気 → 元気であれば

**ば is natural when:**

- Emphasizing the condition as a state or truth:
  - 運がよければ合格するよ。If you're lucky, you'll pass.
  - 薬を飲めば治ると思う。I think if you take the medicine, you'll recover.

- Using potential form in the condition:
  - 行けるなら行く。If I can go, I'll go.

- Expressing timeless truths or advice:
  - 早起きすれば健康にいい。If you wake up early, it's good for your health.

**Where ば breaks down:**

ば cannot be used when the same person performs both the condition and the result as sequential volitional actions:

- ❌ 宿題が終われば遊ぶ → ✅ 宿題が終わったら遊ぶ (when homework is done, I'll play)
- ❌ 飲めば運転するな → ✅ 飲んだら運転するな (if you drink, don't drive)

The rule: when the condition clause describes the *speaker's own deliberate action* leading into another of *their own actions*, ば is unnatural. Use たら instead (Session 05).

ば *is* natural when the condition describes a state or uses the potential form (capacity), because states are not volitional.

---

## と and ば — Key Comparison

| Feature | と | ば |
|---------|----|----|
| Core meaning | Automatic, natural result | Condition as the key variable |
| Can follow volitional result? | No | Yes (with restrictions) |
| Works with scientific facts? | Yes, primarily | Less common |
| Can use もし? | No | Yes — もし + ば is natural |
| Sequential speaker actions? | Not typical | No — use たら |

---

## Why This Matters

と is the grammar of the predictable world: recipes, instructions, signs, nature, habits. You will see it constantly in written Japanese. ば is more nuanced — it appears in advice, logical argument, and conditional reasoning. Together they cover two of the most common conditional situations in everyday Japanese.

---

## Key Takeaways

- と: automatic, natural, habitual results; cannot precede volitional expressions or requests
- ば formation: え-row + ば for u-verbs; drop る + れば for ru-verbs; ければ for adjectives
- ば: condition as the focus; natural for states, potential forms, and timeless truths
- ば breaks down with same-subject sequential volitional actions → use たら
- もし works with ば but not with と
