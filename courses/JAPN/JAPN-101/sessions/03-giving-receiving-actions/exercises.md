Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: Who's Doing What for Whom?
**Type:** Analysis

For each situation, identify which construction to use — てあげる, てくれる, or てもらう — and from whose perspective:

1. Your older sister helped you move to your new apartment.
   - From your perspective: ?
   - From her perspective (if she were telling the story): ?

2. You helped a younger child who was lost find their way home.
   - From your perspective: ?

3. A stranger helped you when you dropped all your groceries.
   - From your perspective (you received help): ?
   - Describing what the stranger did (focusing on their kindness): ?

4. Your boss asked you to proofread a document, and you did it.
   - From your perspective (describing what you did): *Which form is risky here, and why?*

**What to record:** The construction for each perspective, one Japanese sentence for at least three of these, and a note on the social risk in #4.

## Exercise 2: Fill in the Blank
**Type:** Hands-on

Fill each blank with the correct form: てあげる, てくれる, or てもらう (conjugate for tense and politeness as indicated).

1. 田中さんが荷物を持って\_\_\_\_ (past tense — Tanaka-san did this for me)
2. 友達に道を教えて\_\_\_\_ (past tense — I received directions from my friend)
3. 小さい子供に絵本を読んで\_\_\_\_ (present — I will read to the child as a favor)
4. 先生に質問に答えて\_\_\_\_ (past — I had the teacher answer my question)
5. お母さんがお弁当を作って\_\_\_\_ (past — mom made a bento for me/us)

## Exercise 3: くれる vs. もらう — Same Event, Different Lens
**Type:** Thought experiment

The following event: *Your colleague Yamada-san stayed late to help you finish a difficult report.*

Write two sentences describing this event:
1. Using てくれる (Yamada-san as subject — foregrounding their generosity)
2. Using てもらう (You as subject — foregrounding what you received)

Then answer: In which social context would you prefer each sentence?
- When thanking Yamada-san directly to their face
- When telling a third person about Yamada-san's kindness
- When explaining to your manager why the report was finished on time

**What to record:** Your two sentences, and your reasoning for which fits each social context.

## Exercise 4: The てあげる Danger Zone
**Type:** Analysis

Each sentence below uses てあげる. Mark each as (S) safe, (R) risky/inappropriate, or (N) needs rephrase. Explain your reasoning.

1. 弟の荷物を持ってあげた。(I carried my younger brother's luggage.)
2. 社長にコーヒーを持ってあげた。(I brought coffee to the company president.)
3. 友達の猫に水をあげた。(I gave my friend's cat water.) — *Note: Is て here even needed?*
4. 学生に解き方を教えてあげた。(I taught the student how to solve it — I'm the teacher.)
5. 先輩のために資料を準備してあげた。(I prepared materials for my senior colleague.)

**What to record:** Your S/R/N classification for each, and for any R/N answers, provide an alternative phrasing.

## Exercise 5: Request Patterns
**Type:** Hands-on

Write polite requests using:
- てくれませんか (casual-polite — suitable for friends and colleagues)
- てもらえますか (more formal — suitable for semi-formal situations)
- ていただけますか (formal/business — suitable for superiors and formal settings)

Write the same request at all three formality levels:
- Asking someone to wait a moment (少し待つ)
- Asking someone to explain the instructions again (もう一度説明する)
- Asking someone to take a photo of you (写真を撮る)

**What to record:** Your nine sentences (three requests × three formality levels). Note which formality level you would use with: a close friend / a store clerk / a business partner you are meeting for the first time.
