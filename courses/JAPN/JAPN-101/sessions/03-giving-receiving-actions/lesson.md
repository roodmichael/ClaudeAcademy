# Session 03: てあげる, てくれる, てもらう — Giving and Receiving Actions
**Course:** JAPN-101
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to express doing a favor for someone (てあげる), receiving a favor from someone (てくれる), and having someone do something for you (てもらう) — with the correct grammatical structure and social register.

---

## Opening

In English, "Ken taught me Japanese" and "I had Ken teach me Japanese" describe the same event from different angles. In Japanese, these are grammatically distinct constructions with different verbs, different particles, and different social implications. The choice is not stylistic — it is grammatical. And one of them carries a risk of sounding condescending if you use it in the wrong direction.

The Japanese system of giving and receiving actions is built on the same verbs you already know: あげる (give upward / out), くれる (give inward / to me), もらう (receive). When you attach these to the te-form, the direction of the action — and the perspective of the speaker — determines everything.

---

## The Direction of Giving

First, a quick review of the base verbs without て:

| Verb | Basic meaning | Direction |
|------|--------------|-----------|
| あげる | to give | Speaker gives to someone outside in-group |
| くれる | to give | Someone gives to speaker (or speaker's in-group) |
| もらう | to receive | Speaker receives from someone |

The key distinction is **in-group vs. out-group**. The speaker's "in-group" (内, uchi) includes family members you feel close to, close friends, and yourself. Everyone else is "out-group" (外, soto). くれる flows *toward* the in-group; あげる flows *away from* the in-group.

---

## てあげる — Doing a Favor for Someone Else

**てあげる** (te-ageru) describes performing an action as a favor for someone outside your in-group. The speaker (or in-group member) is the actor.

Structure: **[actor] が/は [recipient] に [verb-te form] あげる**

- 友達に本を貸してあげた。(I lent my friend a book [as a favor to them].)
- 弟の宿題を手伝ってあげた。(I helped my younger brother with his homework.)
- 荷物を持ってあげましょうか。(Shall I carry your bags for you?)

**The social risk of てあげる:**

てあげる literally evolved from やる (to give — to inferiors, plants, animals). That origin lingers. Saying てあげる toward someone senior to you — a boss, a teacher, an elder — can come across as patronizing, as if you are bestowing a favor from a position of superiority.

Use て差し上げる (te-sashiageru) — the humble-polite form — when doing something for a superior where social deference is required. Or rephrase the sentence entirely to avoid the issue.

- ❌ 部長に資料を送ってあげた。(Sounds like you are doing the manager a favor from above)
- ✅ 部長に資料をお送りしました。(Respectful framing — no てあげる)

In casual contexts with friends and family, てあげる is natural and warm.

---

## てくれる — Someone Does a Favor for You

**てくれる** (te-kureru) describes someone outside your in-group performing an action that benefits you (or your in-group). The perspective is from the receiver — the speaker is highlighting what the other person *did for them*.

Structure: **[actor] が/は [verb-te form] くれる** (recipient = speaker, implied)

- ケンが日本語を教えてくれた。(Ken taught me Japanese [as a favor to me].)
- 母が弁当を作ってくれた。(My mother made me a bento [as a favor to me].)
- 友達が駅まで送ってくれた。(My friend saw me to the station [for my benefit].)

The nuance: てくれる frames the other person as generous. Using てくれる means you are acknowledging that you were the beneficiary. It is warm and appreciative in tone.

**Negative requests using てくれない:**

- 手伝ってくれない？(Won't you help me? — casual request)
- 静かにしてくれませんか。(Could you please be quiet? — polite request)

This uses the negative form of くれる as a softened request, which is very natural in Japanese.

---

## てもらう — Having Someone Do Something for You

**てもらう** (te-morau) expresses that the speaker *receives* an action from someone. The actor is marked with に; the speaker is the grammatical subject.

Structure: **[speaker] は [actor] に [verb-te form] もらう**

- ケンに日本語を教えてもらった。(I had Ken teach me Japanese / I received Ken's teaching.)
- 医者に薬を処方してもらった。(I had the doctor prescribe medicine for me.)
- 友達に道を教えてもらった。(I had a friend tell me the way.)

**てくれる vs. てもらう — same event, different subject:**

These two sentences describe the same event:
- ケンが日本語を教えて**くれた**。(Subject: Ken — Ken taught me; focus on Ken's generosity)
- ケンに日本語を教えて**もらった**。(Subject: I — I received Ken's teaching; focus on what I obtained)

Both are natural. The choice depends on which perspective you want to foreground: the giver's act (くれる) or your receipt (もらう).

**Requests using てもらえますか:**

- ちょっと手伝ってもらえますか。(Could I have your help for a moment? — polite)
- もう少し待ってもらえませんか。(Could you wait a little longer? — very polite)

These negative potential forms of てもらう make polished, indirect requests — more formal than てくれない.

---

## Formal Variants

Each of the three constructions has a keigo (formal) counterpart:

| Casual | Formal | Direction |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| てあげる | て差し上げる | Speaker gives/does for superior |
| てくれる | てくださる | Superior gives/does for speaker |
| てもらう | ていただく | Speaker receives from superior |

These formal variants are worth knowing at N3 level, particularly ていただく, which is ubiquitous in business Japanese:
- ご確認していただけますか。(Could I have you check this, please?)

---

## Why This Matters

Every time someone does something for someone else in a Japanese sentence, one of these three constructions is at work. Miss them, and your Japanese will sound blunt. Use the wrong one, and you may accidentally imply condescension. Get them right, and your Japanese suddenly sounds like someone who understands how Japanese social grammar works — which is far more impressive than knowing rare vocabulary.

---

## Key Takeaways

- てあげる: You/in-group does a favor for out-group. Risky toward superiors — use て差し上げる or rephrase.
- てくれる: Out-group does a favor for you/in-group. Foregrounds the giver's generosity.
- てもらう: You receive an action from someone. Foregrounds what you obtained.
- Both くれる and もらう can describe the same event — the choice is a matter of grammatical subject and perspective.
- Formal counterparts: て差し上げる / てくださる / ていただく
