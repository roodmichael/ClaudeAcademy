# Session 08: Causative-Passive and the Four Faces of られる
**Course:** JAPN-101
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to form causative-passive constructions, always express that you were forced to do something, and disambiguate the four meanings of られる using context and particle cues.

---

## Opening

Japanese gave you one verb form — られる — and assigned it four completely different jobs. Passive. Potential. Spontaneous. Respectful. Native speakers rely on context to know which is which, but learners are frequently lost. This session names all four, gives you disambiguation tools, and adds a fifth construction built from two of them: causative-passive, the grammar of being forced.

---

## Causative-Passive — Being Forced to Do Something

Causative-passive combines the causative suffix (させる) with the passive suffix (られる) to produce a construction meaning **"to be caused to do / to be forced to do."**

Unlike the plain causative, which can express both letting and making, **causative-passive always expresses compulsion** — there is no permissive reading.

**Long form (standard):**
- Ru-verbs: 食べる → 食べさせる → 食べさせ**られる**
- U-verbs: 書く → 書かせる → 書かせ**られる**
- する: させる → させ**られる**
- 来る: 来させる → 来させ**られる**

**Short/contracted form** (common in spoken Japanese — u-verbs only):
The させられる of u-verbs contracts to される in casual speech. This is formed by:
- Taking the passive base: 書く → 書かれる
- Replacing れる with される: 書か**される** → This does not apply cleanly. The actual contraction: 書かせられる → 書かされる

| Verb | Long form | Short form | Note |
|------|-----------|-----------|------|
| 書く | 書かせられる | 書かされる | U-verb: short form allowed |
| 飲む | 飲ませられる | 飲まされる | U-verb: short form allowed |
| 歌う | 歌わせられる | 歌わされる | U-verb: short form allowed |
| 食べる | 食べさせられる | ❌ | Ru-verb: no short form |
| する | させられる | ❌ | Irregular: no short form |

**Why no short form for ru-verbs?** The potential form of ru-verbs is also られる (食べられる). If you shortened 食べさせられる, you would risk confusion with the potential. The full form 食べさせられる is unambiguous.

**Sentence pattern:**
Subject は/が + **[causer に]** + [object を] + verb-causative-passive

- 兄に宿題をさせられた。(I was forced to do homework by my older brother.)
- 社長に残業をさせられた。(I was made to work overtime by the company president.)
- 子供の頃、毎日ピアノを弾かされた。(As a child, I was made to play piano every day.)

The causer — the one doing the forcing — is marked with **に**, exactly as in the passive construction.

## The Four Meanings of られる

The form られる (attaches to ru-verb base) and れる (attaches to u-verb base) carry four distinct meanings. All four use the same form; context is the only disambiguation tool.

### 1. Passive (受け身)

Someone receives an action performed by another agent.
- 犯人が警察に逮捕**られた**。(The criminal was arrested by the police.)
- この詩は芭蕉によって書か**れた**。(This poem was written by Bashō.)

**Signal:** Agent is often present, marked with に, から, or によって.

### 2. Potential (可能)

The subject has the ability or circumstantial opportunity to do something.
- 私はフランス語が話せ**ます**。(I can speak French.) — u-verb potential form
- この本を1日で読め**る**？ (Can you read this book in one day?) — u-verb
- この料理は食べ**られる**。(I can eat this dish.) — ru-verb potential

**Signal:** No agent present; context involves ability, capacity, or opportunity. Particle on the object tends to be が or を.

### 3. Spontaneous (自発)

An action occurs involuntarily or naturally — not caused by anyone. Often describes feelings, memories, and mental states that "arise" of their own accord.
- 昔のことが思い出さ**れる**。(The old days come to mind / memories arise spontaneously.)
- 彼女の言葉が気に掛か**られる**。(Her words weigh on my mind — the concern arises naturally.)
- 先生のことが案じ**られる**。(I find myself worrying about my teacher.)

**Signal:** Mental/emotional verbs; no volitional agent; the feeling "just happens." Common verbs: 思い出す, 感じる, 案じる, 思われる.

### 4. Respectful (尊敬)

The subject of the action is someone deserving of respect — a superior, elder, or social senior. The られる/れる form is a mild level of honorific expression.
- 先生が来**られた**。(The teacher has come.) — Honorific
- 社長が何か話さ**れましたか**。(Did the president say something?) — Respectful

**Signal:** The subject is clearly a superior (teacher, boss, elder). Context is formal. Note: this meaning of られる is relatively mild; more emphatic respectful language uses dedicated honorific verbs (いらっしゃる, おっしゃる, etc.).

## Disambiguation Strategy

When you encounter られる in a text, ask these questions in order:

1. **Is there an agent marked with に/によって?** → Likely **passive**
2. **Does the subject have the capacity to do the action?** → Likely **potential**
3. **Is the verb a mental/emotional state, and is the feeling involuntary?** → Likely **spontaneous**
4. **Is the subject clearly a superior in a formal context?** → Likely **respectful**

In practice, passive and potential are the most commonly confused, especially for ru-verbs. When you read 「食べられる」with no further context, it defaults to **potential** in modern spoken Japanese ("can eat") rather than passive ("is eaten"). Passive typically includes an agent.

---

## Why This Matters

The causative-passive is essential for describing workplace realities, childhood experiences, and social obligations — all highly productive topics in Japanese conversation and writing. The four-meanings disambiguation of られる is a consistent N3 examination target and an ongoing source of confusion in reading authentic Japanese. Knowing the signals allows you to navigate ambiguity strategically.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Causative-passive: させ + られる; always expresses compulsion, never permission
- Short form for u-verbs only: 書かせられる → 書かされる; no short form for ru-verbs or する
- Causer marked with に; same structure as regular passive agent marking
- られる has four meanings: passive, potential, spontaneous, respectful
- Disambiguation order: agent present? → passive. Capacity/ability context? → potential. Mental/involuntary? → spontaneous. Superior subject in formal context? → respectful.
