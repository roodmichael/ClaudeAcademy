# Session 07: Causative Voice — Letting and Making
**Course:** JAPN-101
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to form causative sentences, distinguish letting from making using particle choice, and correctly structure causative sentences with transitive and intransitive verbs.

---

## Opening

A parent tells their child to eat vegetables. A teacher lets students leave early. A boss forces staff to work overtime. In all three cases, someone is causing someone else to do something — but the relationships and power dynamics are completely different. Japanese grammar captures this with causative voice. And the entire letting/making distinction often comes down to a single particle: に or を.

---

## Causative Formation

The causative is formed by attaching **せる** (u-verbs) or **させる** (ru-verbs and する/来る) to the verb base:

**Ru-verbs (ichidan):** Drop る, add させる
- 食べる → 食べ**させる** (make/let [someone] eat)
- 見る → 見**させる** (make/let [someone] see)
- 起きる → 起き**させる** (make/let [someone] wake up)

**U-verbs (godan):** Replace final kana with あ-row equivalent, then add せる

| Verb | あ-row base | Causative |
|------|------------|-----------|
| 書く | 書か | 書か**せる** |
| 飲む | 飲ま | 飲ま**せる** |
| 話す | 話さ | 話さ**せる** |
| 歌う | 歌わ | 歌わ**せる** |
| 待つ | 待た | 待た**せる** |

**Irregular:**
- する → **させる**
- 来る → **来させる**

The resulting causative verb is a ru-verb and conjugates normally:
- 食べさせた (made/let [someone] eat — past)
- 飲ませています (is making/letting [someone] drink — ongoing)
- 書かせてください (please let [me] write — request)

---

## Letting vs. Making — The Particle Distinction

This is where causative gets nuanced. With **intransitive verbs**, the particle on the person being caused reveals whether the causation is **permission** or **compulsion**:

| Particle | Nuance | Example |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **に** | Letting — the person retains volition; you are permitting | 先生は生徒**に**帰らせた。(The teacher let the students go home.) |
| **を** | Making/compelling — stronger; the person does not freely choose | 親は子供**を**野菜を食べさせた。*See note below* |

Example pair:
- 先生は学生**に**発表させた。(The teacher let the students present.) — Permission
- 先生は学生**を**発表させた。(The teacher made the students present.) — Compulsion

The same sentence with different particles carries genuinely different meaning.

**Note on transitive verbs:** When the verb in the causative sentence is **transitive** (already takes an object with を), the particle on the person must be **に** — because を is already taken by the object:

Pattern: **Subject は [person に] [object を] verb-させる**
- 社長は社員**に**残業**を**させた。(The boss made the employees do overtime.)
- お母さんは子供**に**野菜**を**食べさせた。(The mother made/let the child eat vegetables.)

With transitive verbs, you cannot use を for the person because を cannot appear twice in the same clause. Context determines whether this is "let" or "make."

---

## Causative Sentence Patterns

**Intransitive verb causative:**
- Subject は/が + [person に/を] + [verb-causative form]
- 親は子供**を**眠らせた。(The parent made the child sleep.)
- 先生は生徒**に**外に出させた。(The teacher let the students go outside.)

**Transitive verb causative:**
- Subject は/が + [person に] + [object を] + [verb-causative form]
- 母は子供**に**牛乳**を**飲ませた。(The mother made/let the child drink milk.)

**Causative requests with てください:**
- 使わせてください。(Please let me use it.)
- もう少し考えさせてください。(Please let me think a little more.)
- 確認させていただけますか。(May I confirm? — formal)

The て-form of the causative + ください/もらう/いただく creates polite requests to be *allowed* to do something. This is extremely common in business Japanese.

---

## Common Social Contexts for Causative

**Parents and children:** Children are made to study, eat, clean. This is "making."
- 毎晩子供に宿題をさせる。(I make the children do homework every evening.)

**Teachers and students:** Teachers often permit rather than compel (at least in how it is framed):
- 試験の後、生徒に好きな本を読ませた。(After the exam, I let the students read a book of their choice.)

**Management:** Bosses making employees do things is a standard causative context:
- 部長に会議に出席させられた。(I was made to attend the meeting by the manager.) — Note: this is causative-passive, the next session.

**Requests to be allowed to do something** (self-causative):
- この仕事を任せてください。(Please let me handle this work.)

---

## Why This Matters

Causative constructions are deeply embedded in Japanese social hierarchy. Who causes whom to do what, and with what nuance of letting vs. making, reflects the relationship between the speakers. Getting this wrong — using を when に is appropriate, or vice versa — changes the meaning from "politely permitted" to "forced" and back again. At N3, this distinction is expected.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Causative formation: ru-verbs → drop る + させる; u-verbs → あ-row + せる; する → させる; 来る → 来させる
- に on the person = letting (permission); を on the person = making (compulsion) — intransitive verbs only
- With transitive verbs: person always takes に (を is already used for the object); context determines let vs. make
- Causative て-form + ください/もらう = polite request to be allowed to do something
- Causative reflects social hierarchy — who causes whom is rarely neutral
