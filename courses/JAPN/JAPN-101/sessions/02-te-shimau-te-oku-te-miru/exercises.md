# Session 02 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: Completion or Regret?
**Type:** Analysis

Each sentence uses てしまう. Decide whether the primary nuance is (A) completion/thoroughness, (B) regret/accident, or (C) both are possible depending on context. Explain your reasoning.

1. 宿題を全部してしまった。(I completely finished all my homework.)
2. 大事なメールを消してしまった。(I accidentally deleted an important email.)
3. ケーキを全部食べてしまいました。(I ate all the cake.)
4. 財布を忘れてしまった。(I forgot my wallet.)
5. ついそのことを友達に言ってしまった。(I inadvertently told my friend about that.)

**What to record:** Your classification (A/B/C) and a one-sentence explanation of the nuance you read.

## Exercise 2: てしまう in Colloquial Form
**Type:** Hands-on

Convert these written てしまう forms to their casual spoken contractions (ちゃう/じゃう), then back to the full form. Note whether each uses て or で as the te-form ending to determine which contraction applies.

1. 食べてしまった → ?
2. 飲んでしまう → ?
3. 書いてしまった → ?
4. 読んでしまった → ?
5. 忘れてしまう → ?

**What to observe:** Look for the pattern: if the te-form ends in て, the contraction is ちゃ. If it ends in で, it becomes じゃ. Does your list confirm this rule without exception?

## Exercise 3: ておく Planning Exercise
**Type:** Thought experiment

You are preparing for a trip to Japan next month. List five things you would do now "in advance for later" using ておく. Write each as a Japanese sentence with ておく.

Model: 日本語を勉強しておく。(I'll study Japanese in advance.)

Vocabulary hints if needed:
- 予約する (yoyaku suru) — make a reservation
- 両替する (ryougae suru) — exchange currency
- 地図をダウンロードする — download a map
- 荷物をまとめる (nimotsu wo matomeru) — pack bags
- パスポートを確認する — check passport

**What to record:** Your five sentences. Then: would any of these also work with てある once completed? Rewrite one sentence using てある to describe the same situation after the preparation is done.

## Exercise 4: てみる vs. ようとする
**Type:** Analysis

These two constructions both involve an element of "trying," but they carry different meaning. For each scenario, decide which construction is more appropriate and write a Japanese sentence:

1. You are curious whether you can balance on a skateboard for the first time.
2. You are attempting to lift a heavy box but cannot quite manage it.
3. You want to taste an unfamiliar dish at a restaurant to see if you like it.
4. You are struggling to open a jar that is stuck.
5. You decide to try calling your friend to see if they pick up.

**What to record:** Your choice (てみる or ようとする) for each scenario, one Japanese sentence, and one sentence explaining what aspect of the situation drove your choice.

## Exercise 5: Putting It Together
**Type:** Hands-on (short writing)

Write five sentences about a realistic day, using each te-form extension at least once:
- ている (for ongoing action or resultant state — your choice)
- てある
- てしまう
- ておく
- てみる

The sentences should describe related events in a logical sequence (a morning routine, a workday, a cooking session — any coherent scenario).

**What to record:** Your five sentences with English translations. Underline or mark each te-form extension so you can verify them easily.
