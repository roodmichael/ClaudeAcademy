## Exercise 2: なら — Context Required
**Type:** Thought experiment

なら requires pre-established context. For each scenario, write a なら response. The setup is given; your job is to respond with なら.

1. Setup: Your friend says, "I'm thinking of visiting Osaka next month."
   → Your なら response (suggest something to do there): ________________

2. Setup: A colleague says, "Do you know anyone who is good at Excel?"
   → Your なら response (you know someone): ________________

3. Setup: You observe your roommate putting on their coat and picking up their bag.
   → Your なら response (asking them to do something on the way): ________________

4. Setup: Someone says, "I need to apologize to Tanaka-san but don't know how."
   → Your なら response (give advice): ________________

**What to record:** Your four なら sentences in Japanese, with English translations. Note: is the context explicitly stated (from what someone said) or inferred (from something you observed)?

## Exercise 3: The Default to たら
**Type:** Analysis

These situations call for a conditional. For each, explain why たら is the appropriate choice and write the sentence:

1. You want to say: "When the project finishes, let's celebrate."
2. You want to give advice: "If you have a headache, how about taking a rest?"
3. You want to express a desire: "If I win the lottery, I want to travel the world."
4. You want to warn: "If you get drunk, don't drive home."

Now try substituting と, ば, or なら for たら in each case. Which substitutions break the sentence, and why?

**What to record:** Your four たら sentences, and for each, which substitution fails and what rule it violates.

## Exercise 4: Four-Way Contrast
**Type:** Analysis

For each situation, select the best conditional and explain why:

1. Scientific fact: "When you add acid to baking soda, it fizzes."
   → と, ば, たら, or なら?

2. Timeless advice: "If you study every day, you will improve."
   → と, ば, たら, or なら?

3. Responding to: "I'm thinking of going to Japan this summer."
   → と, ば, たら, or なら?

4. Invitation: "If you have free time tomorrow, won't you come with us?"
   → と, ば, たら, or なら?

5. Instruction with automatic result: "When you turn the key, the lock opens."
   → と, ば, たら, or なら?

## Exercise 5: Short Dialogue — All Four Conditionals
**Type:** Hands-on (short writing)

Write a short dialogue (6-8 lines) between two people planning a trip. Try to use all four conditionals naturally in the conversation. It does not need to feel forced — look for moments where:
- One character describes automatic events (と)
- One character gives advice emphasizing a condition (ば)
- One character expresses desires or makes suggestions (たら)
- One character responds to something the other said (なら)

**What to record:** Your dialogue with each conditional underlined or marked, and a brief note on why each form was chosen.
