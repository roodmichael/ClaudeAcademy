# Session 05: Conditionals II — たら and なら
**Course:** JAPN-101
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to use たら as a general-purpose conditional and なら as a contextual-response conditional — and distinguish both from と and ば.

---

## Opening

Imagine someone messages you: "I'm going to Kyoto next week." Now you respond: "Kyoto? Then you should definitely visit Fushimi Inari." In English, you used "then" — not "if." You are responding to established information, not constructing a hypothetical. Japanese has a conditional specifically for this conversational dynamic: **なら**. It requires prior context to exist before you can deploy it. That makes it unique among the four conditionals.

And before that, let's look at **たら** — the most versatile conditional in Japanese, and the one you should default to when you are not sure which form fits.

---

## たら — The General Conditional

**たら** is formed by taking the ta-form (past tense form) and adding ら. Since the ta-form is one of the most regular conjugations in Japanese — it follows the same sounds as the te-form but with た instead of て — formation is straightforward:

- 来る → 来た → **来たら** (when/if [someone] comes)
- 食べる → 食べた → **食べたら** (when/if [someone] eats)
- 雨が降る → 降った → **降ったら** (when/if it rains)
- い-adjectives: 寒い → 寒かった → **寒かったら** (if/when it's cold)
- な-adjectives: 元気 → 元気だった → **元気だったら** (if/when [someone] is healthy)
- Nouns: 学生 → 学生だった → **学生だったら** (if/when [someone] is a student)

**たら covers a wide range:**

1. **One-time future events (when):**
   - 夏が来たら日本に行く。When summer comes, I'll go to Japan.

2. **Hypothetical conditions (if):**
   - 試験に受かったら泣くと思う。I think I'll cry if I pass the exam.

3. **Volitional desires, requests, and invitations** (something と and ば cannot do):
   - 時間があったら観光したい。If I have time, I want to sightsee.
   - 飲んだら運転するな。If you drink, don't drive.
   - 晴れたら公園に行きませんか。If it clears up, won't you go to the park?

4. **Advice using たら〜どうですか:**
   - お医者さんに行ったらどうですか。How about going to the doctor?
   - 休んだらどうですか。How about taking a rest?
   This pattern is exclusively たら — と, ば, and なら cannot substitute here.

**たら is the safe default:**

The most important practical advice about Japanese conditionals: **when in doubt, use たら.** It handles the widest range of situations and will rarely produce an unnatural sentence. The cases where another form is clearly preferable (natural phenomena → と; contextual response → なら; condition as focus in timeless truths → ば) are ones you can learn to recognize over time. For production, starting with たら is sound strategy.

---

## なら — Responding to Established Context

**なら** is unlike the other three conditionals. It cannot introduce a hypothetical out of thin air. It requires that a **context has already been established** — by something the other person said, by a situation you can both observe, or by information the speaker presupposes.

**Formation:**
- Verb (plain form) + なら: 行くなら (if you're going)
- い-adjective + なら: 忙しいなら (if [you're] busy)
- な-adjective + なら: 元気なら (if [you're] healthy) — no な needed, unlike のに
- Noun + なら: 日本語なら (regarding/as for Japanese)
- Ta-form + なら (with optional ん): 行ったなら / 行ったんなら (if you went / now that you've gone)

**The classic なら use — responding to what someone said:**

Person A: 日本語が話せる人はいますか。 (Is there anyone who speaks Japanese?)
Person B: **日本語なら**、田中さんが話せますよ。 (Japanese? Tanaka-san can speak that.)

Person A established "Japanese" as the topic. Person B responds using なら: "As for that thing you mentioned — Japanese — here is relevant information."

**Situation-based context:**

- 出かけるなら鍵を閉めてね。If you're going out [I can see you're about to leave], lock up.
- 買い物なら卵を買ってきて。If you're shopping [I know you are], get some eggs.

In these cases, the speaker observes or infers something about the other person's situation and responds with a relevant condition.

**The temporal implication of なら:**

なら implies that the main clause (result) can happen *before or simultaneously with* the condition clause — not necessarily after it. This contrasts with たら, which implies the result comes after the condition.

- 日本に行くなら、今チケットを買った方がいい。(If you're going to Japan, you should buy tickets now — buy them before you go)

Here, "buy tickets" happens before "go to Japan." たら would suggest buying tickets after arriving, which is odd.

## The Four Conditionals — Complete Picture

| Feature | と | ば | たら | なら |
|---------|----|----|------|------|
| Natural/automatic result | Best | Possible | Less natural | No |
| Emphasis on the condition | No | Best | No | No |
| General conditional | No | With limits | Best | No |
| Volitional result clause | No | No | Yes | Yes |
| Contextual response | No | No | No | Best / Only |
| With もし | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Same-subject sequential actions | Not typical | No | Yes | With limits |
| Advice (〜どうですか) | No | No | Yes | No |

---

## Why This Matters

The four conditionals are one of the most-tested areas in JLPT N3. Beyond exams, the distinction tells you something about how Japanese speakers frame logical relationships. と says "this is how the world works." ば says "this condition is the key." たら says "after/when this happens." なら says "given what you just told me."

---

## Key Takeaways

- たら formation: ta-form + ら; works uniformly across verb types
- たら is the safe default; handles the widest range of conditions including volitional results and requests
- たら〜どうですか = advice/suggestion (exclusive to たら)
- なら requires pre-established context — it responds to what was already said or observed
- なら can imply the result clause occurs *before* the condition is fully realized
- The four-way comparison table above is worth memorizing — it will guide you through most conditional choices
