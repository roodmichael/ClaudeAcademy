# COFF-100L: Home Coffee Roasting — Lab

## Course Description

COFF-100 taught you the theory. This course puts it in your hands. Across six labs you will roast, cup, compare, and diagnose your own coffee — building the sensory vocabulary and iterative habits that separate a home roaster who gets better from one who just gets beans darker. Every lab produces a roast log entry. Every roast produces tasting notes. The loop is the point.

## Prerequisites

COFF-100: Home Coffee Roasting — Foundations (required). You need the vocabulary, the chemistry framework, and the equipment before this course makes sense.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:
- Describe the flavor differences between light, medium, and dark roasts using specific sensory vocabulary
- Roast a batch of green coffee through all three stages with active observation and full documentation
- Identify how development time affects flavor by comparing two roasts of the same bean
- Distinguish origin character from roast character by cupping two different beans roasted to the same level
- Diagnose a roast defect in the cup and trace it back to the specific roasting decision that caused it
- Apply single-variable adjustment methodology to improve a roast across multiple iterations

## Required Materials

**Beans**
- A natural-process bean as your primary bean — minimum 500g. Recommended: an Ethiopian or Kenyan natural at 1800 masl or higher. These origins are expressive and fruit-forward, which makes flavor changes across roast levels easy to detect. Sources: Sweet Maria's (sweetmarias.com), Coffee Bean Corral (coffeebeancorral.com), or Burman Coffee Traders.
- A washed bean from a different region as your secondary bean — minimum 250g. Recommended: a Central American washed (Guatemala, Mexico, Costa Rica) or a Colombian washed. These pair well with an African natural for a clearly contrasting origin comparison. Same sources as above.
- 2-3 commercially roasted coffees at different roast levels (light, medium, dark) for Lab 01 — buy fresh from a local roaster or well-stocked grocery

**Equipment**
- Home roasting equipment (air roaster, popcorn popper, or drum roaster — from COFF-100 Session 06)
- Kitchen scale accurate to 1g
- Timer (phone stopwatch is fine)
- Instant-read thermometer (optional but useful)
- Cupping cups or small mugs — at least 4 matching cups
- Kettle with temperature control (or thermometer)
- Coarse grinder
- Cupping spoons or regular tablespoons
- Storage containers with one-way CO2 valves or airtight mason jars
- SCA Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel (free PDF at sca.coffee)
- Notebook or lab log (digital or paper)

## Lab Schedule

| Lab | Title | Core Activity |
|-----|-------|---------------|
| 01 | Sensory Calibration | Cup light/medium/dark commercial roasts; establish flavor vocabulary |
| 02 | Your First Intentional Roast | Roast your primary bean to light-medium; full documentation; cup 24-48h later |
| 03 | Roast Level Comparison | Roast same bean twice: light vs. medium; cup side by side |
| 04 | Origin Comparison | Roast your natural-process bean and your washed bean to same level; cup side by side |
| 05 | Intentional Defect | Deliberately produce a defect roast; cup against a good roast; diagnose |
| 06 | Iteration — Single Variable | Roast best bean three times across two sessions; change one variable each time |

## Assessment Plan

- No session quizzes — labs are hands-on and self-correcting
- No midterm — the labs themselves are cumulative practice
- **Final project:** Roast Retrospective and Forward Plan — a structured document synthesizing your 6-lab arc, your current best profiles, and your next 3 roast designs with specific hypotheses

## A Note on Pacing

Each lab has two parts: the roast session and the cupping session. These happen 24-48 hours apart. Plan accordingly — starting a lab the night before you want to cup it will not work. Lab 06 spans at least two roast sessions by design. Give yourself time. The waiting is not dead time; it is when the coffee is becoming what it is going to be. Use it to read your notes from the previous lab.

There is no fixed schedule. Work at the pace your bean supply, your equipment, and your week allow. The only constraint that matters: cup every roast, log every session.
