# Final Project — COFF-100L
**Course:** Home Coffee Roasting — Lab

---

## Overview

You have completed six labs. You have cupped commercially roasted coffee, roasted your own beans for the first time, compared roast levels, compared origins, deliberately produced a defect, and built a three-roast improvement arc. You have a log with real data about your equipment and your beans.

The final project asks you to synthesize that arc into a single document: **a Roast Retrospective and Forward Plan**. This is not a test of what you remember from COFF-100. It is a structured reflection on what you actually did — and a concrete plan for what you do next.

This document is your entry point into continued home roasting practice. Write it well and it will be useful to you in six months.

---

## The Task

Write a **Roast Retrospective and Forward Plan** structured as follows. There is no required length — write as much as the material warrants. Aim for thoroughness over brevity.

---

### Section 1: The Arc — What You Learned About Your Equipment

Describe how your understanding of your roasting equipment changed across the course. Answer all of the following:

- How does your equipment behave in the drying phase — does it run hot, cool, fast, or slow?
- How does first crack sound and behave on your machine? Any surprises compared to what you expected from COFF-100?
- What tendencies does your equipment have (toward underdevelopment, toward baking, toward inconsistency)?
- What settings or approaches did you discover through the labs that you would not have known before?

---

### Section 2: The Arc — What You Learned About Your Beans

Describe what you now know about each bean you roasted in this course. For each bean (at minimum your natural-process bean and your washed bean), answer:

- What is the flavor ceiling of this bean at its best roast level?
- What happens to this bean if you roast it too light? Too dark?
- How does it behave in the roaster — any quirks compared to your other bean?
- What origin and processing character can you reliably detect in the cup?

---

### Section 3: Current Best Roast Profile — Each Bean

State your current best roast profile for each bean. Be specific and quantitative where your data supports it.

**For each bean, include:**
- Green weight (your standard batch size)
- Equipment settings (heat, airflow, or equivalent)
- Target charge temperature (if measurable)
- Target total roast time range
- Expected yellowing time
- Expected first crack onset time
- Expected first crack end time
- Target development time (from first crack end to drop)
- Expected weight loss percentage
- What you expect in the cup (3-5 specific flavor descriptors)
- Overall rating you have achieved so far (1-10)
- Confidence level: how reproducible is this profile right now?

If you do not have reliable quantitative data on some of these fields, say so honestly and explain what you would need to add to your logging practice to get that data.

---

### Section 4: What Worked and What Did Not

Write an honest assessment of your six-lab arc. No platitudes — specific observations.

- Which lab produced the single most useful insight? What was it?
- Was there a roast you are genuinely proud of? What made it work?
- Was there a roast that disappointed you? What caused the problem, and do you know how to fix it?
- Looking at your overall scores across all logged roasts: what is the trajectory? Up? Flat? Variable?
- What surprised you most about the process of home roasting?

---

### Section 5: Forward Plan — Three Roasts with Hypotheses

Design your next three roasts in detail. These are real roasts you intend to actually do, not hypothetical exercises.

**For each of the three roasts, specify:**

1. **Bean:** Which bean? What lot/origin?
2. **Variable being tested:** What specific thing are you adjusting from your current profile?
3. **Starting value:** What is the current value of that variable in your best profile?
4. **Target value:** What will you change it to?
5. **Hypothesis:** What do you predict will happen to the flavor as a result?
6. **Success criterion:** How will you know if this roast was an improvement?

At least one of the three roasts should be on your natural-process bean. At least one should pursue a specific sensory goal (e.g., "I want to increase sweetness without losing the fruit character"). At least one should test a variable you have not yet systematically controlled.

---

## What to Submit

Your completed Roast Retrospective and Forward Plan, shared with the Lab Instructor for review and feedback.

The document can be written in any format — markdown, plain text, a typed document. It should be organized with the five sections above and should draw directly from your lab log entries. Quote your own data. Reference specific lab results. This is a synthesis document, not a general essay about coffee.

---

## Evaluation Criteria

- **Specificity:** Are claims supported by specific data from your logs, not just general statements?
- **Honesty:** Does the assessment accurately reflect what worked and what did not, including failures?
- **Clarity of profile:** Is the current best profile stated in enough detail that you could hand it to another person and they could attempt to replicate your roast?
- **Hypothesis quality:** Are the Forward Plan hypotheses specific, testable, and grounded in what you learned?
- **Synthesis:** Does the document show that the labs connected to each other — that Lab 04's insight informed Lab 06, for example?

## Lab Instructor Evaluation Notes

A strong submission will:
- Reference specific lab numbers and roast entry IDs when discussing results
- Show quantitative data in the roast profile (times, weights, settings) — not just qualitative descriptions
- Demonstrate that the student understands the cause-and-effect chain from roasting decisions to cup outcomes, not just correlation
- Produce a Forward Plan that would actually work — hypotheses that are plausible given what the student's data shows, not wishful thinking
- Be honest about gaps or things that did not make sense — intellectual honesty here is a strength, not a weakness

Watch for:
- Students who rate everything highly without differentiation — ask them to identify the single weakest roast and why
- Forward plans that say "I want to try a new bean" without any variable hypothesis — redirect to controlling a process variable first before introducing a new input
- Profiles stated entirely in qualitative terms — push for quantitative specificity on at least development time and total roast time
