# Lab 02 Debrief: Your First Intentional Roast

---

## What Just Happened

You roasted coffee. More specifically, you roasted coffee with intent — you aimed at a target (light-medium, 30-60 sec past first crack end), you documented what happened, and you tasted the result. That is the complete loop, completed for the first time.

Here is how to read your results.

---

## Reading Your Cup Against Your Process

**If the cup tasted sour, grassy, or harsh:**
Your development time was likely too short. Either you dropped before first crack fully ended, or your 30-60 seconds was on the low end and the bean needed more time. The fix is straightforward: add 20-30 seconds of development time in your next roast.

**If the cup tasted flat, papery, or dull despite a reasonable timeline:**
This is a "baked" roast — the drying phase moved too slowly, the roast lost momentum, and the Maillard reactions never built the complexity they needed to. The fix involves more heat earlier in the roast or a shorter total roast time.

**If the cup tasted balanced, fruity, and sweet — matching the origin notes your supplier described:**
You hit the bean's character. Write down every detail of what you did, because you will try to replicate it.

**If the cup tasted bitter or ashy:**
You likely went past second crack, or your development was significantly longer than 60 seconds. Pull earlier next time.

## About Your Equipment

Here is something that surprises most new home roasters: every piece of roasting equipment runs differently. A popcorn popper moves fast and hot; an air roaster with adjustable settings gives you more control; a drum roaster has more thermal mass and slower transitions. Your first roast taught you how your machine behaves — how fast it moves through the drying phase, how loud and long first crack is, how it responds to heat settings.

That is not secondary information. It is the first thing you need to know to roast well on your machine. You cannot learn it from a book.

## Weight Loss as a Proxy

You calculated your weight loss percentage. Here is the reference range:
- 12-14%: Light roast territory
- 14-16%: Light-medium (your target for this lab)
- 16-18%: Medium
- 18-20%+: Medium-dark to dark

If your weight loss does not match your sensory impression of roast level, trust your senses over the number — but note the discrepancy. Moisture content and batch size affect the weight loss calculation, and home equipment does not always align perfectly with these averages.

## The Natural-Process Character

Your primary bean is natural-processed. Natural processing means the coffee cherry dried with the fruit intact around the bean — and some of those fruit sugars infuse the bean during that drying period. This is why natural-process coffees can taste fruit-forward, sweet, and sometimes wine-like in the cup. That character is in the bean already; your job as the roaster is not to create it but to reveal it.

At light-medium, you are in the sweet spot for most natural-process beans: enough development to bring out the sweetness, light enough to preserve the fruit and floral notes. As you roast darker in Lab 03, you will watch that character change.
