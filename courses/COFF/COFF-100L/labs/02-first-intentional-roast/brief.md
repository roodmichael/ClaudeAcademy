# Lab 02: Your First Intentional Roast
**Course:** COFF-100L
**Learning Objective:** After this lab, you will be able to navigate all three roasting stages on your specific equipment, document each decision point in real time, and connect your roasting observations to the flavor in the cup.

---

## The Setup

You have tasted the spectrum. Now you produce something. This is the first lab where your decisions directly shape what ends up in your cup — and the first lab where you will have to think fast, document in real time, and live with the consequences.

The target is a **light-medium roast** of your primary bean: stop the roast 30-60 seconds after the end of first crack. That stop point sits comfortably in medium-light territory — enough development to round out the bean's character, not so much that you lose what makes it interesting.

This is not a test of whether you can hit that target perfectly. It is a test of whether you can pay attention, record what happens, and learn from what you taste.

---

## Why Start with Your Natural-Process Bean

Natural-process beans are expressive — their fruit character is prominent and easy to detect at lighter roast levels. This makes them good teaching beans: when a roast goes well or poorly, you can taste the difference clearly. If you chose a high-altitude natural (Ethiopian, Kenyan, Yemeni), expect fruit-forward character — berry, stone fruit, floral notes — in a well-executed light-medium roast. Read the tasting notes from your supplier for context on what this specific bean is capable of.

Knowing what the bean can do tells you whether your roast revealed or buried it.

---

## The Documentation Requirement

Full documentation is not optional in this lab. The whole point of Lab 02 is that it becomes your baseline roast — the first data point in your personal feedback loop. You cannot improve what you have not recorded.

Before your roast begins, you should have your log open and the following fields ready to fill in real time:
- Charge weight (green beans, weighed before starting)
- Equipment settings (heat level, airflow, charge temperature if measurable)
- Start time
- Time stamps at: yellowing, first crack onset, first crack end, and drop

After your roast, before cupping:
- Roasted weight (weigh the beans after cooling)
- Color observations at each stage
- Aroma notes at each stage

After cupping (24-48 hours later):
- Full tasting evaluation using the log schema

---

## The 24-48 Hour Rule

Do not taste this coffee immediately. The CO2 released during roasting (degassing) will mask flavor and make your cup taste flat and gassy for at least 12 hours. The sweet spot for a light-medium roast is 24-48 hours after roasting. The bean will continue to evolve — taste it again at 72 hours if you want to see how it changes.

This is one of the things that separates home roasting from buying pre-roasted beans at peak freshness: you control the rest period. Use it.

## Connection to COFF-100

This lab is the practical application of COFF-100 Sessions 04, 05, and 06 combined: the three roasting stages (Session 04), the roast level decision and first crack as your landmark (Session 05), and your actual equipment (Session 06). You are doing, for real, what those sessions described.

If anything surprises you — if first crack sounds different than you expected, if the roast moves faster or slower than you anticipated — write it down. That observation is data.
