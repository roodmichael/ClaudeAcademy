# Lab 02 Procedure: Your First Intentional Roast

**Estimated time:** 15-20 min roast session + 24-48h rest + 30 min cupping
**Beans needed:** 100-120g of your primary (natural-process) bean, green weight (yields ~85-100g roasted)

---

## Part A: The Roast Session

### Before You Start

1. **Weigh your green beans.** Use 100g to start — a manageable batch for most home equipment. Record this weight in your log.

2. **Set up your documentation station.** Have your log open, a pen or keyboard ready, and a timer visible. You need to record timestamps during the roast. You cannot do this from memory.

3. **Preheat your equipment** according to its instructions. If your roaster has a charge temperature recommendation, aim for it and note what temperature you charged at.

4. **Set up cooling.** Have a colander, cooling tray, or the built-in cooling mode of your machine ready. Cooling must happen fast — beans continue to cook from retained heat if you leave them in a warm chamber.

5. **Ventilate your space.** Roasting produces chaff (paper husks from the bean) and smoke. A window fan pointed out or a range hood on high is minimum.

### During the Roast

**Start your timer when you add the beans.**

Set 30-second interval checkpoints in your head, or call out the time as you observe.

**The drying phase (approx. 0 to 35-40% of total roast time):**
- Beans will be green, then begin to turn pale yellow.
- Aroma: grassy at first, then hay-like, then slightly sweet.
- Note the time when you observe full yellowing (beans evenly pale yellow, no green visible).
- Nothing dramatic is happening visually — this is the patience phase.

**The browning/Maillard phase (approx. 35-70% of total roast time):**
- Color shifts from yellow through tan to cinnamon-brown.
- Aroma becomes bread-like, then toasty, then caramel. Some people detect a sweetness in the air.
- This phase is building complexity. Pay attention — you are smelling the Maillard reaction.

**First crack — this is the moment:**
- You will hear a distinct popping sound, like popcorn starting. It may start with a few isolated pops, then roll into continuous cracking.
- **Record the exact time when you hear the first pop.** This is first crack onset.
- First crack will continue for 30-90 seconds depending on your equipment and batch size.
- **Record the time when first crack slows significantly or stops.** This is first crack end.

**The development phase — your decision:**
- After first crack ends, you are in development time. The clock is running.
- Your target for this roast: **stop 30-60 seconds after first crack ends.**
- Watch the color: at light-medium, beans should be medium tan to light brown, no surface oil.
- Smell the air: you may detect fruit aromatics opening up from the Ethiopian natural.
- **At 30-60 seconds past first crack end: drop the beans and start cooling immediately.**

**Record the drop time.**

### Immediately After

- Cool the beans quickly. Stir or agitate if your setup requires it.
- While the beans are still warm (but after they are fully cooled), note the color and smell.
- **Weigh the roasted beans.** Record roasted weight and calculate weight loss percentage: ((green weight - roasted weight) / green weight) x 100. A light-medium roast typically loses 13-16% of its weight.
- Store beans in an airtight container. Label with: date roasted, bean name, batch number.

## Part B: The Cupping Session (24-48 Hours Later)

### Setup

1. Let beans rest 24-48 hours. Resist the urge to taste earlier.
2. At the cupping session, grind approximately 8g of your roasted beans to a coarse grind.
3. Also set up a cup of the commercial light roast from Lab 01 if you have any remaining, or a reference cup of something you know well. Comparative cupping is always more informative than tasting alone.

### Cupping Protocol

Follow the same protocol as Lab 01:
1. Smell dry grounds. Record aroma.
2. Add 150 mL water at 93-95C.
3. Wait 4 minutes.
4. Break crust, inhale, note wet aroma.
5. Skim grounds.
6. Taste once hot (~70C), taste again as it cools.

### What to Evaluate

Fill in the full post-roast evaluation section of your log entry:
- Aroma (dry and wet)
- Acidity (rating 1-5 + notes)
- Sweetness (rating 1-5 + notes)
- Body (rating 1-5 + notes)
- Finish (length + quality)
- Flavor wheel terms (at least 3 specific descriptors)
- Tasting notes (freeform)
- Overall rating (1-10)
- Defect flags (any signs of underdevelopment, baking, or other issues)

### Key Interpretive Questions

Write your answers in the session notes field:
1. Can you detect the origin character of your bean — the notes your supplier described — or did the roast bury it?
2. Compared to the Lab 01 light and medium roasts, where does your roast fall on the spectrum?
3. What one thing would you change about this roast?

## Part C: Complete Your Log Entry

Your first roast log entry should include:
- Bean identity fields (filled in before roasting)
- All roast session and timeline fields (filled in during roasting)
- All sensory during-roast fields (filled in immediately after)
- All post-roast evaluation fields (filled in after cupping)
- Session notes: what happened, what surprised you, what you will do differently
- Next adjustment: one specific change you intend to make for your next roast of this bean

This entry becomes the foundation of your entire course arc.
