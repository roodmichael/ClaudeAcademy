# Lab 04: Origin Comparison
**Course:** COFF-100L
**Learning Objective:** After this lab, you will be able to distinguish origin character from roast character in the cup, and describe how natural versus washed processing affects flavor independent of roast level.

---

## The Setup

In Lab 03 you changed one thing — development time — and held the bean constant. Now you flip the design: hold the roast level constant and change the bean.

You will roast your natural-process bean and your washed bean to the same roast level using the same procedure. Then you will cup them side by side.

The single variable is origin and processing method. Everything about how you roast them will be as close to identical as you can make it.

---

## Two Very Different Beans

**Your natural-process bean:**
High-altitude natural-process coffees are among the most flavor-expressive beans available to home roasters. The combination of altitude (more complex sugars, more fruit development) and natural processing (dried with fruit on the bean) makes these coffees fruit-forward and sometimes wine-like. You know this bean from Labs 02 and 03.

**Your washed bean:**
Washed coffees typically taste quite different. Washed processing removes the fruit before drying, producing a cleaner cup that expresses the bean's intrinsic character — the genetics, soil, altitude, growing conditions — without the fruit-infused sweetness of naturals. Central American and Colombian washed coffees often feature balanced acidity, nutty or chocolate notes, and a clean finish. They are approachable and often described as "classic."

When you cup these two at the same roast level, any flavor difference you taste is origin and processing — not roast level.

---

## Why This Matters

One of the most common confusions in home roasting is conflating roast character with origin character. A very dark roast tastes "like a dark roast" regardless of whether the beans are from Ethiopia or Mexico. Roasting can overwhelm a bean's origin signature. This is why specialty coffee professionals generally prefer lighter roasts — they preserve what makes each origin unique.

In this lab, roasting to the same level lets you hear what each bean is actually saying underneath the roast. After this lab, you will be able to listen to those two different voices clearly.

---

## Choosing Your Roast Level

Use the roast level you found most interesting or revealing in Lab 03 for both beans. Light-medium is recommended — it preserves origin character without making the comparison too stark. If you found that the Durato Bombe G1 needed a bit more development (from your Lab 03 notes), use that adjusted stop point for both.

The target is: same roast level, different beans.
