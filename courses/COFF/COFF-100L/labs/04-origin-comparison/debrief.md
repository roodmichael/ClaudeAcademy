# Lab 04 Debrief: Origin Comparison

## What You Just Separated

You held roast level constant and changed the bean — which means the flavor difference in your cup is origin and processing, not your decisions as a roaster. You isolated the signal that was always there underneath the roast.

This is a more sophisticated insight than it sounds. Many home roasters roast for months before they understand that "I roasted these the same way, but they taste different" is expected and informative rather than a sign that something went wrong.

---

## The Natural vs. Washed Distinction

The Durato Bombe G1 was dried as a whole cherry — fruit on the bean. During those weeks of drying, the fruit's sugars, acids, and aromatic compounds partially infuse the seed. This is why Ethiopian naturals taste like they contain fruit: they literally picked up fruit character from the flesh that surrounded them.

The Mexico washed had its fruit removed before drying. What you taste is the bean's intrinsic character — the genetics, the soil, the altitude, the growing conditions — without the fruit layer on top. That is why washed coffees are described as "clean": the signal is the bean itself, not the bean plus the fruit.

Neither is objectively better. They are different flavor philosophies.

---

## Reading Your Comparison

**If the natural-process bean tasted more complex and fruity:** That is the natural processing. A high-altitude natural-process coffee is one of the most expressive beans available to home roasters — altitude, variety, and fruit-drying all pointing toward complexity.

**If the washed bean tasted cleaner and more straightforward:** "Clean" is not a criticism. A well-executed washed coffee is balanced, approachable, and shows the terroir of its growing region without the added layer of fruit fermentation. If you found yourself appreciating it more than you expected, that is a calibration of your own taste.

**If the difference was smaller than you expected:** Two possibilities. One: your roast level was dark enough that roast character began to override origin character in both cups. At medium-dark or darker, both beans start moving toward the same flavor territory (chocolate, caramel, reduced acidity). Two: the Mexico washed you have is simply not a dramatically different profile from a lightly developed Ethiopian — not all beans fall into their regional stereotypes. Both observations are worth noting.

## A Word About Your Preferences

By now you likely have developing opinions: maybe you prefer the Ethiopia at light-medium, or maybe you were surprised to enjoy the Mexico more than you expected, or maybe you want to try the Ethiopia at a darker stop point to see if it develops more body.

These preferences are not random. They reflect your palate, and your palate is a valid instrument. Write down your preferences and their reasons. They become the basis of your final project.
