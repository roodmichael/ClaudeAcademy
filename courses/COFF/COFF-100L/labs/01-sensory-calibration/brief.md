# Lab 01: Sensory Calibration
**Course:** COFF-100L
**Learning Objective:** After this lab, you will be able to describe specific flavor, aroma, acidity, body, and finish differences across light, medium, and dark roast levels using SCA Flavor Wheel vocabulary.

---

## The Setup

Before you roast a single gram of your own coffee, you need a baseline. Right now, if someone asked you to describe the difference between a light and a dark roast, you could probably say something like "lighter is more acidic" or "darker is more bitter." That is a start. But it is not enough to actually improve.

This lab gives you a sensory reference point that is not your own work. You will cup three commercially roasted coffees — one light, one medium, one dark — and build a working vocabulary before your own roasting variables enter the picture. When you produce your first roast in Lab 02, you will have something to compare it to.

Think of this as zeroing your instruments.

---

## Why This Comes First

The biggest mistake new home roasters make is evaluating their own roasts without a calibrated palate. Everything seems either "good" or "not quite right," but they cannot say why. The flavor wheel helps with vocabulary, but vocabulary without experience is just a list of words.

By cupping coffees across the full roast spectrum — before you have produced anything yourself — you make your future evaluations more precise. You will know what "light roast acidity" actually tastes like, not just what it means in a textbook.

This lab has no roasting. That is intentional.

---

## What You Will Need

- 3 commercially roasted coffees, ideally from the same origin or at least the same species (single-origin arabica preferred):
  - One light roast
  - One medium roast
  - One dark roast
- Buy these fresh from a local specialty roaster if possible. Aim for beans roasted within the last 2 weeks.
- Cupping setup: 3 matching cups, scale, grinder, kettle, cupping spoons
- SCA Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel (printed or on your phone)
- Lab log

---

## What You Are Looking For

You are not trying to determine which roast is "best." You are trying to experience the spectrum and name what you find.

Pay attention to:
- **Aroma** — what do the dry grounds smell like before water? What changes when you add water?
- **Acidity** — does the coffee have a bright, crisp quality? Does it feel lively or flat in your mouth?
- **Sweetness** — is there a sugary, caramel, or fruity sweetness? Or is it absent?
- **Body** — does it feel heavy and coating, or light and watery?
- **Finish** — what is the last thing you taste after you swallow?
- **Overall impression** — does everything work together?

---

## Connection to COFF-100

In COFF-100 Session 05 you learned that roast level determines the flavor outcome: light roasts preserve origin character (fruity, floral, acidic), dark roasts produce roast character (bitter, smoky, chocolatey), and medium roasts balance the two. This lab makes that theory real. You are about to taste exactly that arc in three cups.
