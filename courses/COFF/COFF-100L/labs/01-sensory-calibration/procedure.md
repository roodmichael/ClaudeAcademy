# Lab 01 Procedure: Sensory Calibration

**Estimated time:** 45-60 minutes active, plus 4 minutes brew wait
**Beans needed:** 25g total (about 8g per cup)

---

## Before You Begin

Set out your three coffees and label your cups: L (light), M (medium), D (dark). Have your flavor wheel within reach. Put your phone on silent — you want to be present for this.

Do not eat anything strong (garlic, onion, citrus) for at least an hour before. Coffee or water is fine.

---

## Step 1: Smell the Dry Beans (5 min)

Open each bag or container one at a time. Hold the container close to your nose and inhale.

**For each roast, write down:**
- Color — is it tan, brown, dark brown, nearly black? Any surface oil?
- Dry aroma — what do you smell? Try to be specific. Grassy? Caramel? Smoke? Dried fruit? Toast?

Do not read the bag's tasting notes yet. Form your own impression first.

---

## Step 2: Grind and Smell Again (5 min)

Grind each coffee to a coarse grind (about the texture of coarse sea salt). Use the same amount: approximately 8g per cup.

Place each ground coffee in its labeled cup. Smell again.

**For each roast, note:**
- Does the aroma change significantly when ground?
- What new notes emerge?

Freshly ground coffee releases aromatics that whole beans trap. This is often the most revealing moment.

---

## Step 3: Brew Using the Cupping Protocol (10 min)

For each cup:
1. Add approximately 150 mL (5 oz) of water just off the boil — about 93-95C (200-203F).
2. Start a 4-minute timer as soon as you add water to the first cup.
3. Do not stir. Let the crust form on top.

Once 4 minutes have passed:
4. **Break the crust** on each cup: push the grounds gently from one side with a spoon, lean in, and inhale deeply. This is the most aromatic moment of the entire session. Take 3-4 seconds before moving to the next cup.
5. **Skim** the remaining floating grounds off the surface using two spoons.

Note your wet aroma impressions immediately after breaking each crust.

---

## Step 4: Taste (15-20 min)

Wait until cups have cooled slightly — you want them hot but not burning, around 70C (158F). This usually takes 3-5 minutes after skimming.

Taste in order: light, medium, dark. Then go back and compare in any order.

**For each cup, evaluate and record:**

| Attribute | Rating (1-5) | Notes |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Acidity | | bright? citrus? flat? |
| Sweetness | | caramel? fruity? absent? |
| Body | | heavy? medium? thin? |
| Finish | | long/short? bitter? clean? |
| Overall | | 1-10 |

Use the flavor wheel to identify at least two specific descriptors per cup.

**Slurp loudly when tasting.** This is not rude — it aerates the coffee and spreads it across all your taste receptors. Specialty coffee professionals do this in formal cupping sessions.

---

## Step 5: Comparative Tasting Notes (10 min)

Once you have evaluated each cup individually, go back and taste them side by side with specific comparison questions in mind:

- **Light vs. Medium:** Where does the fruit/floral character change? Where does the sweetness start to shift?
- **Medium vs. Dark:** Where does the acidity drop off? What new flavors appear?
- **Light vs. Dark:** These are the extremes. Put them next to each other and describe the full journey.

---

## Step 6: Log Your Findings

Create your first lab log entry using the COFF-100L schema. For this lab, the roast session fields will be minimal or null — you did not roast these. Fill in the post-roast evaluation fields for each of the three coffees. You can create three entries (one per coffee) or document all three in a single session notes field.

**Key questions to answer in your session notes:**
1. Which roast level do you find most appealing right now? Why?
2. What single flavor descriptor surprised you?
3. What does "acidity" actually feel like in the cup, in your own words?

---

## What to Bring to Lab 02

Your tasting notes from this lab are your baseline. In Lab 02, when you cup your first roast, you will be able to say: "This reminds me of the light roast from Lab 01 — that same bright acidity" or "This tastes flatter than the medium I cupped in calibration — something went wrong." That comparison is the point.
