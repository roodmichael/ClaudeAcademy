# Lab 05: Intentional Defect
**Course:** COFF-100L
**Learning Objective:** After this lab, you will be able to identify a baked or underdeveloped roast by taste and trace the flavor directly back to the specific roasting decision that caused it.

---

## The Setup

This lab asks you to do something counterintuitive: make bad coffee on purpose.

You will deliberately produce either a baked roast or an underdeveloped roast of your primary bean, then cup it against a well-executed roast of the same bean. The comparison is the instruction.

There is no faster way to learn to recognize a roast defect than to taste it on purpose, in isolation, with full awareness of what you did to create it.

---

## The Two Defect Options — Choose One

**Option A: Baked Roast**
A baked roast is not burned. It is the result of a slow, low-energy roast that loses momentum through the drying and browning phases. The bean "bakes" rather than roasts — the Maillard reactions happen too slowly or incompletely, and the result is a flat, papery, dull cup. No brightness. No sweetness. No complexity.

To produce a baked roast intentionally: use significantly less heat than normal through the drying and early browning phases. Drag out the first 60-70% of your roast time. Make the temperature rise sluggish. You are aiming for a roast that takes 5-6 minutes longer than your normal roast of this bean.

**Signs you have succeeded:** The total roast time is unusually long. First crack comes late. The beans may look a somewhat normal roast color but the cup will smell flat and taste dull — no brightness, no sweetness, cardboard quality.

---

**Option B: Underdeveloped Roast**
An underdeveloped roast is stopped too early — before first crack has fully completed. The bean's cell walls have not fully fractured, the sugars have not fully converted, and the result is a sour, grassy, harsh cup.

To produce an underdeveloped roast intentionally: stop the roast 15-20 seconds before you estimate first crack will fully end. You are pulling the roast while it is still actively cracking.

**Signs you have succeeded:** First crack was still audible when you dropped. The beans may look lighter than usual. The cup will taste sour, grassy, and harsh — possibly with a raw or cereal note.

---

## Which Should You Choose?

Choose based on what you want to learn. If you have been struggling with flat or dull results, produce the baked defect to confirm whether that is what you have been tasting. If you have been getting sour or sharp cups, produce the underdeveloped defect to taste it intentionally and compare.

Both defects are common. Both are instructive. Pick one and commit.

---

## The Good Roast Reference

You need a well-executed roast of the Durato Bombe G1 to compare against the defect. Either:
- Use a batch you already have from Labs 02 or 03 (if it is less than a week old and was your best result), or
- Roast a fresh batch using your best process before producing the defect

Cup the good roast and the defect roast side by side. The contrast is the lesson.

---

## Connection to COFF-100

COFF-100 Session 04 introduced the flavor diagnostics framework: sour/grassy = underdeveloped, flat/papery = baked. This lab makes that framework real. After this session, you will never confuse "baked" with "underdeveloped" in the cup — and you will be able to identify both from taste alone, which means you will be able to diagnose and fix your own roasts.
