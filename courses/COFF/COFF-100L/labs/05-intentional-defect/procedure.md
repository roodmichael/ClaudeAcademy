# Lab 05 Procedure: Intentional Defect

**Estimated time:** One or two roast sessions (~15-20 min each) + 24-48h rest + 45 min cupping
**Beans needed:** 200-240g of your primary bean, green weight (100-120g per roast)

---

## Preparation: Decide and Document

Before roasting, write in your lab log:
- **Which defect you are producing:** baked or underdeveloped
- **How you intend to produce it:** exactly what you will do differently from your standard process
- **What you expect the cup to taste like:** based on the COFF-100 diagnostic framework

This pre-roast documentation is important — the comparison between your prediction and your result is where the learning lives.

---

## Part A: The Reference Roast (Good Roast)

If you do not have a good recent roast of your primary bean (within the past 5-6 days), produce one first using your best process from Labs 02-03.

Use your established procedure. Document fully. Label: "Lab 05 — Reference Roast — [date]"

If you have a good roast already on hand from a recent lab, use that and skip directly to the defect roast.

---

## Part B: The Defect Roast

### Option A: Producing a Baked Roast

**Setup:** Use the same charge weight as your reference roast. Set heat lower than normal — if you normally use Level 9, try Level 6 or 7. If you have no adjustable heat, use maximum but try to extend total roast time by aggressively slowing airflow or pulling back in another way available to your equipment.

**During the roast:**
- Your goal is a slow, plodding temperature rise.
- The drying phase should feel labored — beans should take noticeably longer than normal to transition from green to yellow.
- The browning phase should drag as well.
- First crack may come later than normal or may sound more subdued.
- Stop the roast at your normal development time past first crack — you are not pulling early, you are just roasting slowly to get there.

**Document:**
- Total roast time (should be significantly longer than normal — aim for at least 4-5 minutes longer)
- Any differences in how first crack sounded
- Color and aroma at each stage

Label: "Lab 05 — Defect Roast — Baked — [date]"

---

### Option B: Producing an Underdeveloped Roast

**Setup:** Use the same charge weight and heat settings as your normal roast. This defect is created by stop point, not by heat adjustment.

**During the roast:**
- Roast normally through drying and browning phases.
- When first crack begins, note the time.
- **Stop the roast 15-20 seconds before you estimate first crack will fully end.** You are pulling the roast while first crack is still audible.
- If first crack usually ends at 3:45, drop at 3:25 or 3:30.

**Signs you are on track:**
- First crack was still going when you dropped
- Beans are lighter in color than your normal roasts
- Aroma may have a grassy or fresh-cereal quality rather than the caramelized character of a finished roast

**Document:**
- First crack onset, and your actual drop time (should be before first crack end)
- Note whether crack was still audible at drop
- Color at drop

Label: "Lab 05 — Defect Roast — Underdeveloped — [date]"

---

## Part C: Rest Period and Predictions

Rest both roasts for 24-48 hours. While you wait, fill in your lab log fields for both roast sessions. In the lab_context hypothesis field, write:
- What you expect the defect cup to taste like, using specific vocabulary (not just "bad")
- What you expect the reference cup to taste like
- What you think the most dramatic difference will be

---

## Part D: Side-by-Side Cupping

Cup the defect and the reference simultaneously.

### Setup

- Grind 8g of each to the same coarse grind
- Two labeled cups: D (Defect) and R (Reference)
- Brew simultaneously, 4 minutes, break crusts, skim, taste

### Tasting Protocol

Start with the reference — let it remind you what a well-executed roast of this bean tastes like. Then taste the defect. Then alternate freely.

### Document the Following

**For the defect roast:**
- Full post-roast evaluation including defect_flags field — flag the specific defect: "baked" or "underdeveloped"
- Tasting notes in your own words: describe exactly what "baked" or "underdeveloped" tastes like in this specific cup

**For the comparison:**
- How much worse is the defect compared to the reference? Rate both overall (1-10).
- What is the single biggest flavor difference?

**Diagnosis — write this out explicitly:**
Complete this sentence in your log: "This cup tastes [descriptor] because I [specific roasting decision]. To fix it, I would [specific adjustment]."

This is the flavor diagnostic framework from COFF-100 Session 04 applied to your own work. It should feel mechanical and obvious now — because you made the defect on purpose, you know exactly what caused it.

---

## Part E: Log Entries

Two complete log entries — one for the defect, one for the reference. In the defect entry:
- hypothesis: what you predicted the cup would taste like
- hypothesis_result: whether the defect tasted as predicted
- defect_flags: the defect you produced
- session_notes: the diagnosis sentence in full
