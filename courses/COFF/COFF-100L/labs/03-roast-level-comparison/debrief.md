# Lab 03 Debrief: Roast Level Comparison

---

## What You Just Proved

The two cups were the same bean, the same equipment, the same conditions — and they tasted different. That difference was entirely your decision. You chose when to stop each roast, and the cup is the result of that choice.

This is the core insight of home roasting, made tangible for the first time.

---

## Reading the Comparison

**If Roast A (light) tasted more acidic and fruit-forward:** That is exactly what should happen. Shorter development time means the fruit acids (citric, malic) in the natural-process bean are not yet broken down by heat. The fruit and floral character of your bean is most alive here.

**If Roast B (medium) tasted sweeter and heavier:** Also correct. Extended development converts more of those fruit acids to sugars and develops deeper caramelization. You are trading brightness for sweetness and body.

**If you found Roast A sour and Roast B well-balanced:** Your 30-second stop for Roast A may have been slightly too short, or your Lab 02 experience showed you that this bean needs more development than the minimum. This is useful data — your equipment or this specific bean may want more development time for the "light" end to taste pleasant rather than sour.

**If the difference was smaller than you expected:** A few possible causes: the two stop points were not actually as far apart as planned (check your timestamps), the bean's character is so strong it comes through at both levels, or your palate is still calibrating. None of these are problems — they are observations.

---

## The Prediction Check

Go back to your predictions. Were you right?

Most people accurately predict the direction of change (lighter = more acidic, medium = sweeter) but underestimate how much change actually occurs across a 90-second difference in development time. That gap between expectation and reality is where learning happens.

If you were surprised, ask: what assumption was wrong? The answer will inform how you think about future roasts.

---

## What This Means for Your Primary Bean

You have now cupped this bean at two different development levels (plus the Lab 02 baseline). You are starting to understand what this specific bean does at different roast levels.

For most natural-process beans at altitude:
- Very light development risks sourness if your equipment runs hot through the browning phase
- Light-medium (Lab 02 target) is typically the sweet spot for maximum fruit expression
- Medium extends sweetness and builds body, reducing but not eliminating the origin character

Your preference is yours to discover. But now you have data behind the preference.

---

## The Limits of This Lab

One comparison is not enough to draw permanent conclusions. You changed one variable (development time), but you held everything else constant — which means you also do not yet know what happens if you change the heat profile, or the charge temperature, or the batch size. Those are future labs and future experiments.

What you know now: development time is a powerful lever. That knowledge was always in the textbook. Now it is in your hands.
