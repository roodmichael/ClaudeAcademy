# Lab 03: Roast Level Comparison
**Course:** COFF-100L
**Learning Objective:** After this lab, you will be able to describe how development time directly shapes flavor by comparing two roasts of the same bean at different roast levels, with everything else held constant.

---

## The Setup

Same bean. Same equipment. Same charge weight. Two different stop points.

That is the entire design of this lab. You are roasting your primary (natural-process) bean twice — once to a light stop (30 seconds after first crack ends) and once to a medium stop (approximately 2 minutes after first crack ends). You then cup them side by side 24-48 hours later.

The single variable is development time. Everything else you can control, you hold constant.

---

## Why One Variable at a Time

This is the fundamental methodology of home roasting improvement, and it is also how good experimental design works in any field. If you change multiple things at once — development time and heat setting and batch size — and the result is better, you do not know which change caused the improvement. If you change only one thing and the result is different, you know exactly what changed it.

You are essentially running a controlled experiment. The Durato Bombe G1 is your reagent. Development time is your independent variable. Flavor in the cup is your measured outcome.

After this lab, "development time shapes flavor" will not be a sentence you read in a textbook. It will be something you tasted.

---

## The Targets

**Roast A — Light:**
Stop approximately 30 seconds after first crack ends. This is on the lighter end of light-medium — you are aiming to preserve the bean's maximum fruit and floral character.

**Roast B — Medium:**
Stop approximately 2 minutes after first crack ends. This is well into medium territory — sweetness increases, acidity softens, body builds. Some fruit character remains, but it shifts toward dried fruit and caramel rather than fresh berry.

You built Roast A's approximate equivalent in Lab 02. Roast B will be new territory.

---

## What to Expect

Going in, your prediction: Roast A will taste brighter and more fruit-forward. Roast B will taste sweeter and heavier. That is the theory from COFF-100 Session 05.

Write that prediction down before you cup. Whether the cup confirms or surprises you, the prediction-then-result format trains you to connect your decisions to outcomes rather than just reacting to whatever appears in your cup.
