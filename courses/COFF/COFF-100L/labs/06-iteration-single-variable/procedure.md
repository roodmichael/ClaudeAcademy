# Lab 06 Procedure: Iteration — Single Variable Adjustment

**Estimated time:** At least two roast sessions across multiple days + multiple cupping sessions
**Beans needed:** 300-360g of your chosen bean green (100-120g per roast)

---

## Before You Begin: The Pre-Lab Setup

### Step 1: Review Your Log

Read through your log entries from Labs 02, 03, and 04 (for your natural-process bean) or from Lab 04 (for your washed bean). Answer these questions in your Lab 06 pre-session notes:

1. What is your current best overall rating for this bean, and from which lab?
2. What specific quality is missing or not yet right in that best roast?
3. What one variable do you believe will close that gap?

### Step 2: Define Your Variable and Hypothesis

Complete this template in your log before Roast 1:

- **Bean:** [name]
- **Variable I am adjusting:** [e.g., "development time after first crack end"]
- **Roast 1 (baseline) value:** [e.g., "45 seconds"]
- **Roast 2 (first adjustment) target value:** [e.g., "75 seconds — adding 30 seconds"]
- **Prediction for Roast 2 vs. Roast 1:** [e.g., "More sweetness and body, less sharp acidity"]
- **How I will evaluate whether the hypothesis was correct:** [e.g., "Side-by-side cupping, comparing sweetness and acidity ratings"]

---

## Roast 1: Baseline

Execute your best-known process for this bean. Use your established settings. This is your reference point — roast it as well as you can.

Document everything. Label: "Lab 06 — Roast 1 — Baseline — [date]"

In your lab log entry:
- variable_changed_from_previous: null (this is the baseline)
- hypothesis: "This is my established best process; cupping will confirm starting quality"

Rest 24-48 hours. Cup before roasting Roast 2 if possible — ideally you cup Roast 1 and Roast 2 side by side, but you need to complete Roast 1 before that cupping is possible. Cup Roast 1 alone first if timing requires it. Record your tasting notes.

---

## Roast 2: First Adjustment

Roast the same bean at the same settings as Roast 1, except for your single chosen variable.

**Before the roast:**
- Confirm all other settings match Roast 1 exactly
- Write your target value for the changed variable
- Write your prediction for how this will affect the cup

**During the roast:**
- Everything same as Roast 1 until the moment your variable takes effect
- If adjusting development time: set a countdown from your Roast 1 drop point, running to your new target
- Document all timeline points

**After the roast:**
- Note any observable differences (color at drop, aroma during roast)
- Label: "Lab 06 — Roast 2 — [variable]: [new value] — [date]"

In your lab log entry:
- variable_changed_from_previous: describe the change in plain language
- hypothesis: your pre-roast prediction

### Cupping Roast 1 vs. Roast 2

Rest Roast 2 for 24-48 hours. Then cup it side by side with Roast 1 (or your fresh tasting notes if Roast 1 is more than 5-6 days old).

Complete the full post-roast evaluation. Answer:
1. Did the change produce the effect you predicted?
2. Was the direction right but magnitude wrong? (Did you under- or over-adjust?)
3. What is your Roast 3 plan based on what you tasted?

Write your hypothesis_result and your Roast 3 decision in the session notes.

---

## Roast 3: Second Adjustment or Refinement

Based on what Roast 2 showed, choose one of these paths:

**Path A: Continue in the same direction.** If Roast 2 was better than Roast 1 but the improvement was not complete, adjust the same variable further in the same direction (e.g., add another 15-20 seconds of development time).

**Path B: Correct back.** If Roast 2 went too far (e.g., development time extended too much and produced a flat, reduced-acidity cup), adjust back toward the midpoint between Roasts 1 and 2.

**Path C: Replicate and refine.** If Roast 2 was your best result yet and you want to confirm it is reproducible, roast Roast 3 with the same settings as Roast 2, focusing on precise execution.

Whatever path you choose, write the rationale before roasting.

Execute Roast 3. Document fully. Label: "Lab 06 — Roast 3 — [path and variable value] — [date]"

---

## Final Cupping: Three-Way Comparison

Once Roast 3 has rested 24-48 hours, do a three-way cupping: Roasts 1, 2, and 3 side by side (or the freshest two, using notes for the oldest if necessary).

This is your iteration arc in the cup.

### Document the Three-Way Comparison

| | Roast 1 (Baseline) | Roast 2 (Adjusted) | Roast 3 (Refined) |

| Development time (or your variable) | | | |
| Acidity (1-5) | | | |
| Sweetness (1-5) | | | |

| Overall (1-10) | | | |

### Key Questions for Your Session Notes

1. Did your score go up across the three roasts? If not — what happened?
2. Which roast was best, and why?
3. If you had a fourth roast, what would you change?
4. What did you learn about this bean — not just about roasting in general?
5. Write your current best profile for this bean: development time, approximate heat settings, and what you expect in the cup.
