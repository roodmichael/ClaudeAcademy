# Lab 06: Iteration — Single Variable Adjustment
**Course:** COFF-100L
**Learning Objective:** After this lab, you will be able to apply a systematic single-variable improvement cycle to produce measurably better results across three roasts, and articulate why each adjustment produced the outcome it did.

---

## The Setup

This is the most important lab in the course. Not because it is the hardest, but because it is the one you will repeat for the rest of your roasting life.

You will roast your best-performing bean three times across at least two sessions. Between each roast, you will change exactly one documented variable and state a prediction before you cup. The goal is not to make the "perfect" roast — it is to demonstrate that deliberate, documented, single-variable adjustment produces a trajectory of improvement.

That trajectory is the skill.

---

## Choosing Your Bean

Use whichever bean showed you the most potential in Labs 02-04. Your natural-process bean is the likely candidate — you have the most data on it across the most roasts. If your washed bean surprised you in Lab 04 and you want to explore it further, that is a valid choice too.

Stick with one bean for all three roasts. The variable you are changing is your roasting decision — not the input.

---

## Choosing Your Variable

After four labs of cupping your own roasts, you should have a clear sense of where your results are falling short. Your variable should address that gap.

**Common productive variables for Roast 6-8 of a bean:**

| What you tasted | What to adjust | How |
|----------------|----------------|-----|
| Slightly sour / not quite sweet enough | Development time | Extend by 20-30 seconds |
| Good but lacking body | Development time | Extend by 30-45 seconds (more Maillard) |
| Good but losing fruit/floral notes you loved early | Development time | Shorten by 20 seconds |
| Flat/dull despite reasonable times | Total roast time | Reduce by 2-3 minutes (faster roast = more energy through browning) |
| Inconsistent results session to session | Charge temperature | Standardize and note it every time |

Pick one variable. Write down your starting value and your target value. This is your hypothesis.

---

## The Three-Roast Arc

**Roast 1 (Baseline):** Your best execution of this bean to date — the roast you are trying to improve on. Use your established process. Document fully. This is your reference point for the arc.

**Roast 2 (First Adjustment):** Change your chosen variable by the amount you decided. Everything else identical. Cup Roast 1 and Roast 2 side by side before making your decision for Roast 3.

**Roast 3 (Second Adjustment or Refinement):** Based on what Roast 2 showed you, adjust again. You may continue in the same direction (e.g., extend development time further) or correct back (if you went too far). You may also confirm that Roast 2 was the improvement and try to replicate it more precisely.

---

## The Minimum: Two Sessions

Lab 06 spans at least two roast sessions. You cannot rush it. Roasts 1 and 2 may be in the same session, but you must cup them (24-48 hours later) before deciding how to adjust for Roast 3. That cupping is the feedback the iteration depends on.

The arc across this lab — the trajectory from Roast 1 to Roast 3 — is the core evidence of your final project.

---

## Connection to COFF-100

COFF-100 Session 08 covered the feedback loop: roast, taste, adjust, repeat. This lab is that loop, completed three times deliberately. The roast log is not documentation of what happened — it is the instrument that makes the loop possible.

By the end of this lab, you will have a working roast profile for your best bean, supported by evidence, built from your own data.
