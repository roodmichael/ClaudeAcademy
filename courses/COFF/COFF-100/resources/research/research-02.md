## Core Concepts

**Green coffee** is the raw, unroasted seed of the coffee cherry. Before roasting, beans are pale green, smell grassy, and contain all the chemical precursors that roasting will transform into aroma and flavor. Green beans can be stored for up to a year in proper conditions without degrading significantly — a key advantage for home roasters who want to buy in bulk. [perfectdailygrind.com]

The two dominant species are:
- **Coffea arabica (Arabica):** Accounts for roughly 60% of world production. Grown at higher altitudes (typically 1,000–2,000 m). Lower caffeine than robusta. Flavor profile: fruity, floral, sweet, complex. Descended from plants first cultivated in Ethiopia and Yemen. [Wikipedia / Coffee bean]
- **Coffea canephora (Robusta):** Accounts for roughly 40% of world production. Grown at lower altitudes. Higher caffeine content. Flavor profile: earthy, woody, bitter, less fruity. Hardier plant, higher yield. Used extensively in espresso blends and instant coffee. [Wikipedia / Coffea canephora]

For home roasting beginners, arabica from a single named origin is the recommended starting point — cleaner flavors make it easier to learn cause-and-effect from your roasting decisions.

**Origin** matters enormously to flavor. Major growing regions and their general flavor signatures:
- **Ethiopia:** Floral, berry, jasmine, wine-like. Often the most complex and delicate.
- **Colombia:** Balanced, mild, caramel, nutty with mild fruit notes.
- **Guatemala:** Rich body, chocolate, spice, smoky at darker roasts.
- **Brazil:** Low acidity, nutty, chocolatey, heavy body — forgiving for beginners.
- **Kenya:** Bold, berry-forward, bright acidity, wine-like.
- **Sumatra (Indonesia):** Earthy, full-bodied, herbal, low acidity. Distinctive from wet-hull processing.

[perfectdailygrind.com]

**Altitude** matters: higher elevation means slower bean development, more sugar concentration, and generally more complex flavor with higher acidity. However, altitude interacts with climate and variety — it is not a guarantee of quality alone. [perfectdailygrind.com]

**Processing method** is one of the most significant variables in green coffee flavor — and a critical concept for home roasters choosing their beans:

- **Washed (wet process):** The fruit flesh and mucilage are removed before drying. Result: clean, bright, acidic cup that clearly expresses the bean's intrinsic character. Most common in Central America, East Africa, and Colombia.
- **Natural (dry process):** The whole coffee cherry dries in the sun with the fruit still on the bean. Result: fruity, sweet, wine-like or berry-forward flavors. The fruit sugars infuse the bean during drying. Common in Ethiopia and Brazil. Requires careful management to avoid fermentation defects.
- **Honey process:** The cherry skin is removed but the sticky mucilage is left on the bean during drying. Result: a balance between washed clarity and natural sweetness. Pioneered in Costa Rica. Variations (white, yellow, red, black honey) depend on how much mucilage is left and drying time.

[perfectdailygrind.com — multiple articles]

**Buying green coffee:** Specialty green bean suppliers (e.g., Sweet Maria's, Coffee Bean Corral, Burman Coffee Traders) provide sample packs that let beginners explore multiple origins before committing to a larger quantity. Look for suppliers who provide: harvest date, origin (country/region/farm), variety, processing method, and suggested roast level. Fresh crop beans (harvested within the past year) are strongly preferred. [coffeebeancorral.com, perfectdailygrind.com]

## Key Definitions

- **Green coffee:** Unroasted coffee beans; the raw input for all roasting.
- **Arabica (Coffea arabica):** The higher-quality, more nuanced species; accounts for ~60% of world production.
- **Robusta (Coffea canephora):** Hardy, higher-caffeine species; accounts for ~40% of world production.
- **Single origin:** Coffee from one defined location — a country, region, or specific farm.
- **Processing method:** How the coffee cherry is handled after harvest to extract the bean — washed, natural, or honey are the main categories.
- **Washed process:** Fruit removed before drying; produces clean, bright flavor.
- **Natural process:** Fruit dried on the bean; produces fruity, sweet flavor.
- **Honey process:** Fruit skin removed but mucilage retained during drying; balanced result.
- **Terroir:** The environmental factors (soil, altitude, climate, rainfall) that give a coffee its specific character — borrowed from wine terminology.
- **Sample pack:** A collection of small quantities of different green coffees from a single supplier; ideal for beginner exploration.

## Process / Mechanics

Coffee cherries are harvested (by hand-picking or strip/mechanical harvest), then processed to extract the seed. The processing method (washed/natural/honey) determines how much fruit character remains in the final bean. After processing, beans are dried to a target moisture content of approximately 10–12%, then milled to remove the parchment layer (and any remaining fruit). Beans are then sorted, graded, and bagged in burlap or grain-pro bags for export. [perfectdailygrind.com]

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- Starting with blends rather than single origins — blends make it harder to understand what your roasting decisions are doing.
- Ignoring the processing method when choosing beans — a natural-processed Ethiopian coffee will behave differently under heat than a washed Colombian.
- Buying green coffee without checking freshness — old crop beans (over a year past harvest) taste flat and may have moisture problems.
- Choosing dark-roast-intended beans (e.g., Sumatra) for a first light roast attempt — these beans roast very differently.

## Hands-On Elements

- Order a sample pack of 3-4 different green coffees — one washed, one natural, one from a familiar region, one unfamiliar.
- Before roasting each, smell the green beans. Note color variation, size, any defects (black beans, shells, stones).
- After roasting identical batches of each, brew and compare. Document what changed.

## Expert Debates / Open Questions

- **Altitude vs. processing as primary flavor driver:** Practitioners disagree on which variable most determines cup quality. Both matter, and the interaction is complex.
- **Natural vs. washed quality:** Some roasters and cuppers consider washed coffees "cleaner" and more evaluable; others consider natural processing more expressive. Neither is objectively superior.
- **Variety identification:** Genetic coffee variety (Bourbon, Typica, Geisha, SL-28, etc.) is increasingly important to specialty buyers, but origin labeling is inconsistently detailed. Many coffees are sold without specifying variety.
