## Core Concepts

Coffee's origins trace to Ethiopia, where legend holds that a goat herder named Kaldi noticed his animals became energetic after eating cherries from a particular tree. Monks made a drink from those cherries and found it kept them alert through evening prayers. From Ethiopia, coffee knowledge spread eastward to Yemen, where cultivation and trade became organized around the 15th century. [aboutcoffee.org]

The Arabian Peninsula gave coffee its first social infrastructure. Public coffee establishments called *qahveh khaneh* became centers of social life — places for music, games, conversation, and news. Pilgrims traveling to Mecca spread coffee culture westward across the Islamic world, reaching Cairo, Damascus, and Istanbul by the early 16th century. These spaces were nicknamed "Schools of the Wise." [aboutcoffee.org]

Coffee reached Europe in the 17th century and met initial resistance — it was called "the bitter invention of Satan" in some quarters before papal approval from Pope Clement VIII legitimized it. European coffee houses became "penny universities" where the price of admission was a cup of coffee and the reward was stimulating conversation. Lloyd's of London, the famous insurance market, originated at Edward Lloyd's Coffee House. [aboutcoffee.org]

The Americas received coffee through colonial expansion. The Dutch cultivated it successfully in Java, then Sumatra. Gabriel de Clieu transported a seedling to Martinique in 1723. American coffee culture received a decisive push from the Boston Tea Party of 1773, when King George III's tea tax shifted colonial beverage preference toward coffee — permanently. [aboutcoffee.org]

The **three waves of coffee** describe modern coffee culture's evolution:
- **First wave:** Mass production and convenience (canned, pre-ground, commodity coffee).
- **Second wave:** Quality and experience — the rise of coffee chains, espresso culture, and the concept of the coffee shop as a "third place."
- **Third wave:** Specialty coffee emphasizing origin, processing method, roast transparency, and flavor complexity. Alfred Peet, often called the father of specialty coffee, challenged mass-market norms by emphasizing freshness and craftsmanship. [shinzacoffeeroasters.com, aboutcoffee.org]

## Key Definitions

- **Qahveh khaneh:** Early Arab coffee house; the social origin of the modern café.
- **First wave:** Era of mass-produced, commodity coffee (think Maxwell House, Folgers).
- **Second wave:** Emphasis on the café experience and espresso drinks (think Starbucks era).
- **Third wave:** Treating coffee like wine — emphasizing origin, terroir, processing, and roast craft.
- **Specialty coffee:** Coffee scored 80+ points on the SCA's 100-point scale; refers to the premium segment of the market.
- **Alfred Peet:** Founder of Peet's Coffee; mentor to the founders of Starbucks; credited with introducing Americans to darker, fresher-roasted coffee.

## Process / Mechanics

Early roasting was rudimentary — beans were stirred in metal pans over open flames. By the late 17th century, cylindrical drum roasters appeared in Europe, allowing beans to be tumbled over a heat source for more even roasting. Industrial roasting industrialized through the 19th century. The specialty movement of the late 20th century revived small-batch and artisan roasting as a craft. Home roasting as a hobby emerged in parallel with the third wave — enthusiasts who wanted fresher coffee and more control over their cup. [escondidospecialtycoffee.com]

## Common Beginner Mistakes

- Assuming "dark roast = strong coffee." Roast level affects flavor profile, not caffeine content in any simple way.
- Not understanding that freshness is roasting's primary value proposition — pre-ground supermarket coffee may be months old.

## Hands-On Elements

- Visit a local specialty coffee shop and ask about the origin of their beans — a useful introduction to third-wave vocabulary.
- Compare a supermarket ground coffee with a freshly roasted whole bean: the difference in aroma alone is instructive.

## Expert Debates / Open Questions

- Some argue specialty coffee's "third wave" framing is now dated — a "fourth wave" focused on scientific precision, sustainability, and equity in sourcing is emerging.
- The term "specialty coffee" has a technical SCA definition (80+ score), but it is frequently used loosely in marketing.
