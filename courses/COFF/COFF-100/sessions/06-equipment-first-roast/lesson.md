# Session 06: Equipment and Your First Roast
**Course:** COFF-100
**Learning Objective:** After this session, you will be able to select appropriate home roasting equipment for your situation, set up a safe roasting workspace, and execute your first home roast from start to finish.

---

## Opening

There is a coffee roasting community proverb worth holding onto: "Your first roast doesn't have to be good. It has to exist."

Everything you've studied in Sessions 01–05 is preparation for this moment. The chemistry, the stages, the crack landmarks — none of it fully registers until you've actually done it once. So before we go any further into equipment comparison and technique detail, internalize this: your first roast is a learning experience, not a performance. Be curious, be careful, and pay attention to everything.

## Choosing Your Equipment

Session 04 and 05 explained the process. Now let's choose how you'll execute it. Home roasting equipment exists on a spectrum from "things you already own" to "serious hobby investment." Here's the honest assessment.

### Option 1: Stovetop Skillet or Wok — Free

**Best for:** Complete beginners who want to feel a roast before spending anything.
**Setup:** A heavy-bottomed pan (cast iron or stainless) on a gas or electric burner. High heat. Constant stirring with a wooden spoon or whisk.
**Batch size:** 4–6 oz (100–170g) of green coffee.
**What to expect:** Inconsistent results. Heavy smoke. But you will hear first crack clearly, see the color change in real time, and develop immediate intuition for what roasting feels like.
**Ventilation:** Critical. Open windows. Use a range hood fan. This method produces the most smoke.

**Procedure:**
1. Preheat pan to medium-high for 2–3 minutes.
2. Add green beans (no more than half an inch deep in the pan).
3. Stir constantly in circular motions. Never stop stirring.
4. Watch color: green → yellow → tan → brown.
5. Listen for first crack (typically 8–12 minutes in).
6. Stop at your target roast level.
7. Transfer immediately to a colander to cool.

### Option 2: Electric Popcorn Popper (Air Popper) — $20–$40

**Best for:** Most beginners. The community's favorite entry point.
**Critical selection note:** You must use a side-venting popper — one where hot air enters from side vents and swirls the beans. Bottom-screen poppers let beans sit on a screen and burn unevenly. Look for "West Bend Poppery" style, or any popper described as "side-venting" or "stadium popper."
**Batch size:** 3–4 oz (85–115g) of green coffee.
**Roast time:** 8–12 minutes.
**What to expect:** Fast, reasonably even roast. Very audible first crack. Clear color visibility if you watch from above. Chaff flies out — put a large bowl around the popper to catch it, or roast outdoors.

**Procedure:**
1. Set up the popper with a large bowl to catch chaff.
2. Plug in and let warm up for 30 seconds.
3. Add green beans.
4. Watch and listen — color and smell will progress through the stages.
5. Listen for first crack (typically 6–9 minutes in with a popper).
6. Stop at target roast level — unplug and immediately pour beans into your colander.
7. Stir or toss in the colander for 3–5 minutes to cool.

### Option 3: Dedicated Home Air Roaster — $150–$350

**Examples:** Fresh Roast SR540, Fresh Roast SR800, Sandbox Smart R1.
**Best for:** Hobbyists who've confirmed they enjoy roasting and want more consistency.
**Batch size:** 4–8 oz (115–225g).
**Advantages:** Built-in chaff collector. Glass roasting chamber for observation. Some models have temperature and airflow controls. Designed for repeated use.
**Procedure:** Varies by model — follow manufacturer instructions, then apply the stage and crack knowledge from Sessions 04–05.

### Option 4: Drum Roaster — $300–$2,500+

**Examples:** Behmor 2000AB Plus, Hottop, Aillio Bullet.
**Best for:** Serious enthusiasts who want larger batches and more control.
**Batch size:** 8 oz–1 lb (225g–450g).
**Note:** This is not a beginner's first purchase. Get comfortable with a simpler method first.

## Setting Up a Safe Workspace

Before any roast, check these:

**Ventilation:** All methods produce smoke. Open a window near your roasting area. If using a range hood fan, run it on high. If you have a smoke alarm directly above your roasting area, consider temporarily placing it in another room — but make sure to return it after roasting.

**Chaff management:** Chaff (the silverskin) comes off the beans during roasting. It is light and flammable. Have a catching bowl ready. Never leave a roast unattended. Clean chaff from equipment immediately after each session.

**Cooling setup:** Have your colander and a large bowl ready before you start. You cannot pause a roast to find equipment.

**Scale and timer:** Have these on the counter and ready. Write down your green bean weight before you start.

**Roasting log:** Have a notebook open. You should write during the roast, not after — you'll forget the details.

## Your First Roast: Step by Step

Use whichever method you have. This sequence applies to all of them.

**Before you start:**
1. Weigh your green coffee. Record the weight in your log.
2. Pre-sort for defects: pick out any visibly black, broken, or shell beans.
3. Note the origin and processing method in your log.
4. Write your target roast level and planned end-of-roast timing.
5. Set up your cooling colander, timer, and catch bowl.

**During the roast:**
1. Preheat your equipment.
2. Add green beans. Start your timer.
3. Every 60–90 seconds, record: color description, aroma description.
4. Listen for first crack. The moment you hear the first distinct pop, record the time.
5. When rolling first crack begins: record. When it ends: record.
6. Stop the roast at your pre-planned target.

**Immediately after:**
7. Transfer beans to the colander. Stir continuously for 3–5 minutes.
8. Weigh the cooled roasted beans. Record this weight.
9. Calculate weight loss: (green weight − roasted weight) ÷ green weight × 100. This is your **development percentage** — a proxy for roast level.
10. Note the color of the cooled beans — light, medium, or dark?

**Storage:**
11. Transfer to an airtight container. Label it with origin, roast date, and roast level.
12. Wait at least 24 hours before brewing. Ideally 48–72 hours.

## What Will Probably Happen Your First Time

Be ready for these common first-roast experiences:

- **More smoke than expected.** This is normal. Open more windows.
- **First crack is harder to hear than you imagined.** Get quiet and listen carefully. Some setups are louder; some need you to reduce ambient noise.
- **The roast goes faster than expected.** Especially with a popcorn popper — things move quickly once first crack starts. Don't be alarmed.
- **The beans don't look as dark as commercial coffee.** That's fine — and actually a good sign. Most commercial "dark roast" is much darker than necessary.
- **Uneven coloring.** Some beans are darker than others. This is normal for beginner batches and improves with experience.
- **Chaff everywhere.** Normal. Clean up thoroughly — chaff is flammable.

## Why This Matters

You have now crossed from theory to practice. Every subsequent session — tasting, evaluating, adjusting — depends on having your own roasted coffee to work with. This is the moment the course becomes real. The roast you're about to do is the foundation for everything in Sessions 07 and 08.

---

## Key Takeaways

- Choose equipment appropriate to your budget and commitment level; the popcorn popper is the best beginner entry point.
- Always set up cooling equipment, scale, timer, and roasting log before you start.
- Ventilation and chaff management are safety requirements, not optional.
- During the roast: track color, aroma, and crack timing in real time.
- After the roast: weigh beans, calculate weight loss, store properly, wait 48 hours before brewing.
- Your first roast is a learning experience — something will be imperfect, and that's the whole point.
