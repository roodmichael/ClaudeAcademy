# Session 05 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

---

## Exercise 1: The Sound Identification Exercise
**Type:** Hands-on / Sensory observation

Search for one or both of the following on YouTube or a podcast:
- "Coffee first crack sound"
- "Coffee second crack sound"

Listen to at least 3 different recordings of first crack. They will all sound slightly different depending on the equipment and batch size, but the character — a series of distinct pops, like lighter popcorn — will be consistent.

**What to observe / record:**

1. How would you describe first crack to someone who has never heard it? Write 2–3 sentences.
2. Listen to second crack if you can find a good recording. How is it different from first crack in sound and pace?
3. Which would you feel confident recognizing during a roast? What would help you feel more confident about the other?

---

## Exercise 2: Build Your Roast Level Target
**Type:** Planning / Analysis

Before your first roast in Session 06, decide on your target roast level. Use the roast level table from the lesson.

**What to write:**

1. What roast level are you targeting and why? (Consider: what brewing method do you use most often? What flavor characteristics do you prefer?)
2. Based on your target, at what moment relative to first crack will you stop the roast? Be specific: "I will stop X seconds/minutes after first crack ends."
3. What will you do if first crack happens earlier than expected? What about if it doesn't come when you expect it?

This is your pre-roast plan. Having a plan before you start prevents panicking in the moment.

## Exercise 3: The Aroma Progression Walk
**Type:** Sensory observation / Thought experiment

Borrow an oven or a stovetop for this exercise. Do not roast coffee yet — this is preparation.

Smell the following in sequence (you probably have most of these available):
1. A raw grain — uncooked rice, oatmeal, or bread dough
2. Toast mid-toasting (open the toaster and smell at 30 seconds, 1 minute)
3. Freshly brewed drip coffee
4. A dark-roasted coffee (if you have one — smell the dry grounds)

**What to observe / record:**

Describe each smell. Think about how the aroma progression you just experienced (raw → toasting → full roast → dark roast) maps to the aroma stages in the lesson (grassy → bread → nutty/chocolate → smoky).

Write 4–5 sentences connecting this exercise to what you've learned about the roasting stages. This is building your aroma vocabulary before the real thing.
