# Session 04 Exercises

Complete these before taking the session quiz.

## Exercise 1: The Maillard Reaction in Your Kitchen
**Type:** Hands-on / Sensory observation

The Maillard reaction is everywhere. Before you experience it in a roaster, experience it in a familiar context.

Do one of the following:
- Toast a slice of plain bread, watching it carefully from pale to golden to dark brown. Pull it at each stage and smell it.
- Heat a dry pan and add a small amount of plain white sugar; observe as it liquefies and caramelizes.
- Sear a piece of plain meat (or a slice of bread with butter) in a hot pan.

**What to observe / record:**

1. At what point did the aroma become most interesting? Describe it.
2. What color change did you observe? Was it gradual or rapid?
3. What happened when it went "too far"? Describe the aroma and color at the point of burning.
4. How does this observation connect to what you learned about the three stages of coffee roasting? Write 3–4 sentences.

## Exercise 2: Stage-by-Stage Mapping
**Type:** Analysis

Draw or write out a simple timeline of a roast from 0 to 15 minutes (approximate). Label:
- When the drying phase is occurring (roughly what percentage of total time)
- When the Maillard/browning phase occurs
- When first crack would likely happen
- When the development phase occurs
- Where you would stop for a light roast, medium roast, and dark roast

No special tools required — a hand-drawn timeline on paper works perfectly.

**Then answer:** If first crack happens at minute 9 in a 12-minute total roast, how long is the development phase? What does that suggest about the roast level?

## Exercise 3: The Defect Diagnosis Game
**Type:** Analysis / Thought experiment

A friend describes three batches of coffee they roasted at home. Based on their descriptions, identify the likely roast defect and suggest one adjustment for the next roast.

**Batch 1:** "The coffee tastes flat and dull — like wet paper almost. It doesn't really taste like coffee. There's no bitterness but also no sweetness or acidity. Just... nothing."

**Batch 2:** "It's really sour — almost like it hurts the back of my throat. Kind of grassy too. Definitely not pleasant."

**Batch 3:** "The beans came out very dark, almost black. The coffee tastes bitter and harsh. Kind of burnt. I can't detect any of the origin flavors the seller described."

For each batch:
1. What stage went wrong?
2. What is the likely cause?
3. What one adjustment would you recommend?
