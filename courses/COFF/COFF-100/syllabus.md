# COFF-100: Home Coffee Roasting — Foundations

## Course Description

This course is your entry point into the craft of roasting coffee at home. You will learn why roasting matters, how green coffee beans become the aromatic, flavorful beans in your cup, and how to produce your first roasted batches with confidence. By the end, you will be able to make real decisions — about beans, equipment, heat, and timing — and taste the results of those decisions clearly.

## Prerequisites

None. No prior coffee knowledge is assumed.

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

- Explain why freshness makes home-roasted coffee different from store-bought
- Identify the major coffee-growing regions and describe how origin and processing method affect flavor
- Describe the three stages of roasting and what happens chemically at each stage
- Recognize first crack and second crack by sound, and use them to determine roast level
- Select appropriate equipment for home roasting based on your budget and goals
- Conduct a basic home roast using any beginner-appropriate method
- Evaluate your roasted coffee using a cupping protocol and flavor vocabulary
- Keep a roasting log that lets you connect your decisions to your results and improve over time

## Required Materials

**Equipment (acquire before Session 6):**
- Any of the following roasting setups:
  - Electric popcorn popper (side-venting, not bottom-screen type) — approx. $20–$40
  - Stovetop skillet or wok — free if you own one
  - Dedicated home air roaster (Fresh Roast SR540 or similar) — approx. $150–$200 (optional upgrade)
- Digital kitchen scale (accurate to 1g) — approx. $10–$20
- Timer (phone is fine)
- Colander or cooling tray
- Airtight storage container (mason jar works fine)
- Notebook or digital notes for roasting log

**Consumables:**
- Green (unroasted) coffee beans — sample pack of 2–3 origins recommended. Sourced from: Sweet Maria's (sweetmarias.com), Coffee Bean Corral (coffeebeancorral.com), or Burman Coffee Traders. Budget approx. $20–$40 for a starter sampler.

**References:**
- SCA Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel (free download at sca.coffee) — print or keep on your phone
- Your roasting log (paper notebook or a simple spreadsheet)

## Assessment Plan

- **Session quizzes:** After each session, 4–6 short questions. Low-stakes learning tools — immediate feedback, no grades.
- **Midterm:** After Session 06 — a documented first roast. You will execute your first intentional home roast, record a complete roast log, and write tasting notes with analysis connecting your observations to what you've learned.
- **Final project:** After Session 08 — a roast design document. You will write a comprehensive plan for your first COFF-100L lab session: bean profile, roast plan with chemistry reasoning, measurement plan, and success criteria. This document is your entry point into the lab course.

## A Note on Pacing

Each session is designed for a 45–90 minute read, followed by hands-on exercises that may take additional time depending on your setup. Sessions 01–05 are primarily conceptual — read them actively, take notes, and try the thought experiments. Sessions 06–08 are hands-on and require that you have your equipment and green beans ready. If a concept isn't clicking, the Tutor is available to explain it in a different way — just ask. There is no timeline pressure. A student who finishes all eight sessions and the final project understands home roasting. One who rushes through learns nothing lasting. Take the time to actually taste your coffee.
