# Midterm Assessment — COFF-100
**Covers:** Sessions 01–06

---

## Overview

You've covered the conceptual foundations of home coffee roasting and, in Session 06,
executed your first hands-on roast. This midterm asks you to document that roast fully
and reflect on what happened — connecting your observations to what you've learned
about chemistry, origin, processing, and heat.

The goal is not perfection. The goal is *attention* — roasting with awareness and
capturing what you observe honestly.

---

## The Task

**Document your Session 06 roast and analyze it.**

If you have not yet completed your first roast, do that before submitting this
midterm. Use what you learned in Session 06 to guide your setup and execution.

### Part 1: Pre-Roast Plan

Write a short pre-roast plan (1 paragraph, 5–8 sentences) that includes:
- What green coffee you are roasting (origin, processing method, altitude)
- Why you chose it
- What equipment you're using
- What roast level you're targeting and why
- One thing you're specifically trying to pay attention to during this roast

### Part 2: Roast Log

Complete a live roast log during the roast. At minimum, record:
- Green bean weight
- Roasted bean weight and calculated roast loss %
- Time of first crack start
- Time of first crack end
- Total roast time and development time
- Color description of the cooled beans
- Any notable observations (aroma, equipment behavior, how first crack sounded)

### Part 3: Post-Roast Tasting Notes

Wait 48 hours after roasting, then cup the coffee and write 1–2 paragraphs covering:
- How you brewed it
- What you tasted: aroma, acidity, sweetness, body, finish, overall impression
- Whether the result matched your pre-roast expectations — and if not, what was different
- What you think caused any flavor characteristic you noticed, connected to what you
  learned about roasting chemistry (Sessions 04–05) and your bean's origin and
  processing method (Session 03)

---

## What to Submit

Submit three pieces of writing to the Tutor:

1. **Pre-roast plan** (written before the roast)
2. **Roast log** (written during the roast)
3. **Post-roast tasting notes with analysis** (written 48+ hours after the roast)

Total expected length: 400–600 words across all three sections.

---

## Evaluation Criteria

- **Preparation:** Does the pre-roast plan show understanding of bean choice, roast level rationale, and what to watch for?
- **Observation:** Does the roast log capture specific, meaningful data — timestamps, weight, crack behavior?
- **Flavor analysis:** Do the tasting notes connect flavor attributes to roasting decisions, bean origin, and processing method?
- **Intellectual honesty:** Does the student accurately identify what went well and what didn't?
- **Vocabulary:** Are terms from the course used correctly?

---

## Tutor Scoring Guide

**Strong response:** Pre-roast plan shows deliberate thinking about bean choice and target roast level with chemistry reasoning. Roast log has real timestamps, weight data, roast loss %, and at least one specific observation beyond color. Tasting notes use attribute vocabulary (acidity, body, sweetness, finish), connect flavor to roast decisions and bean origin, and identify one thing to adjust. The student doesn't need to have roasted a "good" batch — they need to have roasted an *observed* batch.

**Common weaknesses to watch for:**
- Pre-roast plan that is vague — prompt the student to specify target roast level and why.
- Roast log missing timestamps or weight data — ask them to describe what they remember and note the gap.
- Tasting notes that say only "it tasted good" with no attributes — ask them to cup again using the Session 07 protocol once they've covered it, and update their notes.
- Analysis that blames the beans rather than the process — gently redirect toward what they can control.

**Feedback approach:** Celebrate the act of roasting at all — it takes initiative. Be specific about what was strong. Identify the single most important gap and suggest one concrete next step. Note that COFF-100L will give them the structured space to iterate systematically on what they've started here.
