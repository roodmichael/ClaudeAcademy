# Midterm Assessment — COFF-100
**Covers:** Sessions 01–04

---

## Overview

You've covered the conceptual foundations of home coffee roasting: why freshness matters, what a green coffee bean is, how origin and processing shape flavor before roasting begins, and the chemistry of what happens inside the bean during each of the three roasting stages.

Now it's time to bring those concepts together in practice. This midterm is a practical roast challenge — your first intentional roast, documented in writing. The goal is not perfection. The goal is *attention* — doing the roast with awareness of what you've learned and capturing what you observe.

---

## The Task

**Execute one home roast and document it.** You should have your equipment and green beans from the syllabus materials list. If you haven't roasted yet, this is your first roast.

**Before roasting:**

Write a short pre-roast plan (1 paragraph, 5–8 sentences) that includes:
- What green coffee you are roasting (origin, processing method)
- Why you chose it
- What equipment you're using
- What roast level you're targeting and at what point relative to first crack you'll stop
- One thing you're specifically trying to pay attention to during this roast

**During the roast:**

Complete a live roast log (use the template from Session 06's exercises, or your own version). At minimum, record:
- Green bean weight
- Time of first crack start
- Time of first crack end
- Time of roast end
- Color description of the cooled beans
- Any notable observations (unexpected smells, equipment behavior, how first crack sounded)

**After the roast (48 hours later):**

Write a tasting note (1–2 paragraphs) that describes:
- How you brewed the coffee
- What you tasted: aroma, acidity, sweetness, body, aftertaste, overall impression
- Whether the result matched your pre-roast expectations — and if not, what was different
- What you think caused any flavor characteristic you noticed (connect to the roasting stages from Session 04)

---

## What to Submit

Submit three pieces of writing to the Tutor:

1. **Pre-roast plan** (written before the roast)
2. **Roast log** (written during the roast)
3. **Post-roast tasting notes with analysis** (written 48+ hours after the roast)

Total expected length: 400–600 words across all three sections. Brevity and specificity are valued over length.

---

## Evaluation Criteria

- **Preparation:** Did the pre-roast plan demonstrate understanding of the key concepts (origin, processing, roast level, crack timing)?
- **Observation:** Did the roast log capture meaningful, specific observations — not just "it looked darker"?
- **Flavor analysis:** Did the tasting notes connect flavor attributes (acidity, sweetness, etc.) to specific roasting decisions or stages?
- **Intellectual honesty:** Did the student accurately identify what went well and what didn't, rather than claiming everything was perfect?
- **Vocabulary:** Were terms from the course used correctly and appropriately?

---

## Tutor Scoring Guide

**Strong response:** Pre-roast plan shows deliberate thinking about bean choice and target. Roast log has specific timestamps, color descriptions, and at least one unexpected observation. Tasting notes use attribute vocabulary (acidity, body, sweetness), connect flavor to roast decisions, and identify one thing to adjust. The student doesn't need to have roasted a "good" batch — they need to have roasted an *observed* batch.

**Common weaknesses to watch for:**
- Pre-roast plan that is vague ("I'm going to roast some coffee and see what happens") — prompt the student to specify target and why.
- Roast log with no timestamps or crack timing — this suggests they didn't track in real time. Ask them to describe what they remember and note the gap.
- Tasting notes that say only "it tasted good" without attributes or connection to roast decisions — ask them to cup again and use the Session 07 protocol.
- Analysis that blames the beans rather than the process for any flavor problem — gently redirect toward what they can control.

**Feedback approach:** Celebrate the act of roasting at all — it takes initiative. Be specific about what was strong. Identify the single most important gap and suggest one concrete next step, not a list of corrections.
